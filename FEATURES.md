# RaccoonVTT features

RaccoonVTT is a self-hosted virtual tabletop for D&D groups playing the 2024 rules (5.5e) on a square grid, 5 ft per square. It is made for phones first, and it works on tablets, laptops and a TV too. It suits a group around one table, players joining from elsewhere, or both. It has no voice or video chat: a group that plays apart uses a call of its own for that. One server hosts many groups at once, each in its own room. Dice roll on the server, so everyone sees the same result. Anyone can still roll real dice and type the total.

This page lists what RaccoonVTT does today, grouped by area. "The DM" means anyone who holds DM in the room, co-DMs included. [docs/features.md](docs/features.md) is the planning list: what is planned next, and what was skipped and why.

The app's Docs page (/docs) lists every written doc. To learn the tools as a DM, read the [DM guide](docs/dm-guide.md): a quick start, how to build maps and run fights, and worked scenarios.

## Secrets stay secret

- The DM sees everything. Hidden things show translucent or dimmed to the DM.
- Players' devices never receive what players may not see: hidden tokens, monsters and map labels under the fog, hidden stamps, lights, DM notes, monster stats, other players' companion stat blocks, the DM's fog edits before they are published, the map's other phases, scene folders and thumbnails, DM-only journal pages, secrets not yet revealed, creatures out of the party's sight, traps nobody has found and what any trap does, and, where the DM keeps them secret, monsters' places in the Initiative order and their Initiative rolls until their turn. So even a reload or the browser's developer tools cannot show them.
- The log never tells players what happened to a creature while they could not see it, even after it is revealed.
- The map image itself reaches players whole, and each player's device draws the fog over it. A group has to trust its players with that.

## Rooms and joining

- A room is one campaign. It holds the scenes, tokens, characters, journal, log and Initiative tracker.
- Anyone who can reach the site can create a room from the Home page. Whoever creates it becomes its DM and gets a private DM link, the way back in as DM. Save it before going on.
- Each room has a short ID, such as `K7QH-2MXA`, and a player password the DM sets, at least 8 characters. The form suggests a three-word passphrase such as `owlbear-druid-wand`, easy to type on a phone.
- Players join with the room ID and the password, then pick themselves from the player list, so a new phone carries on as the same player. Someone new joins with a name and a color. There are no accounts.
- Anyone, DMs included, can change their own color at any time: Menu, then Members, then Color on your own row. Your pings, laser, rulers and buttons change at once. Drawings, pins and areas you already made keep their color. Here and on the join page, a color someone else uses carries their initial ("DM" for a DM, or a count when several share it) and stays pickable.
- The player link carries the password, so opening it skips typing it. The DM can copy or share the link, or show its QR code for phones at the table, at any time from the DM panel or Members. "Copy player link" is in the Menu too. "Show password", beside the link, is there for anyone who would rather type it.
- A device stays signed in. Coming back never asks for the password again.
- Home lists the rooms this device has been in, and as whom. "Leave room" in the Menu goes back there.
- The DM can kick a player, change the player password and delete the room. A new password signs every player out and stops old player links. On Members, Manage on a member's row holds Kick and Remove.
- Creating rooms is limited: a few per address a day, 20 a day across the site, and a storage limit per room. The server owner can turn on a free, usually invisible human check (Cloudflare Turnstile) on the create form.
- Wrong passwords meet growing delays. Guessing cannot lock a room's players out. After many wrong passwords in an hour, joining that room with the password needs the human check, if the server owner turned it on.

### DM devices and co-DMs

- Opening the DM link on a device makes it a DM device. A new device asks who it is: "This is me" carries on as the same DM, with the same whispers and undo, and a new name joins as a co-DM. A player can never be picked there.
- A DM device can show a one-time QR code, good for 10 minutes, to add another device.
- The DM devices list under Members shows every DM device and table display, which are online, and Sign out for each. The DM can also take DM away from a co-DM.
- Replacing the DM link signs out every other DM device.
- "Enter a DM link or invite" on Home works in the installed iPhone app, which cannot open links itself.
- If every DM device and the DM link are lost, the server owner can make a new DM link from the command line.

## Phones, tablets and connection

- Every change shows on every device at once, and your own edits show before the server confirms them.
- The connection mends itself. A dead connection is noticed within about 35 seconds. The app reconnects when the phone wakes or the network comes back, and catches up without a reload. Edits made offline are sent once it is back.
- A small dot shows the connection. A banner appears only after a few seconds offline.
- On a short screen, a notice that lasts (Reconnecting, the Starting view bar, a part that did not load, "Viewing as") keeps to one line, its buttons in the same row and More to read the rest, so it never stands over the toolbar. Where even that leaves no room for a row of the tool's options, the options stand over it.
- Other people's token drags move live, with their name over the token in their color.
- One finger uses the current tool, and with the Select tool a drag on empty map moves the map. Two fingers pan and pinch-zoom. With a mouse, the wheel zooms at the pointer, as two fingers on a trackpad do, and pointing at a token shows its whole name. Nothing needs a long press.
- Tapping a token opens its action bar. On a screen 600 px or wider, or a phone on its side, it opens as a window at the bottom right of the map instead of under the token. Drag it by its header, or by the grip at the header's start ("Move window"). With the grip focused, the arrow keys move it, and Shift moves it further. On a computer, drag the round handle at its free corner, or an edge beside it, to resize it ("Resize window"), and with the handle focused the arrow keys resize it. It scrolls inside when it is smaller than what it holds. This device remembers the spot and the size for every token's window, and the reset button left of Close ("Reset window position and size") puts both back. Beside a phone's sideways sheet it narrows, and where it would be too narrow it folds into the sheet's header. "Fit the map" keeps the map out from under it. On a phone held upright it rests as a strip at the foot of the map with the token's name, AC and HP, a Damage or Heal switch, the HP entry and the actions used most that fit, such as Conditions and Roll. It stays put as you pan, so every token can be brought above it, and as it opens it moves the map just enough to keep its token in view. On a screen too short for either, it opens taller, over the Multi-select bar and if need be the toolbar. Close it to reach the toolbar again.
- On a phone held upright, Expand, just left of the action bar's Close, opens the whole bar, up to about two thirds of the screen, and moves the map so the token stays in view above it. Collapse rests it again, and so does Multi-select, so the map is free for picking tokens. Each is a chevron with its name beside it where the resting bar has room for it and the token's whole name, as on a phone at the 85% interface size, and else the chevron alone, its name shown on a long press. Conditions in the resting bar opens the bar with its grid, and closing the grid rests it again. A roll card that comes meanwhile leaves the grid open, and the bar opens again with it as the card goes. A token dropped under the expanded bar moves the map once so it stays in view. A bar you open afresh rests. On a screen too short to expand, the resting bar scrolls to everything else. ## Scenes and maps

All of this is the DM's.

- A room holds many scenes. The DM moves the players to a scene, and can prepare another one privately while the players stay where they are. The Scenes panel marks the players' scene "Players here".
- A new scene is a Battle mat (a blank grid with a background color), a Map image, or a Builder scene. "From a Universal VTT file" brings in a whole map (below). Cancel folds the new scene form away, and during an upload it stops the upload and keeps the form.
- **Starter maps**: "From starter packs", under Map image, makes a scene from one of 45 openly licensed maps: 29 of Dyson Logos's hand-inked maps, the 5 Scarlet Heroes maps, 10 Index Card Dungeons and a painted cavern, by pack and then by setting, such as Taverns and inns or Crypts and tombs, with a search over names and tags. The map is copied into the room with its credit. The 22 maps whose drawn grid the build measured start lined up on it and skip grid alignment. The rest open on Line up grid, as an upload does, with the box already at the drawn squares' size on the 5 where only that was measured. Ink-on-paper maps start with a dark grid that shows on white. See Images and art for the starter packs.
- The server resizes and re-encodes uploaded maps and makes a smaller copy for phones. A scene's map image is added, replaced or removed in its This scene section. Changing the map image of the scene the players are on asks first, since they see it at once.
- "Line up the grid" fits the grid to a map that has one drawn on it: drag a box over 3 by 3 squares and fine-tune it by its corners, or type how many squares the image has across and down. Snapping, rulers and areas then match the drawn squares. A new map scene shows the app's own grid lines over the art, and "Grid lines" in the Grid tool or the Scenes panel turns them off for art that has its own.
- A map image whose file name gives no size, for a new scene or in place of different art, is searched for a drawn grid while it uploads, on the DM's device. A grid found with confidence goes on the scene, and Line up grid opens on it saying "Found a grid of 70 px squares.", with Use default to go back to the first guess and the scene's size before it. A less certain find only places the box on the squares, to check and Apply. On a phone or an iPad, a map over 4,096 px on a side is searched on the server's smaller copy once it uploads, and a search that needs longer goes on in Line up grid after the scene opens, saying "Looking for a grid…". "Find the grid", in Line up grid's options or the scene's Grid settings, looks again at any time on any map, and Undo takes back a grid it applied. Apply or a move of the box stops a search still running, so a late answer never replaces your own grid. If the scene's image, grid or size changes while it looks, on any device, a grid it then finds is not applied, and Line up grid says so. A size in the file name wins, and a Universal VTT import keeps the grid its file states: on such a scene Find the grid, and the search of a new image for it, only place the box, saying the scene came with its own grid. Square floor tiles laid edge to edge can read as a grid, so the box is worth a look.
- Grid lines take a color, opacity and line width, or hide. A scene's size in squares and its background color can change.
- Each scene can have a starting view ("Save current view").
- Switching scenes shows "Loading <scene>…" over the old scene until the new one and its map are in. Players moved by the DM see the same.
- Duplicate a scene from its menu, choosing what comes along: monsters, NPCs and objects, player characters, fog, and drawings and placed areas.
- Folders: "Move to folder…" files a scene under a header that folds with a tap. Each scene shows a thumbnail of its map, redrawn when the DM leaves a scene they changed, or with "Update thumbnail". The menu's last row, "Delete", removes the scene with its tokens, drawings, build and fog. It asks for a second tap on the same row first, and a tap anywhere else cancels it. The last scene in a room cannot be deleted, and neither can the scene the players are on until they are moved to another: its Delete, in the menu and in This scene, says "Move the players to another scene first." An undo or redo that would take it away says the same. Players never see folders or thumbnails.
- Map phases: up to 7 other images of the same map (8 in all), such as day and night or on fire, that keep tokens and fog in place. A chip, or the Phase menu on a wide screen, switches with one tap. A phase can bring its own light level, so Night can make the scene dark. Players get only the phase showing, never the others or their names.
- "Export as image" saves a scene's map as a PNG: the map image and the build, without tokens, drawings or fog. Pick 50, 70 or 100 pixels per square, the grid on or off, and the player view or the DM view. The file name carries the grid size, such as `crypt-30x20-70px.png` (with `-dm` added for the DM view), so other VTTs set the scale on import. A very large scene exports at fewer pixels per square, to stay within what an iPhone can draw, and says so. On an iPhone it saves through the share sheet.
- Universal VTT import: a `.dd2vtt`, `.uvtt` or `.df2vtt` file from Dungeondraft, Dungeon Alchemist or DungeonFog brings the map image, grid, walls, doors, windows and lights at once. It shows what it found before it makes the scene and suggests the scene's light level. One undo takes it all back. A phone takes files up to 40 MB, so large maps import on a computer.

## Building maps

The Builder tool is the DM's. Its modes:

- **Brush**, **Rectangle room** and **Shape room** paint floor, and rooms that touch merge into one floor. Floors come in stone, wood, grass, dirt and cave. Terrain comes in water, difficult ground, rubble, pits, pillars and stairs.
- Walls go up around rooms on their own. The scene's "Automatic walls" setting picks around every painted cell, around floor only (for terrain painted over a map image), or off (for an imported map that brings its own walls).
- **Walls and doors** draws a Wall, Door, Secret door or Window on a square's edge, marks an edge No wall, or removes it. Doors are open, closed, locked or secret. Players see a locked door as closed, and a hidden secret door as plain wall. Drag along a grid line to draw a run. With Door chosen, a tap on a door steps it through closed, open and locked.
- **Erase** and **Erase area** clear everything in the squares: floor, walls, doors, windows, labels, stamps, lights and heights.
- **Difficult ground** marks squares as difficult terrain over whatever is under them, floor or map art, without changing their terrain: Mark or Clear, with the brush or a dragged Area. The marks draw as a thin ochre hatch that leaves the map showing, players see them with the map, and Erase takes them with the rest. Difficult and Rubble terrain cost double to walk through too, and Water costs double for a creature without a swim speed.
- **Heights and stairs** paints ground from 20 ft down to 100 ft up in 5 ft steps. Hold + or − to step quickly. It also marks stairs, which point up toward the higher side. Raised ground looks raised, lighter and casting a soft shadow, and stairs draw their treads. Past 30 ft the look stays the same, so tap the ground to read its height. Heights go over a map image too, so a cliff in a drawn map can be raised. A token placed, dropped or duplicated on raised ground stands on it.
- While Heights and stairs is open, each raised or lowered area shows its height in a pill, such as "↑10 ft". The pills grow and shrink with the map like token names. They draw under token names, condition chips, elevation badges and HP bars, and keep off them. A pill with no free spot at the current zoom shows again once zooming in gives it room.
- **Label**: type, then tap the map. Tap a label, here or with the Select tool, to change its text and size or delete it. Drag it here to move it.
- **Lights**: a tap places a light (Candle, Torch, Lamp, Hooded Lantern, Light or Daylight), up to 100 a scene. Tap one to change its reach, turn it on or off, choose whether walls cast shadows from it, or delete it. Drag one to move it. Players see only the light it gives, never the light itself.
- **Stamp** places pictures: 46 shipped top-down stamps (trees, stones, foliage, village pieces, furniture and rocks) or any token image in the room's library under the picker's Stamps tab, or the 131 props of its Starter tab: Warlock's Gauntlet ruins, wrecks, bones and trees, Poly Haven furniture and gear rendered from above, and Mark Gosbell's flat greyscale props. A starter prop is placed as a shipped stamp is, with nothing copied into the room. A stamp moves by dragging, turns and resizes by its handles, takes one of 8 tints, flips, comes to the front, duplicates, hides from players, or is deleted. Its tint, flip, front, duplicate, DM only, delete and Done sit in the builder's row, or behind ⋯ where the row has no room, on every screen.
- The builder's bar has Undo and Redo buttons, the same undo as everywhere else. "Clear the build" in the scene's settings removes all of the build.

## Tokens

- Token kinds: player character, NPC, monster, and object or marker.
- Each token has a name (which the DM can hide from players), an image, a size from Tiny to Gargantuan or a custom one, HP, maximum and Temporary HP, AC, Speeds, elevation, facing and conditions.
- The DM and a creature's owners change its size on the fly, for Enlarge or Reduce, a Wild Shape or a polymorph: Size in its action bar, below Conditions and Multi-select, steps it one size larger or smaller, and Size in its Stats picks any, with "Back to Medium" where its character or stat block gives its own size. The creature keeps its middle and its elevation, and its height, reach rings and attached areas follow. Shrunk back on the same device before it moves, it returns to the square it grew from. On a scene where walls stop moves, a player's resize is checked like a move: the creature never crosses a wall, and grows over the squares it filled into the room around them, away from walls the least it must, or keeps its size with "No room to grow here". The DM's changes are never stopped. A size change undoes as one step, keeping damage dealt since, and counts no movement. The DM alone sets and changes a custom size, and a locked token keeps its size for its players.
- The DM edits every token. Players move and edit the tokens they own. The room setting "Who can move any token" also lets no one (the default), every player, or chosen players move any visible, unlocked token. A drag that is not allowed says why.
- A player's top bar shows their own chip, their name in their color. On a phone the room name gives way to it, down to its first few letters.
- "Find my token" in a player's top bar jumps to their token. The next press goes to their next one.
- HP takes math: `-7`, `+5`, `=20`. Temporary HP is spent first.
- What players see of a monster's HP, set per token: nothing, a label (Healthy, Bloodied, Dying, Dead), a bar, or the exact number.
- A colored ring shows ally, neutral or hostile.
- Copies of a monster are numbered (Goblin 1, Goblin 2). A token without a picture shows its initials, G1, G2 or "AR" for Ash Rider, the same on the board, in the tracker and in the Characters and Monsters panels.
- Each creature shows its AC on a small shield in the bottom left corner of its token. The DM sees every creature's, a player sees the AC of the creatures whose stats they have: their own character and creatures they own. A dead creature's shield hides, and on a token too small for it the action bar still says its AC.
- Names, condition chips and badges draw above every token and the fog. They scale with the map and make way when crowded.
- Hidden tokens are translucent to the DM and never reach players. A locked token shows a padlock and cannot be dragged.
- A token sits on the creatures layer or the objects layer, under creatures.
- A token at 0 HP shows as dead or dying.
- Each token has three notes: "For everyone", which everyone who can see it reads, written by the DM and its owners, "On the card", the character's or monster's notes for the DM and the card's owners, and "For DMs only".
- Up to 6 other images per token, swapped with one tap for Wild Shape or a disguise. Only the DM and the token's owners get the list. In the token's panel, "Add another image" sits beside "Change image", and its tooltip and the picker it opens say what other images are for.
- A token's panel starts with its picture and name, then a line that says what it is and its size, such as "Level 5 character · Medium (5 ft)", "Monster, CR 1/4 · Small (5 ft)" or "Companion of Bree · Small (5 ft)", and under it its quick numbers: AC on its shield, Speed after its footprints, "PP" for Passive Perception, "Init" for Initiative, its elevation off the ground and a link to its sheet, each only where your copy of the room holds it. Then for a monster or NPC its stat block, whose row says while folded which block it is, such as "Wolf, CR 1/4", then what is grouped by use, each group one fold with a one-line summary while folded: Combat (Hit Points, conditions, range rings and rest), Notes, Position (elevation, facing and sight) and Image (change, crop or remove the picture, and other images). Stats, token settings, a companion's settings and owners fold away at the foot. Combat starts open everywhere, and another creature's Notes starts open, since it shows only with the DM's note for everyone. This device remembers which you opened, your own creatures' apart from others', so folding another creature's Notes never folds your own. Max HP and Temporary HP always sit side by side, with their labels above them on a narrow panel.
- Range rings around a token, such as 30, 60 and 120 ft: preset circles to tap on and off, and a "Custom ft" field for any other distance, up to 4 rings. With 4 on, a line says so, and the other presets and the field stay reachable by keyboard and screen reader, which hear why they do nothing.
- A token can carry a light (Candle, Torch, Lamp, Hooded Lantern, Light, or a custom one), set by the DM and its owners. A scene lights up to 20 token lights. Past that the DM cannot light another, while a player's torch is never refused: the party's own lights light first, and the scene's Vision settings tell the DM how many light nothing.
- A character's token can link to its owner's D&D Beyond sheet.

### Several tokens at once

- Select several tokens with Shift or Ctrl and a click (Cmd on a Mac), with "Multi-select" in a token's action bar or the Initiative panel, or with a box drag: Shift or Ctrl and a drag on empty map with a mouse, or any drag on empty map while Multi-select is on. Drag any of them to move them all.
- With Multi-select on, a tap on empty map hides the group's action bar and keeps the group, and "Actions" in the Multi-select bar brings it back. A tap on a token in the group takes it out. Done ends Multi-select and lets go of the group, and so do Escape and Android's Back while the bar is hidden.
- The group's action bar offers one HP entry for all of them, Conditions, and elevation up and down 5 ft.
- The DM also gets Roll, which opens the Dice panel's group rolls, Request a roll, Hidden, Locked, Start a fight or Add to the fight (objects stay out of the fight), Delete, and More: ring color, size, layer, image, how players see HP, hiding names from players, "Always visible, even under fog", and owners.
- Each action shows only when you may use it on every selected token, except Request a roll and Start a fight or Add to the fight, which show when any of them can take part. A player who selected their own creatures gets conditions and elevation.
- A change to the whole group undoes as one step.

### Riding, carrying and dragging

- Attach, in a token's action bar after Light, lists what it can ride, carry or drag within 5 ft, nearest first, each as what it does: "Ride Warhorse", "Perch on Bree" for a Tiny creature, "Carry Torch", "Get on Cart", "Carry Kobold" for a creature the viewer sees Grappled, and "Drag Goblin 2". The DM's list goes on to tokens up to 30 ft away under "Farther away", for setting up a scene. It opens as a pop-up from 900 px up and as a sheet from the bottom on a phone, and takes the arrow keys and Enter. An empty list says "Nothing within 5 ft to ride, carry or drag.", and tells a player that a grappled creature can be dragged once the DM marks it Grappled.
- Ride asks Controlled (the default) or Independent, two words side by side, with a line under them saying what the chosen one does: "Warhorse moves on Bree's turn." or "Warhorse keeps its own turn." The rider's Token panel shows the same choice under its tags.
- A holder carries what it holds: drag a warhorse and its rider rides along, a cart takes its passengers, a creature the torch in its hand, each with what those carry in turn. What rides takes its holder's change of height. A dragged creature follows beside its holder on its own ground, and comes loose once it is out of reach. A teleport leaves dragged creatures behind, and a move to another scene takes everything.
- Dragging the rider of a controlled mount, or a rider that covers its whole mount, moves the mount, which needs the right to move the mount: "Warhorse carries Bree. Tap Get off to step down." Any other carried token drags alone: dropped on its holder, or within 5 ft of the creature dragging it, it stays attached, and anywhere else it comes loose. One dragged off a holder that stands on the ground walks and lands on the ground, as a Get off does, crossing a step under the holder until it first clears it, but stepping off no cliff at the holder's side and climbing no ledge beyond it, and its line, label and stored cost read that walk. A selection that holds a holder and what it carries moves each once.
- Get off, Put down and Let go show on a carried token's bar. A carried creature keeps Attach beside Get off, offering only Carry and Drag, so a rider can pick up a torch or drag a grappled foe, and changes mounts by getting off first. Get off puts the creature on the first free spot beside its holder, and with none it is disabled, with "No room to get off." under it. Put down and Let go leave it where it stands. Players take back any of the three, or a Ride, with Undo move.
- A carried token draws right after its holder, a rider that covers its whole mount draws inset so the mount shows around it, a press in a holder's middle takes the holder, so a creature carrying a lamp, an owl and a chest drags by its middle and each of them nearer its edges, and a thin dashed line ties a dragged creature to its holder. The bar's group line names the link in the Token panel's words: "Riding Warhorse", "On Bree" or "Dragged by Thorn" on a carried token, and "Carrying Bree, Torch" and "Dragging Goblin 2" on a holder, each name a button that selects that token, with Let go or Put down beside each one a holder carries. The Token panel tags the link the same way: "Riding Warhorse", "On Bree", "Dragged by Thorn", "Carries Bree and Torch".
- Mounted combat, as the 2024 rules have it. Getting on or off a mount on the rider's own turn costs half the rider's Speed, and a player gets on a mount only on their own turn. A controlled mount takes its rider's Initiative and goes right after the rider: it moves and acts on the rider's turn, its moves count against its own Speed, the moved pill sits on the mount with the rider's own movement under it ("Bree: 15 ft"), and its Reaction comes back with the rider's. Its own row that round only marks time, dimmed in the tracker, and a mount that already had its turn this round is passed over until the next. When the mount's own row is acting as it is mounted, its options offer the DM "Move after Bree". Getting off ends the sharing at once. A rider the DM hides, or one whose initiative row the DM hid, never shares its mount's turn or moves its row, so the tracker gives nothing away. A row the order setting keeps from players until its first turn stops only the row move, at a ride taken before that turn. When the turn comes the row shows and the mount shares it, so a mount whose row stayed ahead of its rider's can act on its own turn and on its rider's each round, until the DM's "Move after Bree" on its row puts it right.
- The tracker tags a rider's row "Riding Warhorse", and a controlled mount's "Controlled by Bree: moves and acts on Bree's turn (Dash, Disengage or Dodge)".
- Dragging or carrying a creature every player sees costs 1 extra foot per foot, as difficult terrain does, unless it is Tiny or two sizes smaller than its holder.
- When Prone or Unconscious lands on a rider or on its mount, the DM's device says "Bree may fall off Warhorse: DC 10 Dexterity save.", with "Ask for the save" for a player's rider. Nothing falls off by itself.
- On a scene where walls stop moves, a holder's drag finds a way that fits every creature it drags beside it that every player sees, so a grappled goblin walking beside it comes through a 10 ft gap with it. With no such way the drag reads "No way through", the group stops where its holder, or one of those creatures, meets a wall, and the note names the one that cannot follow when the holder alone could go ("Goblin can't follow through there.").
- Hidden stays hidden: a hidden imp on a villain's shoulder never reaches players, and no stop, cost, refusal or undo depends on a token or link they cannot see. A visible torch held by an invisible creature floats along with it, as at the table.
- A token carries at most 8 others, a chain of carried tokens is at most 3 deep, and a group moves at most 20 tokens.

## Images and art

- The image picker gets an image six ways: Icons, Starter, Device (an upload, or the camera roll on a phone), Link (an image's web address), Search the web, and Library. Pasting a copied image works there too, and in New scene's Map image, a scene's Map image and phases, Show a handout and the stamp picker. On a computer, the DM can paste over the map and choose what it is for.
- **Icons**: 4,180 game-icons.net icons, each on a colored disk, found by name or artist. Picking one needs no upload and no search, so it works when web search is used up, and once the icon list has loaded it works offline too. Each icon names its artist and license where you pick it.
- **Search the web**: type "goblin archer" and tap a result. It searches Google Images, with safe search on, while the server has a Google search key and searches left this month. Otherwise it searches Openverse's openly licensed images, and "Search Openverse instead" asks Openverse on purpose. An Openverse image keeps its credit (title, author, license and what was changed), shown in the library and the token panel.
- **Starter packs**: 501 openly licensed works in 14 packs, each from one artist or project, ready for a DM with no art of their own. They have a selector of their own wherever art is picked, showing only the packs that fit: the image picker's Starter tab (tokens and portraits), "From starter packs" under New scene's Map image (maps) and in Show a handout (pictures to show), and the stamp picker's Starter tab (props). Each lists its works by pack and then by category inside the pack, such as Dragons or Undead, with a search over names, categories and tags across the packs shown. A tap on a work shows its name, pack, author and license, and Use takes it. On a short screen, such as a phone held sideways, that line and Use stay at the foot of the window, as the Icons tab's do. Thumbnails show loading, and Use shows busy until the copy lands or says plainly why it failed.
- **Starter tokens and portraits** (302): the Illustrated Bestiary (60 monsters in color), Cute Heroes and Monsters, Dungeon Crawl Stone Soup monsters (pixel art, scaled up sharp), Lemurs Fantasy Portraits, Fantasy RPG Portraits, Flare Hero Portraits, and Historical art's people and creatures. Use copies the picture into the room's library and opens the crop editor, as a Library pick does. A player gets the Starter tab in the image picker of the creatures they own, such as their character, since a token image is the one kind of art a player may add. A player's pick takes as long, and counts toward the hourly limit the same, whether or not the room already holds the work, so neither shows what the DM has picked.
- A starter pick of a map, token or handout becomes the room's own image, with its credit (title, author, license, the credit line its author asks for, such as "Cartography by Dyson Logos", and what was changed), shown in the library and the token panel. It never counts toward the room's storage, and a backup carries it with its credit. Moved to a server that ships the same work, it stays a starter pick there. Picks have an hourly limit of their own, apart from uploads. The DM picking a work the room already holds costs nothing, and a player's pick always counts.
- Crop to a circle with zoom and pan. The ring takes the disposition color.
- The server checks, resizes and re-encodes every image and strips its metadata.
- The room's library keeps every image for reuse. The DM's library marks images nothing uses as "Not in use" and deletes them one at a time or all at once ("Delete N not in use", under Clean up at the foot), on a second tap. An image in use cannot be deleted. Undo cannot bring back a token or anything else whose image was deleted since. Images picked from the starter packs sit after the room's own, in a "From starter packs" fold that starts closed, in the Library panel and the picker's Library tab, so they never crowd the DM's own maps and tokens. New scene's "Or use a map already in this room" and the handout form list them after the DM's own too, apart. A deleted pick can be picked again. If the list of the room's images does not load, the Library and the stamp picker say so in one line with Try again, and still show what the room already uses, such as the images on its tokens and the DM's handouts.
- A tap on an image in the Library opens it large in the same viewer, so the DM can look at any handout, map or token image, and a player at any token image they can see. The viewer's header gives the image's size, its credit where it has one, and for the DM whether it is in use, with Delete for one not in use. The DM's library lists handouts first, and marks each image "In use" or "Not in use" in its corner.
- The DM's library shows a PDF handout as one item with its page count. A tap opens it with every page, and Delete removes it with its pages.
- A monster's default image: set it once, and every token of that monster placed afterwards starts with it. "Apply to N tokens on this scene" puts it on the ones already there.
- Every SRD monster comes with a default token: a game-icons.net icon on a disk colored by its creature type (a wolf's head on brown for a beast, a skeleton on black for the undead), and dragons in their own color. It applies in existing rooms too, and nothing is stored until the DM changes it. A monster's page shows the default with its credit. "Change image" sets the room's own image for that monster, and "Use the default icon" goes back to it.
- Every piece of art that ships with the app, the starter packs included, is openly licensed and credited on the About page and in CREDITS.md. Each starter pack was checked for signs of AI generation: its page and its dates, and every work in it by eye.

## Conditions and statuses

- The 15 conditions of the 2024 rules, each with its rules text from SRD 5.2.1 behind "Rules text" in the Token panel.
- Exhaustion levels 1 to 6, with a level badge.
- Common statuses: Concentrating (with the spell), Hidden, Flying, Dodging, Raging, Blessed, Baned, Hasted, Hunter's Mark, Hexed, Marked, Surprised, Heroic Inspiration and Readied action. Bloodied shows on its own from HP, Dying (with Death Save pips) and Dead come from HP and Mark dead, and Reaction used is a switch on the Initiative row.
- Custom statuses take an emoji or a letter, a color and a label.
- A token's action bar has a quick Conditions grid, each condition named under its icon.
- A status can carry a counter, such as rounds or charges left, that a tap counts up or down.
- Durations: a number of rounds, or until the start or end of a chosen creature's turn. They count down with Initiative, and everyone is told when one ends.
- A concentrating creature that takes damage gets a prompt with the DC (the higher of 10 and half the damage, at most 30). Its player reports pass or fail, or taps Roll. For a creature with no stat block, Roll d20 opens a bonus field on the card, beside Adv and Dis, and rolls with the bonus typed there.
- A condition can end on a save at the end of each of its turns. The DM's reminder rolls it, for a creature with no stat block by Roll d20 and a bonus typed on the card, or asks a player, whose passing answer ends it. A failed one stays open for a corrected total or an allowed reroll. Players see which save ends it, never its DC.
- The DM can hide a condition from players, the creature's owner included. The log hides it too.

## Height and elevation

- Every token has an elevation in feet, negative for burrowing, with +5 and -5 buttons. A badge such as "↑30" shows whenever it is not 0.
- With Select, tap raised or lowered ground to see its height for a few seconds, such as "↑10 ft", or "Stairs" on a stair. Under the fog it shows nothing. Screen readers hear it too.
- Rulers, distances between tokens and areas of effect all count height.
- Creatures have a height from their size, so a fireball can miss one flying above it.
- A flying token's shadow shows from 5 ft up and drifts down and away as it rises, further for a larger creature.
- On raised ground from the builder, with "Walls and ledges block players' moves" on, a player's walking token crosses an edge only by a stair, while a flier above it passes. A creature sees over a ledge no higher than its eyes, and one in a pit cannot see out.
- A token left inside the ground after a height change says so and offers "Stand on it".

## Measuring and movement

- Ruler: drag to measure, and tap to add waypoints. It shows the total and each segment's length. Players' rulers show to everyone in their color. The DM's stays private until shared. A double tap keeps a ruler on the map, and the Kept rulers chip lists the kept ones, each removed by its row with the eraser. On a narrow phone, the DM's Clear shows as its eraser alone, so "Kept rulers" stays in the row. A point on raised or lowered ground stands on it, so the distance counts the height. A player's ruler counts only ground they can see, and everyone reads the same numbers. Its readings keep one size at every zoom, grow with the interface size, and stay clear of the top bar and the toolbar at the screen's edge.
- The ruler's Measure chip chooses Straight or Walking, shown as Line or Walk where the row is short of room, and the device remembers it. Walking measures how far the creature the ruler starts on (or a Medium creature, from empty ground) would walk to each point, around walls, through doors and up stairs, with difficult terrain and a dragged creature counted: "Walking 40 ft", with "+10 ft for difficult terrain" under it. Each leg reads what dragging the creature there would, from where the legs before it left it. From a rider of a controlled mount it walks the mount, as a drag from her does, its legs drawn from her seat. A leg with no way through draws in red, held at the wall as a player's drag would be, while the DM's own ruler, before it is shared, walks as the DM's drag does and runs that leg on through the wall. While a long leg is worked out the ruler keeps its last reading, and after a moment shows "Finding the way…". Shared and kept walking rulers read the same on every device, and follow the tokens they start or stop on.
- Dragging a token walks it the cheapest way to where it is dropped: around walls and closed doors, up stairs, around creatures in its way, and around difficult terrain when that is cheaper. The line bends at each corner, and its label reads what the walk costs, with what cost extra under it ("+10 ft for difficult terrain"). A walk that would end in another creature's space says so, under the label, and the label and its warnings stay together at the screen's edge. A long search shows "Finding the way…", and a drop made meanwhile waits for it and then lands. A walk stopped at a wall still pays for the rubble it crossed on the way. Walking through difficult terrain costs double, as does swimming without a swim speed and crawling while Prone. Another creature's space is difficult terrain unless it is an ally or Tiny. Group drags move in a straight line. An arrow key moves a token one square and never around a wall, and counts what it walks over as a drag would, so a square of difficult terrain costs it double and a climb costs what the walk up does.
- Dragging a token measures as it goes: green within its Speed, yellow within a Dash, red beyond. A creature above 0 ft with a Fly Speed is measured against that. In a fight only the creature whose turn it is gets the colors, and a drag with no colors to show is white. Inside Spirit Guardians or another Half Speed area that affects it, the creature's Speed is halved, rounded down, and the label says "Half Speed in Spirit Guardians".
- Tokens snap to the grid.
- Distance between two tokens counts from edge to edge, right for their sizes, and includes height.
- Diagonals follow the room's rule: every square 5 ft (the 2024 rule, and the default), alternating 5 ft and 10 ft, or true distance. Feet per square can change too.
- On a creature's turn its path is tracked. Each drop or change of elevation adds a leg, and the trail draws on the map with a ring where the turn began, bending where the walk went around something. "Moved" totals the whole path walked, which helps the table judge Opportunity Attacks. Nothing detects them for you. When difficult terrain made the turn cost more, the DM and the creature's owners read both: "Moved 15 ft, used 20 ft".
- A player's "Undo move" takes back their newest drag, elevation change or turn of facing. "Doesn't count" leaves a shove, Misty Step or a teleport out of the total.
- Players undo their own moves, newest first. An undo is refused when someone else has moved that token since.

## Walls and doors

- With the scene setting "Walls and ledges block players' moves" on, a player's drag goes around walls, closed doors and ledges the party has seen, and stops only where there is no way around, holding at the last free spot with a note saying what stopped it ("There is no way around it that you have seen"). New scenes have it on. A secret door not yet found is a wall to everyone's route, the DM's included.
- "Teleport here" on that note lands the token where it was dropped, for Misty Step and the like. It doesn't count against Speed, and the DM's log notes it.
- A creature squeezes through an opening one size too small, as the rules allow. A Large creature fits a 5 ft door or corridor.
- The DM's moves are never stopped.
- With the Select tool, a tap on a door offers the DM Open or Close and Lock or Unlock, and Mark found or Hide on a secret door.
- Players open and close unlocked doors that their own token can reach, with a tap on the door or "Open door" in the token's action bar. A locked door answers "It's locked."
- The creature that opens a door, which sets off a trap on it: the token whose action bar opened it, else for a tap the token selected when the door was tapped (any on the scene for the DM, the player's own that reaches it for a player), else the player's nearest own token that reaches it. The DM with nothing selected opens it from afar and sets off nothing.
- Walls block areas of effect. Walls, closed doors and pillars block sight.

## Fog and vision

- A scene with a map or a build starts fully fogged, unless a Universal VTT import was made with "Start under fog" off. A blank battle mat starts with none, and keeps none when a build is added to it.
- The Fog tool clears or adds fog with a rectangle, a polygon, a brush, or a whole builder room in one tap (the "Builder room" shape). The DM sees the fog as translucent.
- The DM edits fog privately and publishes it when ready. A live mode shows each edit at once.
- Fog never flashes the map. A player's map stays hidden until the fog has loaded, and missing fog reads as all fogged.
- Monsters and labels standing fully under the fog never reach players. Player characters always stay visible to the party.
- A creature a player drags under the fog vanishes for them, with a note saying so.
- "View as a player" shows the DM exactly what a chosen player sees.
- **Party vision.** The "Party vision" switch in a scene's Vision settings makes players see only what their characters see. The same settings set the light to Bright, Dim or Dark. Every player then sees what any party creature sees.
  - Walls, closed doors, secret doors not yet found, pillars, and raised ground above a creature's eyes block sight. Windows do not.
  - Darkvision, Blindsight and Truesight come from the creature's senses. Darkvision shows darkness in gray.
  - Lights from the builder and on tokens light the scene.
  - Ground the party has seen stays drawn, dimmed and without creatures. Ground never seen stays fogged.
  - Creatures appear when they come into sight and vanish when they leave it, Initiative rows included.
  - The DM can mark a creature, such as an allied NPC, to share its sight with the party.
  - The DM's own map stays fully lit. The "Party sight" switch shades what the party cannot see.
  - Moving players to a scene where no party creature can see warns first.
  - A creature a player drags out of the party's sight vanishes for them too, with a note saying so once the server answers the move, since a light it carries can keep it in sight.

## Areas of effect

- Shapes: Sphere, Cylinder, Cone, Cube, Line and Emanation.
- Place an area freely, or cast it from a token: Cones, Lines and Cubes start at the caster's edge, and Emanations surround the caster. Aim by dragging or with a handle, in 15° or 45° steps or freely. Cones and Lines can aim up or down.
- A player's area tool always shows who the next area comes from, such as "From Thorn". It starts on when Thorn is your only character on the scene, and a tap turns it off or on. With a companion or a second character it lists them, and No caster. With none of your creatures on the scene it stays, greyed out, and says why. It stays as you left it until you reload, and selecting your creature before opening the tool still casts from it. While an area comes from your creature, a tap on another creature aims it there. Set caster in More options casts from someone else. The DM's areas start with no caster, and once one is cast from a token the same button drops or restores it.
- Spell presets from SRD 5.2.1: search "fireball" and get a 20 ft Sphere.
- A private preview shades the squares and lists who is in it ("In area (3): Goblin 1, Goblin 2, Thorn (ally)") before you place it. A Cone, Cube, Line or Emanation leaves its caster out unless you include it. A Sphere or Cylinder catches a caster inside it.
- Auras stick to a token and move with it, such as Spirit Guardians or a Paladin's aura. The list of who is inside updates live.
- Placed areas stay until removed. They move, turn and resize, and show to everyone or to the DM only. Only the DM and the area's owner change one.
- An area's name keeps clear of tokens and their names, and on a player's screen of fogged ground.
- Room settings choose which squares an area covers (at least half the square, which is the 2024 rule, any part of it, or its center) and which creatures it catches (any of their squares, their center, or most of their space).
- Height counts: every shape measures in 3D. An area put down on a platform starts at the platform's height, and one moved before it is placed keeps its height above the ground.
- Walls block an area.
- **Save and damage** (DM): from a placed area, the group save for everyone it catches, filled from the spell and its caster, or No save for damage everyone takes. Temporary HP and Concentration saves follow, and one undo takes it all back. A player's own area offers **Ask for saves** in its place, which asks the DM for that group save (Dice, Roll options). While the ask waits, the area reads "Saves asked" and its player can't move, resize or delete it until the DM resolves it or the player withdraws. The DM still can.
- **Movement inside**: an area can make walking through it cost more, as Difficult terrain, Plant Growth's 4 ft per foot, or Spirit Guardians' Half Speed, for everyone or, with a caster, only the caster's enemies. An area on a creature, or on a token that rides or is carried with it, such as a lamp it holds, moves with it and never slows that creature. Presets set it where the spell says, such as Web, Spike Growth, Grease and Spirit Guardians.
- **Reminders**: an area can remind when a creature enters it, or starts or ends its turn in it, as Spirit Guardians, Moonbeam and Web need. Presets turn them on where the spell says. During a fight's turns, each reminder goes in the log, and reaches players only for what they can see.
- A room setting decides whether players see each other's areas.

## Traps

- **The Trap tool** (DM), a setup tool after Pin with no key. Its options start a new trap from Blank, from Alarm (a note for the DM only, nothing players can see), or from one of the SRD's example traps, credited in [shared/srd/NOTICE.md](shared/srd/NOTICE.md): Collapsing Roof, Falling Net, Fire-Casting Statue, Hidden Pit, Poisoned Darts, Poisoned Needle, Rolling Stone and Spiked Pit, each at a party level of 1–4, 5–10, 11–16 or 17–20 with the SRD's DCs and damage for it. Its zone is Squares (drag a rectangle snapped to whole squares, or tap to place the preset's own size, such as a Hidden Pit's 2 by 2 squares, or one square), a Trip wire (drag from one end to the other, ends on square corners and edge midpoints, at most 20 squares long) or a Door (tap one). A preset picks its zone's kind.
- A tap on a trap with the tool opens its pane, a bottom sheet where the map is under 600 px wide, as on a phone held upright, and otherwise a card over the map beside the trap, its first line saying where it started ("From Hidden Pit, levels 1–4"). Its sections fold to one line each: **Name**. **Goes off when**: a creature steps onto it, starts or ends its turn on it, or steps off it, crosses the wire or opens the door, with Add squares for up to 8 rectangles, and who: Party, Others or Any, with a line under it saying who that is, Small or larger only, and only creatures on the floor, which leaves out fliers, burrowers and riders. **Afterwards**: a switch that arms it again at the start of the next turn, off for a trap that goes off once, its uses left, stops the creature where it goes off, marks itself found when players see it go off, and for squares opens a pit so many feet deep, which reads Opened with its floor once it has opened. **Hits**: the creature that set it off, everyone on the trap, everyone in squares drawn on the map, or everyone in an area placed with the Area tool's shapes and saved into the trap. **Save and damage**: the group save's fields, a dice formula that does not read saying why under it once you stop typing, or on Enter, and going back to the last good dice when you leave it first. **Players read**: a line of up to 200 characters and a sound (click, rumble, crash, whoosh or hiss), with Play to hear it. **It also**: up to 8 things, each added with Add an effect and a tap on the map: close, open or lock a door, put out or light a light, show a hidden creature or a hidden stamp. **Finding and disarming**: up to two checks to find it, the Passive Perception hint, and how it is disarmed, with a check or not. **DM note**. Each change saves as you make it. The sections are the panel's own switches, choices and removes, the last way a trap goes off keeps its tick and says why, and a cleared name goes back to the trap's name, saying why once you stop typing. No note comes as you leave a field, so a press below it always lands.
- The pane's foot holds **Armed** and **Found**, **Ask to search** and **Ask to disarm**, which open a roll request filled with the trap's checks for the selected creatures or the party, **Set it off**, which sets it off by the selected creatures or else those standing on it, and Delete on a second tap. Arming a trap with no uses left asks for its uses first.
- On the DM's map an armed trap is hatched in red with the wolf-trap icon and its name over it, a sprung one is grey, a disarmed one a grey dashed outline, and a found one shows an eye. A wire is a dashed line with a pin at each end, and a door trap a badge on its door. While its pane is open, what it hits draws faintly. Zoomed out as far as a creature's name hides, a trap's name hides too and its icon stays, on players' maps as well.
- **Going off**: a trap goes off for the creatures it is set for as they walk onto it or across it, open its door, land on it from the height controls, or start or end a turn on it, in the order the SRD's rules give, each trap at most once a move. Only traps on the players' scene go off by themselves, and never while their screens are blacked out, so a DM can build ahead safely. A trap that stops creatures stops the creature, and the group it leads, where it set the trap off, or on the first spot after that where it may stand. A pit that opens stops a creature that fits in it at the first spot after that where it lies wholly over the opening, so a Large creature that steps half a square onto a 10 ft pit falls to the bottom, taking down what it carries. One too big for the opening, or whose walk never covers it whole, stops at the first spot where it may stand and stays at the rim.
- The DM gets a toast, "Hidden Pit went off: Thorn.", with **Save and damage** (or **Apply** for conditions with no save) and the trap's sound where sound is on, and a card in the alerts with who set it off, who it hits, what it did and could not do, and the DM note, with **Save and damage** and **Dismiss**. Save and damage opens the group save on the creatures it hits, as they stood when it went off, filled from the trap, and its Apply settles it. When everyone passes and nothing would change, as against Falling Net, the button reads "Settle: it changes no one" and settles it all the same. A trap set off out of the players' sight during a player's move stays armed for them: the toast adds "The players saw nothing.", and the card offers **Do its effects** and **Mark sprung**.
- A trap that goes off while the DM views another scene still toasts, with its sound: "Hidden Pit went off on Crypt." Its **Show** switches to that scene and toasts again with the creatures' names and **Save and damage**, and the card waits there. The Passive Perception hint does the same, with **Mark found** once there.
- Players read the trap's line in the log, "Thorn: The floor gives way!", and the player whose creature set it off gets it as a toast, with its sound where sound is on. The DM's Log shows the DM's own trap lines among the rest.
- A request for a save against a trap names it "Trap" until it is found, and by its name after, while the DM's page keeps its real name.
- Undo move is refused for a move a trap cut short, or whose trap changed anything its player sees (a line, its outline, a door, a reveal, a pit, or a light within the party's sight): "Could not undo moving Thorn: something happened on the way. Ask the DM." A silent trap nobody found that stops a token where it was going leaves Undo move as it is. The token's bar stops offering it, for that move and the moves before it, once the server's answer says so. The DM's Undo takes a stopped move back, the trap's doors, lights and pit included.
- **Found traps**: players see a found trap's name and outline, hatched in a warning color, and "Disarmed" once the DM disarms it, never whether it is armed, its DC or what it does. Dragging a token across a found trap that is not disarmed says "Crosses Hidden Pit" on the drag label. The DM's drags say it for every armed trap.
- **The Passive Perception hint**: when a party creature ends a walk within 10 ft of a trap nobody found and its Passive Perception would notice it, the DM gets a toast, "Thorn's Passive Perception (16) would notice the pressure plate of Poisoned Darts (DC 15).", with **Mark found**. The app never marks the trap found by itself.
- Hidden stays hidden: no player's device holds a trap nobody found or what any trap does, and no stop, refusal or room limit depends on a trap players cannot see. A scene holds at most 50 traps.

## Combat and Initiative

- The DM starts a fight with the selected tokens, every token on the scene, or only the visible ones. Players add their own character with "Join the fight".
- Players type in their own Initiative, or tap Roll in their row's Initiative box. With "Players may type their roll totals" off, they roll it, and the server refuses a typed one, whether set on their row or given as the row joins the fight. The DM types in the monsters', taps Roll in a row's box, or uses **Auto Initiative**: by creature (identical monsters share a roll), one each, or all at once. A row's Roll shows while it has no value and the row has room for it, and otherwise its options hold "Initiative, sets its row". The DM's Roll shows on rows no player plays. Once set, its options hold "Roll again, replaces 17", so a stray tap never reorders the fight. Each roll is a d20 plus the creature's modifier, with Advantage or Disadvantage from its conditions. Its dice roll on the server and show in the DM's Recent rolls. Players see the totals, never the dice. It sits right after the order, with what it rolled.
- "Use Initiative scores for monsters" in Room settings, or "Monsters use Initiative scores" in the Initiative panel, gives them 10 + their Dexterity modifier instead of a roll.
- The order runs high to low. Dragging rows settles ties. A round counter, Start, Next and Previous run the fight. The round counter and its buttons, or End turn, stay at the top while the order scrolls under them, and scroll away once the order has passed. They stay put only while there is room for them and two rows under them, shrinking to one line where the full ones would not leave that much. So they stay put in a panel opened over the whole of a very short screen when its body has that room, and otherwise scroll with the order.
- Previous takes back the last Next: whose turn it is and the round, the durations it counted or ended, the save-ends saves it asked, a Reaction it gave back, the Legendary Action pips it refilled and a hidden creature it revealed. A row the DM added or removed during the turn, a value set, In lair and the conditions and effects added, removed or changed stay as the DM left them, and a save made during the turn stays made. A creature that a new value or a ride moves past the turn Previous goes back to waits for the next round. When the turn it would go back to belongs to a creature that has left the fight, a companion that was dismissed included, Previous goes back past that turn too.
- The active token gets a turn marker. With "Camera follows the turn" on, the DM's map pans to whoever acts. Players' maps stay where they are, unless the DM uses Follow me or Look here.
- Players see the turn order, names and conditions. Turning a token's Hidden off, by its action bar, a group's bar, the H key or its panel's switch, shows its row too, in one undo step, as far as "Initiative order players see" allows: in a new room a monster's row still waits for its first turn. A row's own "Row hidden from players" still hides the row alone, though the creature's owners learn its turn once it moves on it, whoever moves it. With "Reveal hidden combatants on their turn" on, a hidden creature appears when its first turn comes, but only where players could see it: on revealed ground, and with party vision only while the party can see it. While it acts out of their view, they see "Another creature's turn".
- "Initiative order players see" (Room settings, Combat) keeps monsters' places in the order secret until their turn: Player characters, the rest on their turn (the default for new rooms), Player characters and allies, the rest on their turn, or Everyone (what rooms made before the setting keep). Players see the creatures players play from the start, and with allies those with the Ally ring too. A monster or NPC lent to a player shows from the start to that player alone. Every other creature, the DM's NPCs included, joins their order when its first turn comes and keeps its place for the rest of the fight. Before then nothing of it reaches them: no row, value, place or count, and no Initiative roll for it, even one the DM rolls in the open. Previous takes back the turn that showed it. The DM sees every row, and View as a player shows what players see. The DM's row says "Secret until its turn", after the eye struck through that means hidden from players, while players do not see it. The DM's log and Recent rolls mark an Initiative roll kept from players as hidden from players, or hidden from other players for a monster or NPC lent to a player, who still gets the roll.
- A strip across the top of a phone shows whose turn it is. On a phone held sideways it sits in the top bar when the bar has room for it, and on a computer it is a chip in the top bar. Only the DM's strip shows who comes next. On their own turn a player gets "Your turn" in the strip, a vibration on Android or a chime, and an "End turn" button.
- Rows show AC, HP and conditions. The DM sees every creature's AC, and a player their own creatures'. Tapping a row opens the same menu as tapping the token.
- Damage and healing take math, and one entry can apply to several creatures. "Players can apply damage and healing" lets players do it too.
- At 0 HP, monsters die ("Monsters die at 0 HP") and characters get Death Save pips that their player fills in, or rolls with the Death Save row's Roll. "Skip defeated creatures" passes over the dead.
- Healing a dead creature brings it back with the HP healed ("Healing brings back the dead", on by default). Off, healing does nothing to the dead, and the HP entry says so. The DM's Revive is in a dead creature's action bar and its tracker row's options, and brings it back with 1 HP.
- Per-turn resources: Reaction used clears at the start of the creature's turn, and Legendary Action pips refill.
- **Uses**, in a creature's tracker row options and its action bar, lists what it can spend, read from its stat block, 2014 blocks included. Its legendary actions are buttons by name, such as Fiery Rays, or "Wing Attack (2)" for an older block's cost of 2. One is off on the creature's own turn ("After another creature's turn"), once spent where the block says it can't be taken again until its next turn ("Until the start of its next turn"), and with too few pips left ("Needs 2"). Then come Use Legendary Resistance with the uses left, a recharge ability (Use while ready, Roll recharge and Mark ready once used), an X/Day ability with its uses left ("Dominate Mind, 2/Day, 1 left"), one a rest brings back, Regeneration's amount, and a character's class resources. Restore takes back a mis-tap, and a use the block no longer lists shows as used with Restore. A button is busy until the server answers, and a refusal says why under the list, as does the list's newest Roll recharge that was refused or lost, while the turn it was rolled on lasts and until the list's next tap. Opened again, the list never repeats one it showed, and an answer that comes after its turn ended shows as a toast. A recharge's words follow its mark: a roll's "Rolled 6: ready" gives way to "Used" once it is spent again, and "Rolled 2: used" to "Ready" after Mark ready. Uses shows only where its list has something in it. The DM's rows and companion rows show chips for what is spent, on a line of their own under LA and LR: "Fire Breath used", or "Dominate Mind 1/2" for an X/Day ability, at most three, then "+2 more". Players see the uses of their own creatures and no chips. Everyone who sees a creature reads its uses in the log by name ("Adult Red Dragon used Fiery Rays, a legendary action."). Only the DM and its owners read the counts and the line for a use restored ("Adult Red Dragon regains a Legendary Resistance (3 of 4 left).").
- **In lair**, in a monster's row options, gives it its lair's Legendary Action and Legendary Resistance counts and XP, and back to its own when turned off, waiting for the stat block while it loads, and staying off beside a reload where it did not load. What was used stays used. A monster placed in its lair, or added from a saved encounter's lair line, joins the fight with In lair on, whichever way it joins (a saved line keeps the choice it was saved with, even for a creature whose lair changes nothing else): the tracker's Start a fight or Add to the fight, or Start a fight or Add to the fight for a selected group. Those buttons wait while its stat block loads, and stay off beside a reload where it did not load.
- Recharge is room state: an ability marked used, from Uses, the Dice panel or its damage or group save, shows as used on every device and after a reload. Old builds' marks kept in one browser tab are dropped.
- **Turn-start cards**: at the start of a creature's turn, whoever answers for it, its player or the DM for a creature no player plays, gets a card for each thing the turn asks. A recharge: "Fire Breath can recharge (5–6)." with Roll, Mark ready and Dismiss. Regeneration: "Troll regenerates 15 HP." with Apply and Skip, or "Troll is at 0 HP and regenerates 15 HP." for a dying troll, with "Recorded Fire damage may stop Regeneration." when the damage it took since its last turn was typed Fire or Acid (damage typed by hand is not counted). A death save for a dying creature: "Thorn is dying. Death save: 1 success, 2 failures." with Roll, Skip and Dismiss, which reads "Waiting for Regeneration" while that card is open. A monster gets one only with "Monsters die at 0 HP" off, and the DM also gets a player's character's death save card. The first roll from any device, card, bar or Dice panel settles it, its result stays through a reload ("Rolled 5: Fire Breath is ready."), and a second roll, one after Next or one for a creature healed meanwhile is refused before its die. A settled death save card shows its result alone, without the dying line and the pips. Dismiss hides a card on this device for the turn, and Skip answers it for everyone, with Reopen on the turn card and in the creature's Uses, in its tracker row's options and its action bar, until Next. So Reopen stays in reach after the turn card is dismissed, and a skipped card alone is enough to show Uses. Next ends the cards, and Previous brings back the turn's cards as they were left, with their results.
- **The turn card**: from a creature's turn start to its end, whoever answers for it gets its menu, "Adult Red Dragon's turn": Use Legendary Resistance, the legendary actions counted ("3 of 3, after other creatures' turns") with no button on its own turn, its recharge and X/Day abilities, a character's class resources, and a block for each other creature of the turn they answer for, such as a controlled mount. Its x, Dismiss, hides it on this device for the turn, and the chevron before its title, Collapse, folds it back where it was folded, to one line of its name and its count that opens again on a tap. It stands last among the alerts, under the time-up line and notices, so on a phone held sideways those stay whole above a docked action bar.
- A Ready marker (Hold is the same thing) records the trigger and shows on the row and the token until the creature's next turn. Players learn only that a monster readied, not its trigger.
- The combat log keeps each HP change ("Goblin 2 takes 7 damage, 3/7 left"). The DM can take any one back while the creature is still as that change left it.
- An HP change whose take-back record is very large, such as one that removed many effects at once, can't be taken back.
- An optional turn timer, from 30 seconds to 10 minutes. It never ends a turn.
- "End the fight" ends it, with a second tap to confirm.

### Encounter builder

- The Encounters panel (DM) builds an encounter from SRD monsters and the room's own, with a count for each.
- It shows the 2024 XP budgets (Low, Moderate, High) for the party, from each character's level, and which budget the encounter fits. The DM can change the party for one encounter.
- Saved encounters drop onto any map, hidden from players if the DM asks. In a running fight their creatures can join the tracker. One undo takes it all back.
- With unsaved changes, the editor's back button asks "Discard changes?" first, and a second tap leaves.

### Companions

- A companion is a token that belongs to a character: a creature such as a familiar or a summoned spirit, or an effect with no HP such as Spiritual Weapon. Whoever plays the character controls it: moves it, changes its HP, conditions and stats, renames it and dismisses it.
- Each companion takes its own turn with its own Initiative, acts on its character's turn listed under it in the order, or acts on its character's turn and stays out of the order. Only a creature takes its own turn. A new creature starts listed under its character and a new effect out of the order, unless its maker picks another turn. One made with its own turn while its character is in a fight joins that fight at once, in a row of its own with its Initiative still to roll.
- A player adds a companion from their character in the Characters panel. It goes right next to the character's token, up to 10 per character on a scene, and the DM's log says so. The DM can make any token a companion in its panel.
- A creature companion can have a full stat block: an SRD 5.2.1 creature, such as an owl for Find Familiar or a wolf, text pasted from a book with "Paste a stat block", or one typed in the editor. Its players and the DM read it in its panel, and its action bar shows its saves, resistances and immunities. Other players see its token, name and HP as before. An SRD creature picked for a new companion with no picture of its own gives it the monster's default icon, even where the DM picked another image for that monster's tokens. Picking another creature swaps that icon, and the name the first one filled in, for the new one's, so an owl changed to a wolf is named Wolf. A picture or name you chose yourself stays.
- A character keeps up to 100 stat blocks for its companions, so a familiar summoned again starts from the same block. Its players and the DM add, edit and delete them from the character's page, under Stat blocks, a page of its own with Back to the character. "Add a companion" can start from one, which fills in the form.
- Attaching a block can set the companion's AC, max HP (the block's average, a typed number, or the maximum it has), Speed, size, senses, Passive Perception and Initiative. An unhurt companion stays at full HP, and a hurt, dying or dead one stays so. Conditions, effects and Temporary HP stay.
- A block's size goes through the same resize as any token's: a companion its player grows near a wall grows into the space around it, still covering where it stood, and moves just enough to stand where a move could end, never across the wall, or keeps its size with "No room to grow here", while the block and its other numbers still apply. Ctrl+Z right after attaching takes back the size change alone, and the block and its numbers stay. Detach takes the block off.
- A summon cast at a higher level takes its AC and HP as you type them when you add it.
- A companion listed under its character keeps its own Reaction and Ready action in the order.
- A companion's token panel says whose it is under its name ("Companion of Bree"), as its own row in the order does, and its picture carries its character's portrait at the corner. For the DM and the character's players, "Companion of Bree" opens Bree's page in the Characters panel. With a form open in Characters, the link leaves it for the page, asking "Discard changes?" first if something is typed.
- Other players see a party member's companions, under the character in the order, with HP as "Players see the party's HP" allows. A creature companion lends the party its sight, an effect does not.
- Effects on a companion that acts on its character's turn count that character's turns.
- A companion stays on its scene when the party moves on. The DM finds the ones left behind by searching "companion" in that scene's outliner.

## Dice

Every roll happens on the server, with a secure random source, so everyone sees the same result and nobody can fake one.

- The Dice panel is for everyone, after Initiative among the tabs. Type any formula and tap Roll, or press Enter: "10d4+1d8", "4d6kh3" (keep the three highest), "4d6dl1" (drop the lowest), "d%", "2d6+3 fire" (the words after the formula are its label) or "1d20+5 Stealth adv". A roll holds up to 100 dice and 20 parts. The line under the field reads the roll and its range before you roll it, such as "2d6+3, fire: 5 to 15". Quick dice, the d20 first, then d4 to d100, add a die to the field, and line up with its edge. Where the field is too narrow for its hint, the hint shortens to "1d20+5".
- **Next roll** sits under the quick dice: Adv and Dis joined as one choice of two, and Secret beside them, since it answers who sees the roll rather than how it rolls. Where the row has no room, as on a phone at 130%, Secret wraps under them and fills its line. All three are for the next roll only: the tap that rolls takes them, and they go back to their start, so a second roll never shares the first one's Advantage. Advantage and Disadvantage cancel to a normal roll however many of each a roll has, the words "adv" and "dis" typed in the field included.
- Secret: a DM's rolls for monsters start secret. The Secret chip follows the selection, so a formula the DM types with a monster selected starts secret too, and one typed with no creature selected, or only creatures players play, starts public. One tap changes it. A press holds for the creatures it was made for, and selecting another creature starts Secret again, so a monster's roll starts secret even after the last monster's was shown. A player can send one roll to the DM only. The DMs and the roller read it in full. Other players read "Mara rolled in secret." only when the same roll made public would have reached them, so a roll for a creature they cannot see leaves no trace.
- The newest roll stays at the top of the panel, with its dice as chips, a dropped die struck through and a natural 20 or 1 marked, and Again rolls it once more. An answer to the DM's request and a Death Saving Throw show there too, with no Again, since neither can go again as it went. A refusal shows there with its reason. On a narrow screen it keeps the total, a Critical Hit's sum and Again whole, shows the dice that fit whole with "+2" for the rest, and ends a long name in "…". A tap on it opens the roll in Recent rolls. It is the one place a roll's result shows in the panel: no row repeats it, so the panel never grows under a button as rolls land.
- Recent rolls lists every roll in the log, newest first, a roll of several dice groups as one row, with Again on your own. Again rolls it as it went, so a creature's save rolled again is that save again and a condition that fails it, such as Stunned on a Dexterity save, still records the fail. A row of one line is one touch target tall, its words level with Again. Older rolls are in the Log. Recent rolls folds like the panel's other groups, open at first, and stays folded or open on this device whoever you roll for. Folded, its row shows the newest roll, such as "Thorn 20 Longsword". A tap on the newest roll at the top opens it for the page without changing what is kept, and brings the roll into view.
- Others' rolls show as toasts while your Dice panel is not on screen, one for each roll ("Bree rolled 19 for Stealth.", or a damage of two types as its sum, "Bree rolled 14 for Flame Tongue damage."), and a roll of your own whose panel closed before it landed shows as one too. This device can turn the others' off. A toast of one d20 roll (an attack, a save, a check or Initiative) leads with a flat d20 showing the die that counted. A Natural 20, or a Critical Hit against an AC, rings once in your accent, and a Natural 1 is outlined in red. A screen reader hears the verdict after the toast's words.
- **Roll animation**: a roll you make in the Dice panel throws 3D dice into a band under the newest roll, from the button or field that rolled. They tumble and land on the server's faces 1 second after the answer arrives, and the total, its chips and the Recent rolls row show then. The deciding d20 of a Natural 20 or a Critical Hit rings once, and a Natural 1 is ringed in red. Up to two rows of dice show, the rest counted on a "+N" disc, and percentile dice throw as a tens die and a units die. Any tap or key in the panel during the throw, a newer roll, closing or resizing the panel, or leaving the page shows the result at once, and the tap or key still does its own job. A screen reader hears the result as it arrives. Only your own rolls from the Dice panel throw: others' rolls, the tracker's Roll, rolls for several creatures, the group save and Auto Initiative show at once, as before. The dice load in the background after the room opens, so a roll before they are ready shows at once. With reduced motion, the dice appear already at rest and the result shows at once. With Low power, the switch off, or a device that cannot draw them, every result shows at once. The dice are drawn in code, with no image files.
- Rolling very fast pauses you for a few seconds ("You are going too fast. Wait a few seconds."). A room's day holds 10,000 rolls and applied changes, a player's 2,000, and the players together leave the DMs 2,000 of them.
- A roll needs a connection: while the room is offline, Roll is off and says "Rolling needs a connection." A roll sent just before the connection dropped is sent again and still rolls once. If its answer was lost, the device asks the server what became of it, and never rolls it again by itself: "This may have happened. Check the log."
- **Roll options**: the DM taps a token, a player picks a character, a companion or another creature they own, and the Dice panel lists its saves, checks, skills, Initiative, Death Saves, attacks with riders and Critical Hits, save actions, healing and recharge, from its stat block, 2014 text included. They sit in a card under a line that parts them from the roller: the creature's picture, its name and a line such as "Level 5 character · Medium (5 ft)", then the Ask switch, Death Save while it is dying, a typed bonus where the app knows none, and Initiative, whose tag says what the tap does ("replaces 12 · −4"). A player with two or more creatures, such as a character, its companion and a lent NPC, picks one from tabs across the card's top, the picked one underlined. Under the card the options fold into groups in the stat block's order: Saves and checks, Skills, Traits, Actions, Bonus Actions, Reactions, Legendary Actions and Spells, the spells under Cantrips, Level 1 and so on where they span levels. On a computer Saves and checks start open and on a phone folded, Skills and Traits start folded everywhere, and the rest open. A group you fold or open stays that way on this device, kept apart for characters, companions and monsters, and viewing as a player never stores one. A folded group shows a one-line summary, such as "Str +4, Dex +1, Con +3…" or "DC 14 · +6 to hit · 13 spells". A new ask for a save or check, or a new ask for saves you cast, opens its group once, and while one waits in a group you folded again, its summary leads with it: "Asked: DEX save". A token's Roll lands the panel on Next roll, whole under the newest roll, with the card after it. A named roll is one line of text as a stat block reads ("Longsword +6 to hit, 1d8+3 Slashing, +2d8 Divine Smite", or "Sacred Flame DC 14 DEX, 1d8 Radiant" for a save spell, which names its DC) over one line of its controls, Attack, Damage, Critical and its riders by name, and a slot level between a minus and a plus. A button's words wrap inside it, and a row's buttons share each line. Saves and checks are three across. Conditions such as Restrained, Poisoned or Bless set Advantage, Disadvantage or a die on every d20 roll, and an effect every roll of a group shares is named once in the group's label ("Saves: +1d4: Blessed") rather than on each button. A roll the DM asked for answers the request, Initiative goes into the tracker, and the newest damage, a player's included, shows in every HP entry as "Use 9". Roll in a token's action bar opens them. For a creature a player plays, the DM's "Ask Bree to roll" switch starts on and asks its player for saves and checks. A creature with no stat block, or a character whose numbers go only to the DM and its own players, still lists its saves, checks, skills and Initiative, with no bonus shown: a tap that asks its player needs nothing, and a roll made here takes a bonus typed above them ("STR d20", then "STR d20+3"), never an unknown +0. A player's save spell or save action has **Ask for saves**, which opens a short block under its row: the save and DC, who it hits (the creatures selected, or everyone the player sees in an area placed from it, such as "In area (2): Goblin 1, Bree (ally)"), the casting level, and **Roll 9d6 Fire and ask**, which rolls the damage on the player's own dice in the same tap, or **Ask, the DM rolls**. The ask waits on the row, "Asked the DM for saves. Waiting.", with Withdraw, until the DM resolves it ("The DM resolved Fireball.") or sets it aside. A player has at most 3 asks waiting, and a room 20. Other players never learn of an ask, and a creature the player can't see never enters one.
- **Recharge and X/Day in the Dice panel**: a recharge action's row has **Used**, the same mark as the creature's Uses, and **Recharge**, which rolls 1d6 on the server and settles the turn's recharge card. Its Damage, Group save, a group save's Apply and a player's Ask for saves mark it used, and only while it shows ready, so they never draw a refusal. An X/Day action's row reads "2/Day, 1 left" with **Use**, the only thing that spends a use, so a reroll never does. A legendary action rolled there spends no pip. A Use that fails says why on its row, and a tap on Use with none left says "No uses left" there and spends nothing. Damage rolls carry their types, and the HP entry and the group save pass them on with the damage, so a troll's Regeneration knows Fire from Acid.
- **Critical Hits**: an attack's Critical, which a natural 20 (or a wider critical range) presses on its own, rolls every damage die of the hit, riders such as Sneak Attack included, by the room setting "Critical Hit damage" (Room settings, Combat): Roll the dice twice (2d6+3 crits as 4d6+3, the 2024 rule and the default), Max plus a roll (12 + 2d6 + 3), Double the dice rolled ((2d6 × 2) + 3) or Double the total ((2d6 + 3) × 2). One rule for monsters and characters alike, applied by the server. The roll shows how it was reached: in the log and Recent rolls ("12 max + 2d6+3 (4, 6) = 25"), and at the top of the Dice panel and in its toast ("25 (12 max + 13)"). Saves never crit.
- **Several creatures** (DM): a card with up to three of their pictures, "3 selected" and their names, holding a DC, Initiative for the ones in the fight and Set up a group save. Under it, Saves and checks, Skills and Shared attacks fold as a creature's groups do, each kept on this device. A save's button reads "DEX" under the label "Saves for 3", one line at every size, and is still named "DEX save for 3". One tap rolls a save or check for all. A shared attack rolls with each creature's own bonus against an AC, then "Roll damage for 2 hits" rolls the hits' damage. Folding Shared attacks, or selecting other creatures while two or more stay selected and still share the attack, keeps the hits and the damage button, and a folded group names the hits that wait ("Scimitar: 2 hits"). Fewer than two selected clears them, and so does a selection that no longer shares the attack, even one made with the Dice sheet closed. A group save left open shows as a notice above the card, "Fire Breath's save: set up.", with Open.
- **The group save** (DM): monsters rolled at once, players' creatures asked with the DM's roll one tap away (with a bonus the DM types for one with no stat block), a creature with no stat block rolled on the server with a bonus the DM types ("Server d20+3") or typed, pass or fail against a DC, damage rolled once and kept by type, full or half, conditions on a fail with their end, resistances, immunities, Evasion, Magic and Legendary Resistance on each row, every row changeable, its damage included, one Apply and one undo. Until the roll its creatures follow the selection, so a save action's Group save fills with the creatures selected after it. Monsters' rolls the DM shows to players carry the DC only with "Players see the DC" on, and a save-ends or Concentration roll in the open never shows its DC. Those switches fold into one "What players see" line that says what they are, as on the request sheet, and the damage field is labelled Damage. It stays on the DM's device through a reload, and an Apply whose answer was lost is never sent again unless the DM says so. A player's ask for saves comes as a card above the live cards, "Thorn asks for saves: Fireball, level 4", with the player's roll ("Thorn rolled 31 Fire") and the area's count, hidden creatures included, or the targets, with Open and Dismiss. It counts among the newest two on a phone and leads the alerts summary over a sheet. Open fills the group save from the caster's stat block as the DM sees it, at the asked level, whose Slot level stepper changes it, with the player's roll as the page's damage, and an ask on another scene reads "Go to Crypt and open". Apply resolves the ask in the same step, and where nothing would change it reads Close the ask. Discard leaves the ask waiting, and Dismiss sets it aside for good. An ask withdrawn or set aside while the page is open leaves a plain group save, and one resolved on another device stops Apply, so a spell never lands twice.

## Rolls

The DM asks players for a saving throw or an ability check, and each player rolls it in the app or types the total they rolled.

- "Request a roll" is in the action bar of several selected creatures that players play. For one creature, the bar's Roll opens its options, whose "Ask Bree to roll" switch asks its player. "Ask the party" is in the party bar, shortened to "Ask" while the bar is folded.
- One tap picks the roll: a save or a check by ability, or a skill from a list, which starts at its usual ability and can go with another. Nothing is picked until the DM picks it, and a later tap replaces the whole roll. "Or a second choice" lets the creature pick between two, such as "Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics)". Either can be removed.
- The DC, or none, on one row with the typical DCs for a check as chips from 5 (Very easy) to 30 (Nearly impossible).
- Who rolls, as chips. "Ask the party" starts with the characters on the players' scene picked, or every character when the DM is looking at another scene.
- What players see (the DC, and pass or fail), "Against a spell" for a save, and a note such as "against the poison" fold into one line that says what they are. Both switches start as the DM's last request from that device had them, else on. With the DC hidden and pass or fail shown, a typed answer is final once it passes or fails, so changing the total cannot find the DC. The DM can reopen it.
- The last three requests from that device are one tap each, with their DC, switches and note. Enter in the DC field or the note sends. If the server refuses the request, the sheet stays open with everything set and the reason. Closing the sheet while the request is sending does not stop it, and a refusal that comes after shows as a message.
- The player's device shows a card, with a vibration on Android or the turn chime. It asks the question with its DC or "DC hidden", and shows reminders from the creature's conditions and statuses, such as Restrained's Disadvantage on Dexterity saves or an Exhaustion penalty. With two choices each names its bonus where the app knows it, and the higher one starts picked.
- While the creature waits, Roll rolls on the server with its bonus ("Roll +3"), or with a bonus the player types when the app knows none ("Roll d20", then "Roll d20+5"), with "Add a stat block" for a character that has none, shown only to the DM and the character's own players. Conditions such as Restrained, Bless or Magic Resistance against a spell apply as in the Dice panel, the player can add Advantage, Disadvantage or extra dice such as +1d4, and a line under Roll says how it will roll, or that they cancel. A condition that fails the save, such as Stunned on a Dexterity save, offers "Fail (Stunned)" first, and a roll made anyway records Fail with its total. Rolling needs a connection.
- A rolled result shows in the card, with its dice, a dropped die struck through, and a natural 20 or 1, until Done, then in the chip: "DEX save: rolled 17 (1d20+5: 12). Pass. Final." A rolled answer is final. The player can ask the DM, from the card or its chip, to reroll one die (Heroic Inspiration, the d20 or any other die such as Bless's d4, or Halfling Luck on a 1), to add Advantage (Lucky) or to add a Bardic Inspiration die, and can withdraw the ask. A typed total that is final, with the DC hidden, can be fixed the same way with "Fix my total", whose Allow lets the player type it again but not roll.
- Or the player types the total and taps Send, and once a total is sent, Roll is gone. A save also offers "Fail without rolling". With "Players may type their roll totals" off in Room settings, under Players (on by default, rooms from before it included), every card a player answers offers Roll alone, with "Fail without rolling" on a save, no Total and no "Fix my total", and the server refuses a typed answer. Their Initiative is rolled too, never typed. A typed answer already in stands, and the DM's own rolls and totals are unaffected. A refusal, such as rolling too fast, shows under the card's rows in the server's words. A typed total folds the card into a chip, such as "DEX save: sent 17. Pass." (with two choices it names the one rolled: "STR or DEX save: sent DEX 15. Pass."), which opens again to change the answer while the request is open. A card can also fold away to answer later. On a phone the chips fold into one button while a sheet covers the map, and otherwise only the newest two show, with a "Rolls" button that counts the answers (such as "Rolls 1/3"), shows the rest and folds them again. A card that would cover an open sheet on a phone folds too, into a "Roll asked" button that closes the sheet and opens the card again, with any total typed into it kept. A card at a phone's foot folds the same way while a popover of the tool's options is open, such as Shape or the spell results, and comes back when the popover closes. On a phone a card at the screen's foot stands on the tool's options, such as the Multi-select bar, and the token's action bar keeps above it. Where the screen is too short for the card's question, Later and total, or for both it and the bar, the card folds, and a "Roll asked" row at the top of the map stands for it. Its tap, like a chip's, hides the bar, keeping the token or group selected, and opens the card over the tool's options, rising to the screen's top where it needs the room, with its question, Later, total and Send in reach at once, until it is answered or put off with Later.
- The DM's live card counts passes and fails ("2 of 3 answered: 1 pass, 1 fail") and shows each creature waiting, or how it answered: "rolled 17 (1d20+5: 12)" with its dice, a natural 20 or 1 and a condition that failed it, or "typed 17", with the choice first when there were two, and an answer it replaced. A waiting creature whose bonus the DM's view knows has a Roll, such as "Roll +5, Disadvantage (Restrained)", and one with no stat block a Roll with a bonus the DM types beside it ("Roll d20+3"), which never replaces an answer that came first: "Thorn answered before your roll." Pass and Fail mark a roll with no DC, or overrule one, and the mark is kept apart from what the DC says. A player's ask shows on its row, "Asks to reroll (Heroic Inspiration)", with Allow, which rolls the change on the server, and Keep, one tap each. A card with an ask opens on a computer, names the ask on its folded line on a phone, and leads the alerts summary over a sheet. A request a save-ends card sent stays folded to its one line while that card is up, so its Allow and Keep show once, on the save-ends card. Closing a request keeps any ask, and "Close all answered" leaves a request with an ask open. "Reopen for one more answer", Close, and "Close all answered", which shows while two or more cards are up, finish it. On a phone the DM's cards start folded to one line, only the newest two show, with a "Rolls" button (such as "Rolls 1/3") for the rest, and all of them fold into one button while a sheet covers the map.
- Only the DM and the creature's player see an answer, and its log line. With pass or fail hidden, a Pass or Fail mark from the DM adds no line to the player's log, unless it only repeats what the answer already said.
- No card shows on a player's screen while the game is paused.

## Characters and monsters

- Every token has a light stat card: HP, AC, Speeds (Walk, Fly, Swim, Climb, Burrow), Initiative bonus, Passive Perception, senses, size, creature type and notes. It is not a full character sheet. In Stats, Walk and any other speed that is set have a row each, and for whoever may change the speeds, the rest fold under "Other speeds" with Hovers.
- A player character can have a stat block: level, ability scores, saving throw and skill proficiencies with expertise, Jack of All Trades, Alert, a spellcasting ability, resistances, immunities and vulnerabilities, a critical range for Improved Critical, Evasion, and its weapons, spells and other rolls, with riders such as Sneak Attack or Hunter's Mark on an attack. Modifiers, saves, skills, Initiative, Passive Perception, spell save DC and spell attack follow the scores and the level. Type a total to keep a difference, such as a Cloak of Protection's.
- The DM and the character's players read and edit it. Only they get Add a stat block, Edit, Take a shape and End the shape, so a player lent a token of another character rolls for it but never changes its block. Its numbers stay theirs too: the lent token's player rolls its saves, checks, skills and Initiative as a d20 with a bonus they type, and its row's Roll in the tracker opens the Dice panel for that. Other players and the table display never see it.
- Fill it from pasted text, read as a stat block, or from D&D Beyond with a public character's share link or its JSON. It reads numbers, proficiencies, weapons, spells and class resources, never their text, and lists what to check before anything is saved. Re-sync from D&D Beyond, beside Edit, brings in what changed there since the last sync and keeps what you changed here, such as a Spellcasting ability or a renamed weapon. It lists each kept change with Overwrite, and Overwrite all, and Undo takes a re-sync back. It changes the maximum HP but never brings a character back or puts one down, and never changes the size. It is best effort, since D&D Beyond can change its format.
- A character with a stat block shows it in its token's panel and on its page, right under the name and quick numbers, its six abilities three across with their saves, a proficient save in bold, its saves in the action bar, and "Stat block" in its tracker row's options. Its Initiative and Passive Perception come from the stat block. Folded, its row says what it holds: "Level 5, PB +3" (or "Level not set"), or the shape it is in, such as "Wild Shape: Brown Bear", and never a count of known forms. A character with none has the same fold, reading "None yet", and, opened, offering "Add a stat block" and "Take a shape". Once the character has known forms, Take a shape reads "4 shapes" ("1 shape") on the fold's row and inside it, named "4 shapes, take a shape" to a screen reader.
- Take a shape on a character's own token: an SRD creature as Wild Shape, where the druid keeps Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma and proficiencies and each save and skill takes the higher of the two, or as Polymorph, with all of the creature's numbers. A character with no stat block takes all of the creature's numbers in Wild Shape too, and the shape's words point at adding one. The token takes the creature's AC and Speeds, and HP stays the character's. The picker's Druid level (Any, then 2 to 20) keeps only the forms a Druid of that level may take: up to CR 1/4 at levels 2 and 3, CR 1/2 at 4 to 7 and CR 1 from 8, and no Fly Speed before 8. Each device remembers the level or Any last picked for each character. Until one is picked there, it starts at the character's level once the character has known forms and is level 2 or more, and at Any otherwise. Choosing a level turns Beasts only on, a line under the controls states the limit, and the list runs from the highest CR down. One checkbox, on by default, gives the 2024 Temporary HP, the Druid level for Wild Shape and the creature's HP for Polymorph, only where they are more than the character holds. That Druid level is the one picked in Take a shape, or the character's level while none is picked or Any is. End the shape to go back: the character's AC and Speeds return, and what is left of Polymorph's Temporary HP goes, unless Temporary HP from another source replaced them.
- A Druid's known forms: up to 8 Beast forms the character knows for Wild Shape. "Choose known forms" in Take a shape (then "Change") opens a step with the same search, Beasts only and Druid level, where one tap adds or takes out a form, and the known ones stay listed first whatever the filters. They come first in Take a shape, where one tap takes the form as Wild Shape with its Temporary HP, or changes to it in Wild Shape. The Stat block group draws no list of them: the Take a shape button on its row reads the count ("4 shapes", "1 shape") and stays in Wild Shape, so a druid in Wild Shape changes form, or opens Change ("Choose known forms" with none), without ending the shape. In Wild Shape the form the druid is in shows pressed and unavailable, and while shaped the picker offers only Wild Shape. Polymorph or another shape hides the button until End the shape. With a Druid level picked, the count reads against the Beast Shapes table (4 forms at level 2, 6 at 4, 8 at 8), and a form over the level's CR or with a Fly Speed before level 8 is marked but still taken. The rule is shown, never enforced, and replacing a form after a Long Rest is left to the table. The DM and the character's players see the list, and no one else.
- The DM sees every character's page. A player sees and edits their own character's page. Other players see only the name, conditions and whatever HP the DM allows.
- Player characters belong to the room, so their HP and conditions follow their tokens to every scene. Monster tokens are independent copies.
- "Players see the party's HP" shows every player the exact HP of the other player characters. Off, they see only Healthy, Bloodied or Dying.
- The Characters panel lists the party. With "Players can create their own characters" on, each player can make up to 3 and place their tokens on the players' scene. New rooms start with it on. A room made earlier keeps its own setting, and one stored before the setting existed reads it as off. The DM can still edit, take over or delete the characters players make. A character's row opens its page, named in the panel's header with Back to Characters: its picture, name, level and size and quick numbers, Place a token (or a player's Place my token), then its stat block, then Combat (Hit Points, conditions and rests) and Companions (the list, Add a companion and Stat blocks) open, then Notes, Image, Stats and, for the DM, Played by folded, each as this device left it, and Delete at the foot. A player who plays one character opens straight onto its page, until they go back to the list, which then stays until they leave the room or reload the page. New character, Add a companion and a character's stat blocks open as pages too, with Back in the header. The two forms have Cancel at their foot, and a form with something typed in asks "Discard changes?" before Back or Cancel throws it away.
- A player can delete a character only they play, whoever made it and whether or not players can create characters, at any time, mid-fight included. Every token of it on every map, its companions and its place in the fight go with it, as when the DM deletes it. It cannot be undone, so the page says first what goes, counting only what the player can see and naming the rest in general words, and Delete asks for a second tap. A screen reader hears the same as Delete's description. The DM's log says who deleted what, such as "Pat deleted Bree, with 2 tokens and its place in the fight." A character the player shares with someone else stays the DM's to delete, and its page says so.
- **Class resources**: a character's counted features, such as Rage, Channel Divinity or Lay on Hands, at most 12. Add picks them by class and class level from the 29 SRD 5.2.1 presets, with their counts filled (Natural Recovery is two, its free spell and its spell slots, each back on a Long Rest of its own), a subclass's left unticked, and a count from a modifier read from the stat block or typed, or takes one of your own, with a count or an amount and the rest that brings it back. They are used and restored from the token's panel, the character's page, the turn card, the tracker row's Uses and the action bar's Uses, a pool such as Lay on Hands by an amount you type. When the character's level rises, the section asks once per class to update the counts, and Edit and Remove change one. Re-sync from D&D Beyond, and the stat block's D&D Beyond import, bring in the class's, species' and feats' limited uses with their counts and rests. A resource added this way takes the uses spent there. For one you already have, the count spent here stays unless you tick D&D Beyond's in the review. Items' charges and spells stay out. Only the DM and the character's players see them.
- Short Rest and Long Rest follow the SRD. A Short Rest adds the HP the player rolled on Hit Point Dice, restores one use of each class resource that returns one at a time, such as Rage, all of each that returns on a Short Rest, and the abilities a Short Rest restores. A Long Rest restores all HP, ends Temporary HP, removes one Exhaustion level, and restores legendary uses, spent abilities and every class resource. A chosen rest names the class resources it restores. The DM can rest the whole party at once, and when any of the party has a class resource spent, the rest's card says it also restores every class resource.
- The Monsters panel (DM) holds all 330 SRD 5.2.1 stat blocks. Search, then drop one on the map with average HP (or a typed number), AC, Speed, size, Initiative and senses.
- Filters like D&D Beyond's: challenge rating, creature type, legendary, lair, size, condition and damage immunities, resistances, vulnerabilities, movement, senses and alignment. Or type it in the search: "cr 5", "cr 1-5", "cr 1/2-3", "cr 10-".
- Custom monsters: the DM enters a full stat block from a book the group owns, or uses "Paste a stat block" to read pasted text into the editor. They stay in that room only.
- Full stat blocks on tokens: a monster or NPC token's panel has its stat block right under its name for the DM, with its abilities three across as a character's are, saves, skills, senses, languages, CR, traits, actions, Bonus Actions, Reactions and Legendary Actions, and folds. "Stat block" in the token's action bar or a tracker row's options opens it. Edit opens a room monster in the editor, and an SRD monster offers "Copy to this room's monsters and edit". "Attach a stat block" links a token that has none, and Detach unlinks it.
- A player whose token is an SRD monster, such as a druid's Wild Shape, can read its SRD block, and a companion's players read its block. A room monster's block never reaches players, a companion's included.

## DM tools

- "View as a player" shows exactly what a chosen player sees.
- "Follow me" makes players' maps follow the DM's as they pan and zoom. "Bring everyone here" moves them once. A ping with "Look here" on moves every player's view to that spot.
- "Black out players' screens" for a break or a reveal. Players see only a black screen that says "The DM has paused the game." Meanwhile their devices receive nothing from the game but the X-card. When the game resumes, each player's selected token, its action bar and any HP typed into it and not applied come back as they were.
- Undo and Redo for the DM's changes, each saying what it took back. They sit in the top bar.
- **Docs page**: the app lists every written doc at /docs, each with its title and a line on what it covers, for anyone, signed in or not. Home has a quiet "Docs" button after its cards, and the DM guide has an "All docs" button beside Home. For now the list holds the DM guide.
- **DM guide**: the app serves the [DM guide](docs/dm-guide.md) at /docs/dm-guide, for anyone, signed in or not, laid out like a printed guide. Its contents shows Start here cards for the opening sections and a card per numbered part with its numbered sections, and each part opens like a chapter. Procedures read as numbered steps, keys as keycaps, tips and notes as callouts, and screenshots as framed figures with captions. On a wide screen an outline beside the text marks the section you're reading, and on a phone the Contents button opens the same outline as a sheet. A link such as /docs/dm-guide#areas opens on that section. Menu, DM guide opens it in a new tab for the DM and the players. For the DM, the panels it covers (Token, Initiative, Dice, Areas, DM, Scenes, Monsters, Encounters, Characters and Outliner) end in a small Guide link to their section, and the empty Token and Initiative panels link to its quick start.
- The DM panel leads with Handouts, then Players' screens (Follow me, Bring everyone here and Black out), then Check their view (View as a player and Party sight), then the player link and the table display.
- **Outliner**: a panel listing everything on the scene in view, in groups that fold: Creatures and objects, Map (stamps, labels, pins and lights), Areas, Drawings, Doors, and Traps. Each row says what players get, such as Hidden, Under fog, Out of the party's sight, DM only, or a secret door hidden or found. The search filters as you type. A tap frames the item on the map and picks it: a token opens its action bar, a label or pin its pane, an area the Area tool, a stamp or light the builder in its mode, and a trap its pane with the Trap tool. A drawing, door or window flashes.
- **Party bar**: each player character's portrait, HP with Temporary HP and a bar, AC on the same shield as the tokens', hidden while dead as theirs is, Passive Perception as "PP 13", and up to three conditions. Dying, Stable or Dead replace the HP bar. On a wide screen it sits above the side panel's tabs, hides with the panel, and folds to one line: the names and HP, which scroll sideways when they don't all fit (with a mouse the wheel scrolls them, and Tab reaches each one), and Ask, short for Ask the party. On a phone it is the Party panel. A long name ends in "…" and its HP stays in view. A tap jumps to that character's token and opens its action bar, and a character with no token on the scene in view reads "Not on this scene".

## Journal and map pins

- The Journal panel holds pages in folders. The DM writes them, and picks who can read each one: DM only, Everyone, or one player.
- Pages use Markdown: bold, italic, headings, lists, numbered lists, quotes and https links.
- A page is made of blocks, and any block can be a secret. Players don't see a secret until the DM taps Reveal on it, and Hide takes it back. Revealed secrets read as ordinary text.
- When a page a player can read is new or changes, the Journal button shows a dot and the page reads "New" or "Updated".
- **Map pins**: the Pin tool (DM) places a pin with a tap. Its pane sets a label, a linked page, a color and "DM only", and says who will see the pin. If the server refuses a change, the pane keeps it and says why. Tap a pin to open its pane, or drag it to move it. A page's "Pin to the map" places a pin linked to that page.
- Players see a pin only on their scene, out of the fog, and when they can read its linked page. Deleting a page makes its pins DM only.
- With the Select tool, a tap on a pin opens its page, or shows its label when it links none, until a few seconds pass or the next tap elsewhere on the map.
- A room holds up to 200 pages, and a scene up to 200 pins.

## Log, chat and handouts

- The Log holds chat and the important events: HP changes, conditions and turns.
- Whisper to the DM or to one player. Only the sender and the recipients see a whisper.
- While the Log is closed, its button counts new messages, and a whisper to you shows a short notice.
- The Log shows its newest lines, and Show older brings back the rest, a room brought in from a backup included. Each person's whispers, the lines only some players read, such as a roll's answer, and the DM's own notes keep a count of their own, so a whisper between others, or a line someone else's creature or only the DM gets, never pushes a line out of anyone else's Log. A line written while the table is paused that no player could see stays the DM's when play resumes.
- **Export** in the Log saves it as a Markdown file, one evening or all of it. A gap of more than 4 hours starts a new evening. Everyone can export, and the file holds only what that person's Log shows. On an iPhone it saves through the share sheet. Listing the evenings and each export count toward 10 an hour per person.
- **Handouts** (DM panel): show an image to everyone or to chosen players as a popup. It pops up for the players in the room when the DM shows it, or shows it again. A player who joins, or opens the room in a new tab, finds the handouts already showing in the Library instead. A handout can also be a PDF: Upload a file in Show a handout takes an image or a PDF, and "From starter packs" there offers 200 openly licensed pictures to show: the Illustrated Bestiary's monsters, the portrait packs, and Historical art's people, creatures, places, items and documents, each copied into the room with its credit. The DM chooses which pages, up to 100, and the DM's browser turns them into pages, showing its progress in the DM panel, and names the handout after the PDF's title or file name. Players scroll it like a PDF, page with the Previous page and Next page buttons or Page Up, Page Down, Space, Home and End, and zoom over the whole document, down to a whole page. Download saves a handout of several pages as a PDF. A PDF with a password or an XFA form is refused, and nothing inside a PDF runs. A tap on a handout's picture under Showing now opens it for the DM as the players see it. Players can zoom in with a pinch, a double tap, the wheel, the zoom buttons or the + and − keys. On a handout of several pages the wheel scrolls through them, and Ctrl with the wheel, or a trackpad's pinch, zooms. Zooming uses the whole popup. On a tablet or a computer the popup starts as a card, and zooming in opens it to the whole window, at once with a button, a key or the wheel, or when a pinch ends. A small pinch then settles with the whole picture fitted to the window. Zoom out or Reset zoom, the turning arrow left of the zoom buttons, brings the card back, and Expand opens it to the window without zooming. On a phone it fills the screen from the start. Anyone shown a handout can download it with the popup's Download button, saved under the handout's title. On a phone that can share files, Download opens the share sheet instead, to save the image to Photos or Files or send it on. A closed handout stays closed after a reload, and a reload of the same tab brings back the one that was open. Any handout showing opens again from the Library. Its picture there opens it too, at the page it was left on, and closing it comes back to the Library.

## Drawing, pings and the laser

- The Draw tool: freehand, line, rectangle, circle, polygon, arrow and text, with color, fill, opacity and line width. Everyone draws in their own color by default.
- Players erase and undo their own drawings. The DM can clear everything and can make drawings DM only.
- "Who can draw" in Room settings: no one, every player (the default), or chosen players. A player who may not draw keeps the drawings they made. While they have some on the scene, their Draw tool offers only the eraser and "Clear mine". Rulers, pings and the laser stay for everyone.
- **Ping**: tap a spot to ping it, with a sound, and a vibration on Android for everyone but you. The DM's "Look here" moves every player's view there. With the Select tool, a press held still on the map, off any token, pings that spot too.
- **Laser**: drag with the Ping tool for a stroke that fades on its own and is never saved.
- Pings, the laser and rulers show who made them, in their color.

## The table display

Show the players' view of the map on a TV at the table, with nothing else on screen.

- The DM sets it up from the DM panel's Table display section:
  - "Add a TV" shows a code of 10 letters and numbers (such as `K7QH 2MXA 4F`), a link and a QR code, good once and for 10 minutes. On the TV's browser, "Set up a table display" on Home takes the code.
  - "Open on this device" opens the display in a new window, for a laptop wired to the TV. The window draws only what players get, but the laptop is still signed in as the DM.
- The display shows the players' scene as players see it: the map, the fog, party vision, tokens, shared areas, drawings, pins (except those linked to a page not every player can read), and pings and rulers. It shows no bars, panels, chat or log, roll requests or Initiative strip, and the room sends it no log lines at all.
- It also shows the pause screen during a blackout, handouts shown to everyone, the X-card, and "Waiting for the DM to choose a scene" when there is none.
- A handout of several pages shows one page at a time, with its page number. The arrow and page keys step through it, and a tap shows Previous page, Next page and Close.
- A tap, a click or any key shows a menu for 5 seconds: Follow the DM, Fit the map, "Show whose turn it is" (off at first), Text size (85% to 130% for the names, chips and badges on the map), Full screen, and Leave. The display remembers these choices. While the X-card or a one-page handout is open, a tap closes it instead.
- Full screen works on the display as in the room: in Chrome and Edge, Escape still closes the menu or a handout, and holding Escape leaves full screen.
- **Follow the DM** shows the part of the map a DM device shows, scaled to the TV. Turn on "Show my view on the table display" on that DM device. It follows only while the DM views the players' scene. Follow me, Bring everyone here and Look here move it too.
- **Fit the map** fits what players can see, the revealed ground and the party's tokens, and fits again when a reveal adds more.
- A display can't move or change anything, and it is not on the player list. A room can have up to 4.
- The DM devices list shows each display with Sign out. Changing the player password signs every display out. Leave, pressed twice on the display, signs it out for good. A display that has not connected for 30 days is signed out. A display shows one room: pairing it with another room, in another tab or window, signs it out of the first, which then says it now shows another room.

## Safety: the X-card

- "X-card" sits at the top of the Menu, for the DM and every player. A tap opens a small card, and a tap on its large X sends it, so a stray tap never does. Once it goes out, the sender's Menu closes, so their screen looks like everyone else's.
- Every device in the room then shows the same calm card, the sender's included: "Someone tapped the X-card. Edit out what just happened, with no questions asked." It shows on table displays too.
- Nothing covers the card. On a member's device, a handout the DM shows while it is up, or one that comes back when a pause ends, waits until the card is dismissed. On a table display the handout opens under the card, and a tap closes the card first.
- A player behind the pause screen still has the X-card there, and the card shows to them as to everyone.
- The DM's card adds "Pause the game", which blacks out the players' screens.
- Nothing about a tap is saved, not even in the log, so nobody at the table, the DM included, can tell who sent it.
- A tap within 10 seconds of a card joins that card instead of showing another. Each device can send the X-card up to 6 times an hour.
- The X-card is John Stavropoulos's safety tool, credited on the About page.

## This device

Menu, This device holds settings kept on this device alone:

- **Interface size**: 85%, 100%, 115% or 130%. It scales the bars, the panels, the names and marks on tokens, and the readings of rulers and drags on the map, and dialogs and panels still fit the screen at every size. The map keeps its own zoom.
- **Full screen**: the room fills the whole screen, where the browser allows it. The switch is not there on an iPhone.
- **Low power**: draws the map at a lower resolution, loads the phone size map, skips small animations, and shows rolls without dice, to save battery on long sessions. Text on the map looks softer.
- **Map detail**: Phone size (up to 4096 pixels across, loads faster) or Full size (sharper on a large screen).
- **Turn sound** and **Ping sound**.
- **Show others' rolls as they happen**: a toast for each roll someone else makes while the Dice panel is not on screen. On at first.
- **Roll animation**: your own rolls in the Dice panel throw dice that land on the result in about a second. On at first. Its help says so where Low power or the device keeps the dice from showing.
- **Dice**: eight dice sets for your throws, Ivory (the default), Obsidian, Ruby, Sapphire, Emerald, Amethyst, Gold and Steel, each a d20 showing 20 in its colors and finish. The choice is kept on this device, and nobody else sees it. Off with the Roll animation switch.
- "Show the shortcuts": the keys, and what a key or another mouse button does with a click, a drag or the wheel. With a keyboard, each single key can be changed there for this device.

## Look and accessibility

- A dark theme. In a room, buttons take your own member color, adjusted so they stay readable.
- One font everywhere: the app draws its text, the names and numbers on the map included, in Inter, an open font that comes with it, so a row reads and wraps the same on an iPhone, an Android phone, Windows and Linux. Numbers that line up or change in place, such as HP, Initiative, rounds, dice totals, counts and the ruler's reading, keep every digit the same width. Inter is credited on the About page.
- The app's words follow the SRD 5.2.1's style: ability abbreviations in capitals (STR, DEX), and the game's terms capitalized where they name a rule, such as Initiative, Advantage, Hit Points, Short Rest, Critical Hit, Concentration, a Death Saving Throw, a 20 ft Sphere and 8d6 Fire. A modifier reads with a true minus (DEX −1), while a formula keeps the hyphen you type (d20-1). What you type stays as you typed it.
- Large touch targets, 16 px text fields so an iPhone doesn't zoom in, and room for the notch and the home bar.
- Icons with their names on hover and focus: a button that is an icon alone, such as Close, Undo, a trash can for Delete or the arrows that move a row, shows its name beside it after half a second of hover, at once on keyboard focus, and on a touch held half a second, and a screen reader hears its name. The name stays up a moment and a half after the finger lifts, until another name shows, since one shows at a time, and a hold that showed it never also presses the button, while Enter, Space and a screen reader still press it at once. A stepper that repeats while held, as the Height tool's 5 ft lower and 5 ft higher do, is the one exception: it steps from the moment the finger lands. The name sits just above or below its icon at every interface size, never wider than the screen. Where a mouse would learn an icon from its name but a finger cannot hover, as for Hidden, Locked and Duplicate in the token's action bar, a touch screen shows the words beside the icon. In the token's action bar, on a pin, a light or a map label, and in the journal, Delete is a trash can alone until its first tap, then the trash can and its question. In the token's action bar the trash can keeps room for its question beside it, so the second tap lands where the first did on any screen. One icon means one thing everywhere: Hidden from players is an eye struck through, Roll again two looping arrows apart from Redo, and Ping a radar apart from Pin. The Menu's rows wear their icons, and the toolbar keeps its tools' names under their icons. Each panel has an icon of its own, in the side panel's tabs, on More and in its list, on the bar between two panels and in a phone's Panels list. Each group of the Token panel and a character's page starts with an icon before its name, and a folded group's summary gives way first, so the name and a button such as Take a shape keep their line. In a fight, the tracker's and the party bar's condition chips show the same pictures as the map's chips, where a chip shows no number: exhaustion keeps its level, as "E2", and a counted effect such as Hex its letters and count. The turn timer wears a stopwatch, the turn card folds by the chevron before its title and closes by its x, and the time-up line and the line for an effect that ended start with the stopwatch or the effect's picture. A token's panel reads "Hidden" with the eye struck through, its full words "Hidden from players" as the tag's tooltip and for a screen reader, and "Locked" with its padlock. New page, New encounter, New character and Add a companion start with a plus, and a character page's Delete with a trash can. The interface icons come from Lucide, credited on the About page.
- Respects the system's reduced-motion setting.
- In a room, the browser tab reads `RaccoonVTT · <room name>`.
- A click or tap outside a dialog closes it, except a handout or a picture from the Library.

## Backups and moving a room

- Room settings, Backup: "Download a backup" saves the whole room as one .zip file, with every scene, token, character, journal page and image, starter picks and their credits included. At most 4 an hour.
- Home, "Import a room": "Import from a backup" makes a new room from that file, on this server or another, up to 95 MB. The server owner can import a bigger one from the command line. A backup whose images are more than a room may store on this server is refused whole, saying so.

## Running the server

For whoever runs RaccoonVTT. [README.md](README.md) has the setup steps and every command.

- It runs with Docker Compose behind any HTTPS reverse proxy or tunnel, such as Caddy, nginx or Cloudflare Tunnel, with no open ports. An outbound proxy is its only way to the internet, for image search and imports from a link.
- Google image search needs a SerpApi key. Openverse needs none. The Turnstile human check is optional.
- The strict egress port also allows `character-service.dndbeyond.com`, for the D&D Beyond import. Removing it from `docker/squid.conf` turns the link import off, and pasted JSON still works.
- **Nightly backups**: every night (at 04:00 by default) a backup copies the database and the images it needs, then checks the copy. It keeps the newest 7 good backups and one from each of the last 4 weeks. A run that cannot finish deletes nothing, so the last good backup always remains, and every run leaves 2 GiB free on the disk. One command runs a backup now, and another restores one. The backups live on the same disk as everything else, so copy them off the server now and then.
- **Admin page** at `/admin`, off until the server owner sets an admin password:
  - Storage: the image volume, the database, the images on disk, the images waiting for cleanup, and the last backup and the last good one. It warns when the last backup failed or no good one has run for 36 hours.
  - Every room with its size, images, last activity and whether it is loaded, sorted by last activity or size.
  - Pin or unpin a room (a pinned room is never unloaded to make space), or delete one after typing its room ID.
  - It never shows what is in a room and cannot enter one. A session lasts 12 hours, and repeated wrong passwords close the login for a while.
- **Cleanup of unused images**: once a day the server deletes replaced scene thumbnails, and once a week image files nothing names. Deleted image files wait 48 hours first, so a backup that is running still finds them. Images in a room's library are deleted only by its DM (see Images and art).
- **Admin commands** on the command line list rooms, create a room past the daily limits, pin or unpin a room, replace a lost DM link, import a large room backup, and make the admin password's hash.
- The X-card leaves nothing in the app's data or its logs. The HTTPS front end's own request logs, such as a reverse proxy's access log or Cloudflare's, can still record each request's address, the X-card's included, as they do for every request.
