# Writing the docs

These rules cover every written doc in the repository: the README, the operator docs, FEATURES.md and the DM guide. They keep the docs short, true and pleasant to read. Each Bad and Good below is a short quote or a description of the pattern.

**For every doc**

1. **Say each fact once, where its reader looks for it, and link to it from anywhere else.** Bad: an update note that writes out the whole going-back recipe ("check out the commit you ran before, rebuild, then follow Restore under Backups with the backup taken just before the update") that the note above it already holds. Good: one "Going back to an older build" recipe, and a note that gives only what is its own: "Companion stat blocks: room format 10, change format 6. Until it reloads, an old page fails on the first change to a companion's stat block it receives."
2. **Give the value: a path, port, key, setting, default, limit or command.** Bad: "Anyone who reaches the site can also create a room from its Home page, a few a day per address." Good: "... 5 a day per address by default (`ROOMS_PER_IP_PER_DAY`)." In the guide, Bad: "Each room can store only so much". Good: "Each room stores up to 1,024 MB unless the server sets another limit".
3. **State, don't sell.** No hype words (powerful, seamless, effortless, beautiful, intuitive, smarter, handy), no "makes it easy", "earns its place", "pays off" or "works perfectly well". In the README and the operator docs, write "you can" or the verb rather than "lets you". In the guide, "lets you" is natural prose and stays. Bad: "A stat block makes the rest of the app smarter about that character." Good: "A stat block feeds the rest of the app: the Dice panel offers its rolls, roll requests know its bonuses, and the group save knows its resistances and Evasion."
4. **List real things only.** No "no X, no Y, no Z", and no three-part rhythm for its own sake. Bad: "With it a server needs no copy of this repository and builds nothing: two files are the whole setup." Good: "Each release is an image on Docker Hub, so a server needs only two files from the release, `compose.yml` and `.env`."
5. **Say what the app does, not what it lacks.** Leave out what is absent unless a reader would otherwise assume it exists and act on it, and then say what happens instead. Bad: "There are no accounts." Good: "Whoever creates a room becomes its DM through a private DM link, and players join with the room's ID and password, or by asking the DM to let them in, as the DM chooses." Bad: "There is no voice or video chat." Good: "a group that plays apart talks over a call of its own". An absence a reader would act on stays: "None of the art is AI generated."
6. **Assume the reader knows the basics.** An operator knows Docker, a shell and a reverse proxy. A DM knows D&D, a phone and a browser. Explain the app, not the world. Bad: "A phone or a tablet shows no keys, since it has nothing to press them on, until you press a key on a keyboard joined to it." Good: "A phone or tablet shows the keys once you press one on a keyboard joined to it."
7. **Open with the first useful fact.** The heading names the topic. A first sentence that defines the tool is useful, one that announces the section is not. Bad: "This section builds the image on the server from a copy of the repository." Good: "The **Initiative** panel runs the fight: who is in it, the order they act in, and the turns and rounds."
8. **Concise is not choppy.** Join related facts into sentences with "and", "so", "while" or "which", and cut padding, not connectives. A paragraph of short flat statements reads as a fact list. Join only facts that belong together, and never extend a list with a clause that is not part of it. Split a sentence a reader has to read twice. Bad: "There are no accounts. Players can sit at one table, join from elsewhere, or both. There is no voice or video chat." Good: "A group can sit around one table, play from elsewhere, or both, and a group that plays apart talks over a call of its own."
9. **Keep the reason and the caveat.** A step's "because", a limit and what can go wrong stay, however short the rest gets. Good: "Open exactly that address, not `localhost`, because the WebSocket checks Origin exactly." Good: "The map image itself reaches players whole, and each player's device draws the fog over it. A group has to trust its players with that." Bad: either cut to its first clause.
10. **Say what a guarantee means for the reader, not how it is enforced or tested.** Keep the mechanism only where an operator needs it to act. Bad: "So even a reload or the browser's developer tools cannot show them." Good: "Players' devices receive only what the DM has revealed and what the party can see." In the assurance case and SECURITY.md, the mechanism stays.
11. **Write for this page's reader.** Name what they see: a release, a setting, a button, a log line. Internal names (waves, batches, phases, design section numbers, field names, reducer) belong in design notes, and contributor rules in CONTRIBUTING.md. Bad: "The update that splits Undo into Play and Setup history (docs/design/design-v1-undo-scope.md, batch A) saves rooms at snapshot version 30". Good: under **v0.3.0**, "Play and Setup history: room format 30, change format 34."
12. **Plain American English, no em dashes, no semicolons in prose.** Contractions are fine. docs/development.md's Words line says the same for comments, docs and the app, and links here. Bad: two clauses joined by a semicolon or a dash. Good: "Back up first, then update:"

**The README and the operator docs**

13. **Concrete steps.** Steps are imperative, commands whole and ready to paste, and each step says what success looks like. Bad: a step that ends at `docker compose ps` and says nothing of what it should show. Good: "`raccoon`, `postgres` and `egress` should show `(healthy)` within about 30 seconds, and `backup` should show `Up`."
14. **The README is the front page:** what it is (a paragraph of connected prose), what it does (a short list of the main areas, then FEATURES.md), how it runs (a paragraph with real values), how to try and install it (the commands), the docs, the license. The list is not complete and need not be: FEATURES.md is. Bad: a count that grows with each new pack ("45 openly licensed starter maps"). Good: "the openly licensed starter maps".
15. **Update notes:** one bullet per update, under its release, with the formats it steps, what a page left open gets wrong until it reloads, any step beyond the going-back recipe, and any other effect an operator sees or must act on, including its own going-back caveat. Bad: a note that repeats the whole recipe and the reload line. Good: "**The isometric view**: room format 26, change format 25. Until it reloads, an old page has no View button, and an old table display drops what a DM in Isometric shows it, so Follow the DM stands still there."

**FEATURES.md**

16. **One bullet, one fact.** A bullet past about 100 words becomes a lead line and sub-bullets, one fact each. Bad: one bullet of several hundred words, its facts joined by commas. Good: "The DM sees everything. Hidden things show translucent or dimmed to the DM."
17. **Plain words first, layout last.** Pixel gaps, millisecond timings and layout rules go in the bullet's last sub-bullet, labeled "Layout:", after the plain-words sub-bullets. A number a DM acts on (a cap, a size, a time) stays in the plain words. Bad: "Toasts keep 8 px left of a docked action bar" in the middle of what a DM reads. Good: "A request ends by itself after 10 minutes." in the plain words, and "Layout: toasts keep 8 px left of a docked action bar" last.
18. **The app's words, exactly.** Quote what the screen says, in quotes or bold, and keep the registry's words for a key or gesture. The Shortcuts section matches the in-app sheet word for word (client/state/shortcuts.test.ts), so it changes only with the registry. Bad: a button named in the writer's words rather than its label. Good: "press **Hide panels**, the last button in the row of tabs (or the `S` key)".
19. **One home per fact.** A feature that shows in two areas is described in one and named in the other. Bad: one roll option told in full under each area it touches. Good: "Images in a room's library are deleted only by its DM (see Images and art)."

**The DM guide**

20. **Guide prose, as in a Dungeon Master's Guide:** full sentences, "you", the reason behind each step, and an example from play where it shows when to use a tool. Bad: a clipped list of steps with no reasons. Good: "There is no Let in all on purpose: one careless tap should never let a crowd in."
21. **One point per paragraph, said once.** The alt text describes the picture for a screen reader, and the body says what to do. Bad: a paragraph under a picture that describes the picture again, then repeats the bullets above it. Good: alt text that says what the picture shows, and a paragraph that says what to press.
22. **The rule first, then its edge cases in a short list, or in FEATURES.md.** Bad: a rule followed by every exception in the same long paragraph, so the rule is hard to find. Good: "Asking has limits so that a stranger who knows your room's ID can't flood you with requests. At most 8 requests wait at once from one address and 50 in a room".
23. **Phones first, and one sentence for what differs elsewhere.** The phone is the main device, so its gestures are taught in full. Another device gets one sentence on what differs, not a tour. Bad: a mouse paragraph that retells each phone gesture in mouse terms, arrow by arrow. Good: "On a tablet held upright, the sheet keeps to the width of a dialog and sits in the middle of the screen."
24. **Scenarios walk through the steps and link the rule.** They don't teach it again. Bad: a scenario step that explains again how a pit works. Good: "Pick the **Fog** tool and reveal what the party can see (see [Fog](#fog))."
25. **Warmth once.** One sentence of encouragement, one evocative line where it says when to reach for a tool. Bad: "The app is there to add to your game, never to get in its way." after a line that already said so. Good: "The most important rule at any table is that everyone has fun, you included."
26. **What the page needs:** no tables (the page draws them a cell to a line), asides as `> [!NOTE]` or `> [!TIP]` alerts, every picture in docs/dm-guide/ shown, and headings never renamed, since panels, tests and outside links use their anchors. Bad: a renamed heading that a panel's Guide link points at. Good: a tighter paragraph under the same heading.

**Release notes**

27. **feat, fix and perf subjects become CHANGELOG.md and the GitHub Release notes,** so write them for the DM, player or operator: what changed, in the app's words. Internal steps use refactor, test or chore, which the changelog skips. CHANGELOG.md is generated: never edit it by hand. Bad: "**migrate**: op version 37, an identity step with the gates for trap stops between a walk's ends and the hex outline clip". Good: "**traps**: a trap stops a creature by the ground every player sees".
