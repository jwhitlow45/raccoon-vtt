## 2. Summary

- **A player holds the build only where they have seen ground.** On every scene with fog on, chunks, labels and stamps are projected through each player's own fog. Free walls are cut at the edge of held cells.
- **Memory.** Where the DM edits ground a player remembers but does not see, the server keeps the old values (`memory`, `memoryBy`), written by a server-made op after the batch (`sys.remember`). The player sees those until their sight or a DM reveal reaches that ground again.
- **Derived facts come from the server.** A player's chunk says which cells it holds (`known`) and carries the automatic walls and stair directions of what it holds (`auto`, `upDir`). Stub chunks carry them where no chunk is stored, so no device invents or loses a wall at the edge of its map.
- **The touch rule.** Players move into unseen ground as today. A walk the real build stops lands at its last clear spot, and a stop that touched ground the player does not hold says only "Something blocks the way."
- **Scene setup.** "Players see": Explore, Reveal by hand or Everything, with a room default, "Like <scene>" and "Use these settings on other scenes". The settings page regroups around what players see.
- **Cost**, prototype at the caps: 2 to 4 ms to mask a 200 x 200 build for one fog class, 0.1 ms for a move's 9 chunks. Seven classes take 11 ms and 0.7 ms.

## 3. Decisions, with rejected options

Asking the owner is off, so each choice the decisions leave open is a guess at the owner's likely answer.

1. **Held.** A cell is held when any of its sub-cells is revealed in the viewer's fog. Terrain, heights and marks go by their cell, an edge by either cell beside it, a label or stamp by its box (today's rule). On hexes a cell's sub-cells are those inside the hex (HX-D10). Rejected: a sub-cell test per element (a free wall could then be withheld inside a held cell and still stop a move there).
2. **Free walls are cut at held cells** (D-261026-29 items 7 and 8). Every free wall as players see it, hidden secret doors and windows included, is clipped to the held cells it crosses, never truncated and never merged across an unheld gap. A cut end is the exact crossing of the cell's side. Every end on a cell's side or corner, cut or stored alike, is then moved away from the piece's other end: each coordinate to the next multiple of 1/10000 cell strictly past it, where a coordinate equal to the other end's stays (LK-36, D-261026-29 item 16). A stored end strictly inside a cell stays exact, and a split inside a hex moves like a cut. So no end on a side tells a wall that stops there from one that runs on, past held ground or into remembered cells whose memory keeps the old wall (MD2-LK-1, MD2-LK-6). The step is taken in exact integers, floor(10000x) + 1 moving up and ceil(10000x) - 1 moving down, since a crossing is a ratio of lattice integers on squares and on hexes alike, whose corners lie on the 1/64 lattice (`cornerAt`, shared/rules/hex.ts:219), so floating error never leaves an end short (LK-37). So a coordinate prints in at most its integer digits and 7 characters, each end moves at most 1/10000 cell per coordinate, and every point of the exact piece lies within 2/10000 cell of the piece sent, since both are segments. The piece sent can tilt along its whole length, which the device's tolerance covers (5.2, ASTRA-30). Two pieces meeting at a split line or a seam overlap by up to 2/10000 cell, which is harmless since they are one wall. An end on the scene's own side moves too, and a coordinate whose step would leave the scene is clamped to the side, so a wall that runs off the scene reads as one that stops there and every coordinate stays on the grid (MD2-LK-6, MD2-O-7). On a scene with fog on, every free wall a player gets but a visible door, whatever cut it (an unheld cell, a side between live and remembered cells, or nothing), gets the id formed by the first 12 symbols of the free-wall id alphabet from `hash128` (shared/project.ts, so it runs in shared code) over its scene, its two ends as sent and its public kind, and is filed under its own midpoint's position (D-261026-29 item 13). The ends go in `byCoordinates` order, which is also the order the piece is sent in, and each number as `String(n)`, so the id never depends on how the stored wall was drawn (LK-33, MD-R4-2). Every input is public, so no secret is needed and no real id enters. Equal pieces in one position are one piece, and a collision between different ones hashes again with a counter ("#1", "#2", and on), in `byCoordinates` order of the colliding pieces (MD-R4-1). Only visible free doors stay whole, with their real id and chunk, since door ops need the midpoint (`doorPlace`): their geometry, at most 8 cells, is an accepted channel. Pieces exist only in players' copies, so no op or stored wall leaves the lattice. Rejected: whole free walls (ASTRA-1, LK-1), hidden doors or windows whole (MD-O-33, LK-22), pieces sharing the real id or chunk (ASTRA-8), moving only the stored ends that border an unheld cell (a wall that stops on a live cell's side beside remembered ground then reads apart from one the DM ran on into it, MD2-LK-1), real ids where no end borders an unheld cell (a cut between live and remembered cells then keeps one id on both sides of an unseen gap, ASTRA-8 reopened), ids keyed by the real id (a player who once held the wall whole knows it) or by a per-room secret (one to persist, export and rotate for no gain), keeping only the longest pieces (drops held walls and shows unseen gaps, ASTRA-16), stepping cut ends to the wall's next 1/64 lattice point (an imported diagonal often has none between its ends), rounding to the next double (a coordinate prints in up to 24 characters, LK-36), a 1/65536 grid (its multiples print in up to 18).
3. **Live where the viewer sees or the DM revealed**, remembered elsewhere. A DM's reveal means "show them this". Rejected: memory on DM-revealed ground (fog-only scenes show revealed ground live by decision).
4. **Doors follow memory** (D-261026-29), replacing maps-vision question 16 and D-261025-17. Round 3 rejected per-player door memory for four failures, and this memory avoids each:
   - Seeding disclosed unseen doors: nothing is seeded. An entry is written only for an element on ground its layer already remembers, as that layer showed it.
   - Route corners leaked private door knowledge: corners follow decision 12, which reads only the receiving owner's sight and DM reveals.
   - Unbounded growth: memory holds only differences, at most 64 KiB per layer and scene.
   - Import breakage: memory is keyed and mapped like the explored layers (5.4), and goes with its layer when that cannot map.
5. **Labels and stamps follow memory, pins and found traps do not.** Pins are notes for players and found traps are players' own knowledge. Rejected: memory for pins.
6. **One memory per explored layer, read per element.** The party's layer has one, and each holder's own layer has one. If any cell an element touches is held by the viewer's own layer, they read their own entry, else live. Otherwise they read the party's entry, else live. Rejected: falling through to the party's entry on own ground (shows a holder a state they saw change, LK-7).
7. **Memory is a diff against the build now**, empty unless the DM edits remembered ground out of sight. Rejected: full chunk copies per layer (7 MB at the caps).
8. **Written after the batch, replayed as stored**, by the explore step: `sys.explore` then `sys.remember`. Rejected: the reducer writing memory inside each build op (each op would recompute sight).
9. **Past its cap a layer forgets whole fog tiles, and the builder asks first** (D-261026-29). Rejected: forgetting cell by cell (draws the edit's outline, LK-2), the whole scene (harsh for one paste), refusing the DM.
10. **Vision or fog off forgets the scene's memory**, and the controls say so first. Rejected: keeping memory while off (hides edits players already saw live).
11. **The touch rule** (D-261026-29). Its words are judged on the stopping step, from the landing to the next spot, and every stop or refusal whose step touches a cell off live ground gives the generic words, remembered ground included (LK-34). D-261027-04 amends a walk's words: a stop names the blocker the walk met where the part of it met lies on the player's live ground, a wall or door beside a live cell or through one, a ledge only between two live cells, whatever lies unseen past it. Rejected: M4's ban on entering unheld ground (freezes a blind or unlit creature, stops every step past a reveal, changes play at deploy), refusing a stopped walk in place (free probing from one spot, MD-O-32), drawing what a creature touched (their map would hold geometry their sight never reached), the whole attempted leg for the words (a stop at a wall the player holds would get generic words).
12. **Route corners on scenes with memory** reach a non-moving owner of a private leg only where every spot is in that owner's sight or on DM-revealed ground. Rejected: any ground the owner's fog reveals (draws the mover's memory or private sight, ASTRA-3).
13. **Areas, per receiver** (D-261026-29 item 5). A player gets an area's reminder, caught line or target ring only when every ray its inclusion test reads (footprint samples under `anyCell` or `majority`, every emanation origin) lies on that player's live ground: in sight or DM-revealed, or revealed on a fog-only scene. This holds for every receiving player and for the DM's areas. The DM's roll requests stay the DM's choice. Rejected: the receiver's held build with memory (stale memory would tell a player they are caught when they are not), one representative line (ASTRA-9), the caster's ground alone (LK-24).
14. **Border facts ride stub chunks.** Every chunk position with a held cell or held edge is sent, one position past the east and south bounds included, as a stub where no chunk is stored. Rejected: east and south border bits in each chunk with `known` from the device's fog (the device's walls would then read fog and lose their cache by chunk identity).
15. **Case 6 is sight, and the builder says so.** Ground seen empty stays empty while out of sight. On open ground sight reaches everything, so what the DM builds in view shows as built. Rejected: unpainted void blocking sight (changes sight and blinds a mat).
16. **Presets** are Explore (fog and vision on), Reveal by hand (fog on, vision off) and Everything (both off). Fog off with vision on reads Custom. Light and Sight stay their own rows. Rejected: presets that set light.
17. **The room default copies the preset only.** Under Explore a map image or starter map starts on Reveal by hand, since sight with no walls shows all its art. A blank mat starts on Explore: the party sees the open mat anyway, and a dungeon built on it later follows memory. The room's first Battle mat stays Everything, so joining players see a mat, not fog. "Like <scene>" copies fog, vision with its light and Sight, and movement blocking, and keeps the new kind's automatic walls rule (D-261026-29). Rejected: mats on Everything, copying a builder's walls rule onto an image.
18. **Copy onto other scenes copies Players see, Light and Sight.** Rejected: copying walls and movement (changes walls of built scenes).
19. **The settings page folds on phones**, What players see open. Rejected: a separate vision page.

## 5. The model

### 5.1 What a player holds

| Scene | Never-seen | Remembered (explored, not in sight) | In sight or DM-revealed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fog on, vision on | nothing | memory per decision 6, else the build now | live |
| Fog on, vision off | nothing | live (no memory) | live |
| Fog off | the whole build | live | live |

- **Whose fog.** A player's own (the DM's, the party's and a holder's own layer, per-player 6.3). A display and a non-holder read the shared fog. A DM gets the whole build as today and no memory. View as a player is the server's projection, so it shows exactly this.

### 5.2 The player's chunk

`projectChunkForPlayer` becomes `playerChunk(state, at, view)`, for a chunk position and a fog class (5.6):

- **Flattened** as today (hidden secret doors as walls, locks closed, free walls sorted), plus automatic walls by the scene's rule on the real cells across borders. A hidden secret door on an edge the rule walls is an automatic wall there, so it sets that edge's `auto` bit (MD-O-1).
- **Each element** is held or not (decision 1) and takes its live or remembered value. Memory can bring back an element the build no longer has. A free wall goes per cell (D-261026-29 item 11, ASTRA-21, LK-32): its live value and each remembered version are cut by the viewer's held cells. Cells in sight or DM-revealed get the live value's pieces. Every other held cell gets the pieces of the remembered version that covers it, where one does, else the live value's. Equal pieces on neighboring cells join. Every run is then split where it crosses the lines x = 4m, or y = 4m for a run steeper than 45 degrees, counted from the scene's origin, and only then are ends rounded and ids made (ASTRA-25, ASTRA-26). So a piece spans at most 4 cells along its major axis and 5.66 in all, it depends only on the walls and held cells within those 4 cells, and a change at one end of a long run moves no piece past the 8 neighbors. Every run splits, whatever its length, since a length test would read the whole run, and 5.5 counts the pieces the lines add.
- **Player-only fields**, which the state schema refuses in stored chunks: `known` (44 base64 characters: the 256 held cells), `auto` (88: an automatic wall on each held north and west edge) and `upDir` (256: the direction of each held stair cell, `n`, `e`, `s`, `w` or `.`, present only where a held cell is a stair).
- **Stubs.** A position with a held cell or held edge and no stored chunk, from the bounds to one position past their east and south sides, gets a stub. So the east wall of a room on a chunk border or on the scene's own east side still reaches players, and so does a ledge beside a held empty cell there (MD-O-2, ASTRA-11). A position is withheld only when no cell, edge or wall piece of it is held.
- **One constructor** builds every position, stored or stub, so two that hold the same thing are the same bytes: `cells` and `edges` in sorted key order, since the socket sends plain `JSON.stringify` (connection.ts:155, MD-R3-3), `heights`, `up` and `rough` omitted when every held value is 0 or `.`, and `grid` on every position of a hex scene or on none (MD-R2-4).
- **The device.** The player's copy of the scene carries `autoWalls: "off"` on a scene with fog on. `walls.ts` reads `auto` as automatic walls, `upDir` in place of `stairDirections`, and derives no raised edge beside a cell outside `known`. A position with no chunk is all unknown.
- **Pieces on the device** (ASTRA-10, MD-R2-1, ASTRA-17, LK-30). A player's chunk takes its free walls through a piece path in `walls.ts` beside `validFreeWall`: ends finite, length above zero and at most 8 cells, and bounds checked exactly. Every piece passes with no slack, since it is at most 5.66 cells long and each end is a lattice value, a grid value or on the scene's side, so ASTRA-17's slack goes. Pieces skip `segments.ts`'s counting split, which indexes by lattice coordinates. The device tests pieces two ways, by what follows the test (below). Its `SegmentIndex` buckets each piece by every cell within 2/10000 cell of it. The device never works out which cells a piece crosses: selection and filing come from the server's exact geometry (ASTRA-30, MD-R7-1). Drawing reads them as they are, as Isometric already splits free walls by cell (`wallPieces`, client/canvas/relief/runs.ts:55). The device's area sweep splits them against the rest in a plain pass bucketed by cell, since device coverage is drawing and the receiver rule of decision 13. A contact within 2/10000 cell of any piece can differ from the server, whose landing then decides.
- **Two device tests** (ASTRA-30, ASTRA-34). Previews with no server check after them (area rays, caught lists, rings, walking rulers, a drag's drawn path, the words shown before sending) use the conservative test: a piece blocks anything within 2/10000 cell of it, so a T-junction on a tilted piece stays closed and a crossing that rounds to a corner touch still blocks. Gates on an action sent to the server use the permissive test: a piece blocks only where the path crosses its 2/10000-cell band clear through, more than 4/10000 cell from its ends, where the exact wall surely blocks. Within the band the device sends the intended action, and the server's landing or refusal decides by the touch rule, its words by live ground. The invariant: the device never refuses, shortens or withholds an action the exact retained pieces it holds allow. A remembered wall the DM removed can still stop a drag before it reaches the server. Looking there again or using Teleport here, which tests no wall, passes it. Its gate errors are only permissive, and the server corrects those.
- **The gates**, each built by MD4 with the permissive test: a drag's landing, its fallback and group legs (`planOne`, client/features/movement/drop.ts:255, `squeezedSpot`, :232), and a lone walk the permissive gate stops at its very first step sends nothing (D-261027-05): that gate stops only where the exact wall surely blocks, so nothing the exact retained pieces allow is withheld, and a remembered wall the DM removed stops a one-cell and a longer drag alike. Route legs and arrow steps (`LegCheck`, routeDrag.ts, routeWork.ts), the Isometric drag (client/canvas/isoDrag.ts:135) and Teleport here's squeezed spot (teleport.ts:39). The attach walk (client/features/attach/walk.ts), detach spots (detach.ts:105) and companion placement (`companionLegBlocker`, client/features/tokens/companions.ts). Door reach, for the pill's list and its press (`reachesDoor`, client/features/doors/doors.ts:181, DoorPill.tsx:117), and any panel that disables resize or squeeze by a local fit.

### 5.3 Memory: storage and growth

```ts
RoomState.memory: Record<string, MemoryChunk>     // "<sceneId>:<cx>,<cy>", the party's layer
RoomState.memoryBy: Record<string, MemoryChunk>   // "<sceneId>:<memberId>:<cx>,<cy>", a holder's
type MemoryChunk = {
  sceneId: SceneId; cx: number; cy: number; rev: number; memberId?: MemberId;
  // Each element as the layer last saw it, as players see it, only where that differs from now. null: absent then.
  cells?: Record<string, TerrainId | null>; edges?: Record<string, EdgeFeature | "auto" | null>;
  walls?: Record<string, { wall: FreeWall | null; cells: string[] }[]>;   // by real id: versions, each whole and flattened, and the cells it covers
  heights?: Record<string, number>;
  up?: Record<string, StairMark>; upDir?: Record<string, StairDir>; rough?: Record<string, boolean>;
  labels?: Record<string, Label | null>; stamps?: Record<string, Stamp | null>;   // by id
};
```

- **The map step**, after every batch that holds ops (server/rooms/explore.ts): `sys.explore` as today, then one `sys.remember { sceneId, layers, forget? }` per scene it changes, each layer a list of chunks with entries to set and keys to drop. Server-made, on no one's undo stack, replayed as stored, never computed on replay. No undo step guards or restores memory.
- **What it records.** The elements the batch can have changed: each touched chunk with its east and south neighbors, every chunk whose derived stair map changed (the scene's stair map before and after, ASTRA-4), every chunk of a scene whose `autoWalls` or `bounds` changed, and each touched label and stamp. An element whose flattened value changed, on a layer's remembered ground, out of that layer's sight after the batch and off DM-revealed ground, gets that layer's entry. Its value is what the layer showed before: its entry if it had one, else the value before. A changed free wall gets a version only over the cells it touched, before or after, that are remembered, out of sight and not covered by one of its versions already. A layer's sight is the shared sight for the party and theirs for a holder, on the player scene only, and none behind a blackout.
- **Auto walls** keep their kind: `"auto"` is a remembered automatic wall, and a null entry hides an automatic wall that appeared out of sight (MD-O-7).
- **Labels and stamps by id** (MD-O-9). An entry is judged by both boxes. It drops when the live box touches the layer's sight or revealed ground. It becomes `null` when only the remembered box does, since they see the thing is gone from there. An index by id finds entries, and an id only memory names gets a key, which the memo and `dependents` read.
- **Free walls by cell.** Sight or a reveal takes covered cells off a version, which drops when it covers none, and the entry drops when it has none. A version equal to the live wall drops. Every version counts toward the cap.
- **Dropping.** An entry equal to the build now drops, so a DM's undo leaves nothing. On the player scene the step drops every entry whose element touches the layer's sight or DM-revealed ground, a free wall by every held cell as above, scanning only entries on sight tiles the batch changed (by tile object, as explore.ts does).
- **Doors touched.** A player's `door.set` accepted, written or not, and one refused GONE after reach passed (6), drop that door's entries in every layer the sender reads (MD-O-6).
- **Effective caps and the ask** (D-261026-29, MD-O-34, ASTRA-13, MD-R2-6, MD-R2-9, ASTRA-19). A layer's effective cap is 64 KiB, or its share, in charged bytes (5.5). The actor keeps a running estimate per layer through the batch: its memory now plus what each accepted op could add, counting every changed element on remembered ground as out of sight, so it is never low, and any lowered share. A DM op that would put an estimate past its effective cap is refused with a DM-only code and "Players' remembered map here will be forgotten. Go ahead?", and resent with `forget: true` on yes. The reducer ignores the flag, and the forget lands in `sys.remember` as stored.
  - **Undo and Redo ask before the step is popped**: a preflight on the peeked step, as the asset check does (actor.ts:927-932). The yes names that step's sequence number, and one that no longer heads the stack is refused as stale. A player's refused redo still consumes its step.
  - **Ask:** the builder's tools (labels and stamps placed there included), Clear the build, a paste and the Universal VTT import, in the builder's confirm sheet or the import dialog, once per gesture. The DM's Undo and Redo of those ask in the undo toast's confirm.
  - **Forget silently:** every other DM op (a door from its pill, a label or stamp edited from its panel, an op that grows the room and lowers a share) and every player op. A player's op never gets a memory-dependent refusal, since it would tell them another layer's size.
- **Forgetting by tiles.** Taking entries in chunk and key order, a layer past its effective cap forgets every fog tile each element that does not fit touches: those tiles leave its explored layer, except the sub-cells in that layer's sight now, which the same `sys.remember` keeps (MD-R2-5). Its entries for elements that touch only forgotten tiles drop, and entries of elements still held through another tile stay. An element held only through forgotten tiles leaves the projection in the same batch. Undo does not bring the players' map back (MD-O-16, MD-O-30, LK-19).

### 5.4 Everything else that changes layers or memory

- **Fog.** A hide drops an entry only when its element touches no cell its layer still holds (MD-O-5), and takes the hidden cells off free-wall versions. Fog all drops the scene's memory with its layers. Reveals leave memory to the step.
- **Vision or fog off** drops the scene's memory, by an edit or an undo, as bookkeeping.
- **Per player.** Unticking a holder, member removal and a scene's deletion drop that memory with the layer. The fold to Whole party pools per element, a free wall per cell: an element touching a cell the party's layer held keeps the party's entry or none, any other the first holder's in list order.
- **A stamp set DM-only** drops its entries, so its art stops reaching players (MD-O-10).
- **A grid shape switch**, which needs a scene with no cells (HX-D14), drops the scene's memory and forgets its tiles.
- **Duplicate** with Fog copies memory, giving labels and stamps the copies' ids (`copyId`, duplicate.ts:53) and fresh ones to ids only memory names. Free wall ids are chunk-scoped and kept.

### 5.5 Sizes, migration and hexes

- **Cap** 64 KiB per layer and scene (`LIMITS.memoryBytesPerLayer`). Every memory, the party's and each holder's, is reserved at its cap on every scene with fog and vision on, never counted at its size (D-261026-29 item 9, LK-29), so no measure reads what the DM edited out of sight. It is reserved only on such a scene that is the players' scene or carries the played mark, and whether it does reads no explored layer, since on a per-player scene every layer is private (D-261026-29 item 12, ASTRA-24, MD-R5-1). The mark (`RoomState.playedScenes`) is set by an op of the new op version that first makes a scene the players' scene (`room.setPlayerScene`, or a deletion that moves the players), before any exploring and whatever its settings, and it stays while the scene exists. Older stored ops replay without it (MD-R6-1). The upgrade step (Versions, below) marks every scene that exists then, since which of them the party explored cannot be told without reading the layers (D-261026-29 item 15, ASTRA-27). Duplicate with Fog, the room export and import carry it. Explored layers are written only on the players' scene, so memory never grows on an unmarked scene. A marked per-player scene adds a cap for each player on the roster, up to 6, as `perPlayerStoredReserve` reserves their explored layers. The cost, said in the guide: 64 KiB for each such scene and 384 KiB more for a per-player one's holders, so 20 Explore scenes the players have been on reserve 1.25 MiB of the 8 MiB room (MD-R4-3). Where it does not fit, Players see, a new Explore scene, Move players and the copy sheet answer "The room is too full to keep players' maps of this scene. Delete unused scenes to free space.", and a switch to Per player says `PER_PLAYER_FULL`.
- **One room-wide share** (MD-O-14, MD-R2-6, MD-R3-5). The upgrade step marks every scene with fog and vision on then (`RoomState.memoryShared`), and the room export and import carry it, mapping its scene ids. The reserves of every marked scene count, together, at most the room's free space without them less a fixed margin of 64 KiB, split equally over their layers (the party's, and 6 holders' on a per-player scene): share = min(64 KiB, (limit − exact measure − explored reserve − whole caps − 64 KiB) / sharing layers), rounded down. So no room becomes full at deploy, and in a near-full room a small op's growth bound stays under the limit, so it neither measures the room whole nor lowers the share (D-261027-03). A share is lowered only on an exact measure, never on the guard's growth bound, and the batch that lowers it forgets, in its own `sys.remember` by whole tiles, every layer above its new share. A marked scene keeps its mark while fog and vision stay on: a fold takes it from 7 layers to 1 and keeps the party's share, and a switch back to Per player needs the holders' full reserve. On a scene whole party at the upgrade only the party's layer shares, since only it existed at deploy, so its first switch to Per player needs the holders' full reserve too (MD3-D-12). The mark goes only when fog or vision goes off (ASTRA-22).
- **Projected scene** (ASTRA-12, ASTRA-16, MD-R3-6, D-261026-29 item 14, LK-36). The check counts each free wall at the largest form a player copy can hold of it, never the most any wall can give, reading only the wall, whatever the fog: its stored contribution, a visible whole door with its real id, state, secret and discovered fields, and its clipped pieces, a window's with their longer kind (ASTRA-32). A wall crossing c cells, counted by walking it through the grid (squares, hex rows or hex columns), with s split lines strictly inside its span along its major axis, has at most (c + 1) / 2 runs of held cells. On squares a split line is a cell side, so a piece needs a cell of its own, and the wall gives at most (c + s + 1) / 2 pieces, rounded down: 8 for an 8-cell wall, which crosses at most 14 cells and 2 lines. On hexes a split can fall inside a hex, so a wall gives at most (c + 1) / 2 rounded down, plus s (ASTRA-29): astra's wall from (5.1875, 5.375) to (1.203125, 12.296875) crosses 18 hexes, has 9 held runs with alternate hexes held, which the line y = 8 or y = 12 makes 10 final pieces in both orientations, and is counted at 11 (ASTRA-33). A piece prints in at most 43 bytes of keys, brackets, id and commas, its kind (15, a window's 17) and four coordinates (decision 2): 98 bytes for a wall on a scene under 1,000 cells. A chunk position adds at most about 0.5 KB (stub header, `known`, `auto`, `upDir`, a `walls` field, `grid` on a hex scene), 85 KiB at the 196 of a 200 x 200 square scene.
- **Projected memory** (ASTRA-23, MD-R5-2, D-261026-29 item 14). Each memory entry is charged against its layer's cap at the larger of its stored bytes and the most it can add to a player's chunks, a free-wall version at its wall's own bound. So the projected reserve is the caps themselves: 64 KiB for a whole-party scene, 128 KiB for a holder's view, which reads the party's memory and their own. A memory of walls forgets sooner, which fails closed.
- **The numbers** (scratch measure on `projectedSceneBytes` plus these reserves, each wall at its largest form): the bench scene (bench-vision.ts `capRoom`, 1,814 free walls of 2 to 8 cells, 4.4 pieces each at their own bound) measures 2.05 MiB for the whole party and 2.59 MiB per player (`perPlayerRoom`, a holder's view). With free walls added to the 4,000 cap on Reveal by hand it is 2.54 MiB, 4,000 8-cell diagonals on an empty 200 x 200 scene are 2.73 MiB, and the bench room with 4,000 short closed doors in place of its walls, each from (x + 0.25, y + 0.25) to (x + 0.75, y + 0.75) and counted whole at about 130 bytes, is 1.40 MiB. All five fit 3 MiB. Without short ends the first three are 2.49, 3.02 and 3.50 MiB, and round 5's flat bounds gave 4.25 and 5.40 MiB, so both changes are needed. The size test commits this measure, repeats these and adds a real Universal VTT import.
- A scene whose new reserve would not fit at deploy keeps working: edits that do not grow it pass, a DM edit that grows it gets "This scene is too full for players' maps of it. Remove walls or stamps to make room.", and players' ops are judged as before deploy. Its player copies may then pass 3 MiB by at most the reserve, since nothing is truncated.
- **Versions.** Snapshot version +1 adds empty `memory` and `memoryBy`. Op version +1 adds `sys.remember`, `forget` and the played mark. No stored op changes meaning.
- **The upgrade step** (ASTRA-31). `loadRoom` (server/rooms/persistence.ts:72) migrates the snapshot, then replays later ops. Over a snapshot from before the upgrade, every upgrade step (played marks for every scene then present, `memoryShared`, the shares) runs once over the state recovered from everything stored before it: during replay just before the first op of the new version, or after the last op when none is new. Old ops keep their own reducer rules (MD-R6-1). The step reads only the recovered state, so a reload before the next snapshot runs it at the same point with the same result, and a snapshot written after it records the result, so a later load never runs it. A room import of a legacy file takes the same step after its migration.
- **The moment this deploys** every device takes a fresh snapshot. Never-seen build leaves every player's copy, memory starts empty, and remembered ground shows the build as at deploy, which players already saw live.
- **Hexes** (H2 and H3 integration, MD-O-15, LK-11). Hex cell data maps to the fog tiles its hexes' world box covers (as HX-D22), a chunk's free walls to world tiles, `known` indexes the chunk's hex keys, and a free wall is held by the hexes whose sub-cells it crosses.

### 5.6 Projection and fan-out

- **Chunks and stubs move to the fog classes** (fanout.ts:124), projected once per class: each holder alone, every other player and display together. `allKeys` lists every chunk position of a fog scene, one past the east and south bounds included. Memory is never projected.
- **The memo** (fanout.ts:165) keys a position by its chunk object and its 8 neighbors' chunk and memory objects, stubs included, the scene's `autoWalls`, its derived stair map, the class's fog tiles of the 3 x 3 tiles around it, and while memory exists the class's sight tiles and the DM's fog tiles there.
- **`dependents`.** Positions one past the east and south bounds count as any other. A chunk write or a wall-memory write brings all 8 neighboring positions, stubs included, in the state before and after, since a piece filed by its own midpoint can lie up to 4 cells from its source chunk (ASTRA-18). A fog, explored or `exploredBy` write brings the chunk positions of the 3 x 3 tiles around it. A changed sight tile brings those of the 3 x 3 tiles around it where any memory the class reads has entries (MD-O-24). A memory write also brings the labels and stamps it names. A changed stair map brings every chunk where it changed.

## 6. Movement and the rest

- **The touch rule** (D-261026-29). Players move into unheld ground as today, and the server tests the real build. A player's walked move, route piece, arrow step, group leg or attach walk that the real build stops, whatever the held build said (MD-R9-1), is cut on the server at `landing(..., "walk")`'s last clear spot (shared/rules/movement.ts:893) and stored as the move made. The sender's `ev` carries `blocked: [n]`, naming no kind. The stop note's blocker may be unknown, and its "Teleport here" sends `teleport: "unknown"`, which the DM's log reads "teleported past something they could not see" (LK-21). A refused redo is gone: the actor pops it before the check (actor.ts:934), so a retry needs another undo.
- **Squeeze, resize and companion placement** (D-261026-29 item 10, MD-R2-2, MD-R3-2, LK-31) refuse in place only where the footprint they read lies on live ground, in sight or DM-revealed, which the device predicts. Elsewhere they land, stored as made, by the real build: a squeeze is judged at the cut walk's landing and lands unsqueezed where the full footprint fits or is cut earlier, a resize takes the nearest of a drop's half-cell shifts where its new footprint fits, and a companion the nearest clear spot its placement finds. Only where nothing fits is the op refused, with the generic words, a touch of the cells beside the creature (risk 1).
- **The words** (MD-O-31, LK-17, LK-20, MD-R2-11). The server tests the stopping step's closed sweep, from the landing to the next spot (resize: the new footprint, squeeze: the full footprint, door reach: the reach line less its end at the door), against the sender's live ground (in sight or DM-revealed, or revealed on a fog-only scene). Any touch of a cell off it gives "Something blocks the way.", else today's words: one rule for every stop and refusal, remembered ground included (LK-34). A walk's stop, and every refusal worded as one, names instead the blocker the stopping step meets where the part of it the core meets is live to the sender (D-261027-04): a wall, door or window beside a live cell or through one, a ledge only between two live cells, since a raised edge beside an unseen cell tells that cell's height. The landing already shows where the stop is, and the player sees that blocker, so its name tells nothing new. The device's live ground is all ground with fog off, its revealed fog on a scene with fog on and vision off, and its sight alone with both on, since there its fog merges DM reveals into explored ground (`sharedFogTile`, project.ts:1308) (ASTRA-28). With both on, on DM-revealed ground, it can predict the generic words, a landing where the server refuses in place, or a shorter caught list or fewer rings, and the server's answer corrects it (LK-35). Nothing leaks: the server's live ground holds the device's, and DM-revealed ground is live in the player's data. Routes, attach walks, redo, companion placement, squeeze and door reach take the same rule.
- **What the device sees.** Its preview walks the held build, so a drag into the dark goes to the finger. The server's landing replaces it within the batch.
- **Doors** (MD-O-6). The pill shows only held doors. A `door.set` is judged first by the shown door's geometry: memory's where the sender remembers one. While reach fails the answer is a real door's. Once reach passes, a stored door that is gone, moved, or a secret door the DM hid again refuses GONE with the touch recorded (5.3, MD2-O-2), with no dereference of a missing wall (`doorPlace`, permissions.ts:1993). A remembered door whose real state is already the one asked is a no-op, its touch recorded, so the sender then sees it as it is.
- **Routes** search seen ground (walk.ts:57) over the held build. Corners follow decision 12.
- **Rulers.** A walking ruler reads its device's held build. On a per-player scene a holder's device can walk a shared ruler differently where only their sight shows a change. Nothing of a walk is sent.
- **Areas** (decision 13, ASTRA-15). The server judges each receiving player's reminders by the receiver rule, witnesses and the DM's areas included, and a reminder only some players get settles as a party line (per-player 6.5). The device applies the same rule, on its own live ground (6, the words), to its own caught list (coverage.ts:63), AreaOptions' list (AreaOptions.tsx:129) and the preview's target rings (AreaPreviewLayer.tsx:109), whose caches key on the sight and fog tiles they read. A device still clips coverage by the walls it holds.
- **The server reads the real build** for sight, explore, area sweeps, traps and every walls test.

## 11. Security rules

1. On a scene with fog on, no player's projection or socket holds any piece of geometry on a cell their sight or a DM reveal never reached, except visible free doors, whole by decision 2, labels and stamps, whole by their box as today (a stamp up to 20 cells, MD-R2-8), and a rounded end's sliver within 2/10000 cell of a held cell. No id or chunk position of a piece says anything of a wall beyond held cells, and no player's copy of such a scene holds a free wall's real id or chunk but a visible door's.
2. A held element is live only where it touches the viewer's sight or DM-revealed ground. Elsewhere it is the viewer's memory by decision 6.
3. `memory`, `memoryBy` and `sys.remember` reach no player's projection or socket. The DM gets them only in the room export.
4. A player's chunk carries derived facts only for held cells and edges: automatic walls on held edges and directions of held stairs, which may read unheld cells. Their scene copy never carries the real automatic walls rule on a scene with fog on.
5. The map step writes a holder's memory only from that holder's sight, and the party's only from the shared sight.
6. A stopped player walk lands at its last clear spot, stored. A refusal or stop names a kind only for the part of the blocker it met that is live to the sender (D-261027-04), in words and the teleport op alike, and `blocked` names none. No preview, refusal or reminder says anything about unheld ground the creature did not reach or touch.
7. An area reaches a player, as reminder, caught line or target ring, only when every ray its inclusion reads lies on that player's live ground. Corners of a private leg reach a non-moving owner only on their sight or DM-revealed ground.
8. No measure reads a memory or an explored layer: each memory counts at its cap or its share, and only the players' scene and the played mark say where it is reserved.
9. Fail closed. Memory that cannot be kept forgets whole tiles. A device treats a missing chunk, `known` or fog tile as unknown.
10. Sight, explore, areas, traps and the walls test read the real build. With fog off nothing is withheld, as before.

## 12. Risks and accepted channels

1. **Touch.** What stops a creature on unheld ground, or on remembered ground changed since, is something it touched: the landing says where, and the height it lands at says how high the ground is there, since the server sets that height from the real build for a walk, a Get off and a placed token (MD3-D-14, MD3-LK-5). Probing costs real moves, logged, and each undo and retry is another real touch. Door reach on remembered ground can be stopped the same way, and a resize or companion placement where nothing fits answers for the cells beside the creature. A route can rarely bend around a wall added out of sight that touches no cell in sight (LK-13).
2. **Forgetting and size.** Past the cap, or with a shrunk share in a full room, whole tiles go back under fog, which says something changed there. Undo does not bring the players' map back. A size refusal near the room or scene limit, to any op a player sends, says how large the content is, hidden build included, never where it is (D-261026-29 item 9, ASTRA-20). Accepted: it is a byte count at the limit, the room-full refusal every op already risks, and separate player capacity in the size guard would be a large change for it.
3. **The map image is whole** (PV-IMAGE). **Visible free doors are whole**, at most 8 cells past held ground. **Labels and stamps go whole by their box**, as today, a stamp up to 20 cells. **A cut end shows the wall runs on**: rounding hides which ends were real, but an end off the 1/64 lattice shows it was moved, so a player can infer that the wall goes on past the held side, as a lit wall face shows at a table (LK-27).
4. **Per player.** `ownExplored` never adds party-held sub-cells to a holder's layer, so on party ground a holder's map returns to the party's memory once their own creature looks away. A shared walking ruler can walk differently on their device there.
5. **Own acts are public.** A party member, always shown, seen walking through a door others remember shut shows it open (beside per-player risks 4 and 6). Light reads real doors, so a torch through a door opened out of sight still lights what the party sees. Areas follow the receiver rule, the DM's included, so what a DM's area catches reaches a player only on their live ground.
6. **A door a player touches** refreshes for everyone who shares that memory.
7. **Op counts** (MD3-LK-3). Every `ev` carries the room's op sequence, so a player sees how many ops went by between two of theirs, hidden ones included. A DM edit out of sight on ground some layer remembers adds the map step's `sys.remember` to its batch, and one on ground nobody saw adds none, so in a quiet moment the count can tell which. Accepted: the count already shows every hidden DM op, the bit says only that the edit lay somewhere players have been, never where or what, and hiding it would take a stored, replayed step on every DM batch on a scene that remembers.

## 15. As built

The builders fill this in, lane by lane, with every place the build went its own way.

**MD1, contract and reducer.**
- `memory`, `memoryBy`, `playedScenes` and `memoryShared` are optional on `RoomState`, since a projection never holds them, and every reader takes absent as empty. Every stored state holds them: the snapshot step to version 27 adds them empty, and the stored-state schema defaults them to empty for a state that names none. Each stored chunk lies under the key the reducer files it by, `chunkKey` for the party's with no member and `holderChunkKey` for a holder's naming theirs, and each mark under its scene's id.
- A mark is `{ sceneId, rev }`, by scene id. `memoryShared` holds only marks: the upgrade step writes no share, and the size guard works each share out from the marks and the room's exact measure (5.5).
- `sys.remember` is `{ sceneId, layers: [{ memberId?, chunks: [{ cx, cy, drop?, set? }] }], forget?: [{ memberId?, tiles: [{ tx, ty, keep? }] }] }`, the party's layer without a member. Each chunk drops its keys, then sets its entries. A layer's entries whose element touches no cell the layer holds are then dropped, so memory never outlives the ground it is on. A forgotten tile keeps the sub-cells of `keep` it held, and goes when none are left. The reducer refuses a scene without fog and party vision on, a holder's layer for a member who does not see for themselves, a cell or edge outside its chunk, a free wall's version under another id or chunk or covering a cell twice, a label or stamp under another id or scene or in two chunks of one layer, and a stamp entry for a stamp the DM keeps to themselves (an absent-then entry is allowed). A remembered free wall has two different ends at most 8 cells apart, as every free wall. Memory chunk positions run to 14, the widest hex span of the largest scene (`sceneHexSpan`), for the op and the stored state alike, and a forgotten tile's to 12, since fog is a square raster on every grid.
- Every layer that loses explored ground in an op, by any path (a hide, a forgotten tile, a per-player scene shrinking or turning per player, by an edit or a restore), is pruned the same way in one pass after the op (`pruneLostGround`), keeping an element still held through another cell. So is every layer that remembers a label or stamp the op moved or took away (`pruneMovedMarks`), since an absent-then entry is judged by the live box alone.
- What a layer holds, for pruning and the fold: the party's by its explored layer, a holder's by their own layer alone. A cell-keyed element goes by its cell, an edge by either cell beside it, a label or stamp by the square cells of both its boxes, the one remembered and the one it has now, and on hexes a cell by the sub-cells inside it.
- The fold reads "the first holder's in list order" as the first holder on the list whose own layer holds the element, with their entry or none, so each element reads as one layer saw it. A label or stamp lies in every box any layer remembers it in and its live one, worked out before the layer is chosen, so on ground the party held the party's absence of one deleted since wins over a holder's entry. It pools by its id, into the chunk of the layer it reads from.
- Counts in a chunk of memory are bounded by bytes, not by a wall's geometry: the map step keeps each layer under its cap and the fold pools at most seven layers, so walls, labels, stamps, a wall's versions and a version's cells are each bounded by seven caps over the fewest bytes such an entry takes in JSON. So a fold of layers under their caps always fits the stored schema. This rests on MD3 charging each entry at least its JSON bytes.
- The shape switch drops the scene's memory and forgets its explored ground on every scene, whatever its fog and vision, and only for ops of version 26 on, so older ops replay as they did. Vision or fog off, a deletion, Fog all, a hide, an unticked or removed holder and a DM-only stamp change only memory and marks, which older ops never met.
- The played mark is written after every op of version 26 on that changes the players' scene, a restore's included. A new room's first mat is marked at creation. Duplicate with Fog carries the played mark but not the share mark, so a copy reserves its memory whole. A deleted scene's undo brings it back without its played mark, its explored layers gone too, and the next move of the players there marks it.
- The ask: a builder op carries `forget: true`, an Undo or Redo `forget: <seq>`, and the refusal code is `forget` with the words `FORGET_ASK` (shared/protocol.ts). `blocked` rides the `ev` as a list of the client's op numbers.
- The upgrade step is `mapDiscoveryUpgrade` in shared/migrate.ts, with `needsMapDiscoveryUpgrade`. `loadRoom` runs it at the version boundary of replay. A room import ran it on every remapped state, whatever the file's version, since the remap carried no marks and the room is stored at version 27, which loadRoom never upgrades. Since MD3 carries a version 27 file's own marks and memory through the remap, the import runs the step only for a file before version 27 (`withDiscoveryUpgrade`).
- The DM-only stamp rule sits in map.ts's bookkeeping pass beside vision off, not in room.ts. Projections answer null for memory, holders' memory and both marks by name (shared/project.ts). The player chunk fields are types and lengths only (`PlayerChunkFields`, `KNOWN_CHARS`, `AUTO_CHARS`, `UP_DIR_CHARS`): the stored chunk schema was already strict. `LIMITS.memoryBytesPerLayer` is defined for MD3.

**MD2, projection and rules.**
- `playerChunk(state, at, view)` builds one position for a fog class, and `heldChunks(state, sceneId, view)` every position of a scene, cutting its free walls once. A position goes only when it holds a held cell, an `auto` bit, a drawn edge feature or a piece: a held edge with nothing on it makes no position, so the east wall of a held room still reaches players in a stub and an empty border does not. A drawn `none` is never sent. `known` is always there, `auto` only when a bit is set, `upDir` only with a held stair, and the fields go in the order `sceneId`, `cx`, `cy`, `rev` (0), `cells`, `edges`, `walls`, `heights`, `up`, `rough`, `grid`, `known`, `auto`, `upDir`. Hex scenes have no automatic walls or stair directions yet, so their positions carry neither.
- Live ground lies inside held ground (rules/held.ts): sight the explore step has not yet added to an explored layer holds nothing, so no projection shows a cell no layer holds. A cell is live when any sub-cell is. A class's sight counts only on the players' scene, with vision on and no blackout. With fog on and vision off all held ground is live, and a fog-only scene keeps no memory.
- Free walls join geometrically: pieces on one line with one public kind join across cells and across the stored walls that drew them, then split at the lines of 4 cells and round. Every end on a cell's side or corner moves, whether a cut or a stored end (round 1, D-261026-29 item 16), a stored end strictly inside a cell stays exact, a split inside a hex moves, a coordinate that would step off the scene is clamped to its side, and cells off the scene take no other part. A visible door goes whole under its real id and chunk: live where any held cell it crosses is live, else its first remembered version. A piece's id hashes the text `<sceneId>|<a0>,<a1>,<b0>,<b1>|<kind>`, then `#1`, `#2` on a collision.
- Labels and stamps go by today's rule where no layer remembers them. A remembered entry shows where its box is held and out of sight, and none where its box is in sight. A holder reads their own entry where their own layer holds any of the boxes (the live one or either layer's), else the party's.
- The memo lives in shared/project.ts, not in server/rooms/fanout.ts, so `projectState` and `projectEntity` share it: per position and class, keyed by the scene fields a position reads (id, bounds, automatic walls rule, grid shape, fog and the Sight switch), the objects of the 3 x 3 chunks and both memories there, the class's fog, explored and own tiles over the position's tiles, the class's sight where memory is near, and the stair map where the position has stairs. Not the scene object, which every build stroke and door toggle replaces to bump its walls version (MD2-O-4): measured on bench-vision.ts `capRoom` with the scene under fog, one class, medians of 11, a painted cell or a door toggle reprojects its 169 positions in 9 ms with 80 x 80 cells explored (24 ms before) and in 13 ms with all of it explored (294 ms before), the party's sight worked out first as the batch does. The 9 neighbors of the stroke miss, 0.6 ms each, and the rest hit. A scene write still brings every position in `dependents`, which reads one state at a time and so cannot tell a size change from a walls version bump. It keeps two answers per position, in slots hung off the scene's bounds object, so they go with the room (MD2-O-5). A position with no held cell within reach of it (one cell for a whole scene, whose pieces are cut first, four for one position, a piece's longest reach from its midpoint) is skipped before its key is made, and a position's live edges are worked out once for every class.
- The performance pass (after round 2, on scripts/bench-server4.ts's rows over these files, per player with 6 holders, medians on the development laptop): a player's move went from 88 to 13 ms bright and 94 to 16 ms dark, a move exploring a tile from 780 to 8.2, one with memory full from 152 to 18, a group move from 592 to 84, a build edit in sight from 171 to 59 and a stair edit from 434 to 89.
  - A slot's answer is also found by `base`, every input but the fog layers' tiles and the class's sight, with `region`, the cells of the position's tiles each layer holds, and live and own where memory is near (`Ground.cellsKey`, one bit a cell, squares only). An explore rewrites tile objects whose cells stay as they were held, which missed every key before.
