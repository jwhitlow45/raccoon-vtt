Decisions D-261102-460 to D-261102-479 (section 15), with the coordinator's D-261102-513 (the server owner's switch) and the builders' D-261102-700 to D-261102-704 ("As built"). The "As built" section says where the build went its own way, and the numbers in sections 6.7 and 13 are the design's, so read them with it. Code was cited from main at `796ce059` (OP_VERSION 41, SNAPSHOT_VERSION 31). It builds after animated maps (design-v1-animated-maps.md), whose Range answers, `media` column, quota headroom, per-session intake slot, upload progress path, `media-src 'self' blob:` and backup patterns it reuses.

## 1. Summary

- **Three kinds of sound, run by the DM** (D-261102-461): **Music** (a playlist or one track, with repeat, shuffle and crossfade), up to 4 **Background sounds** looping at once, each at its own volume, and **Effects** fired with a tap, plus 5 built-in effects (the trap sounds the app already synthesizes). One new panel, **Sound**, in play. A scene can keep a sound of its own, silence included, that plays when the DM moves the players there.
- **Off unless the server owner turns it on** (D-261102-513). `ROOM_SOUND=1` turns on sound files. While it is off the server refuses sound uploads, serves no sound bytes and refuses ops that name a sound file, and the Sound panel says the server does not offer sound files. The built-in effects still work, since each device makes them and nothing is downloaded.
- **The DM's own files** (D-261102-460, D-261102-463). The DM's browser reads each file within a memory budget, evens its loudness, and makes the room's copy: a constant bitrate MP3 of 64 to 192 kbps, its frames copied when the file already is one, else encoded in a Worker. The server walks the MP3's frames and never decodes. Music 30 seconds to 15 minutes, Background 2 seconds and up, Effects up to 30 seconds, 24 MiB a file, in the room's storage quota. No music ships in this phase. A starter pack is S2 (section 14).
- **Synced** (D-261102-468). Every device that plays it starts within a second or two of the others: the room stores each sound's start in server time, each device measures the server's time and starts each voice at its place, then lets it play (D-261102-940 replaced the speed changes and seeks that kept devices in step). A device that joins mid-track starts at the right place.
- **Each device decides** (D-261102-469, D-261102-470). A player's device asks once, "Play here" or "Not on this device", the first time there is something to hear, and the tap that answers lets the browser start audio. DM devices and table displays start on. Volume for Music, Background and Effects and a switch live in This device and the Sound panel. Pages of one browser hand off so that only one plays.
- **Privacy** (D-261102-467). A player's device receives only what plays now, the next track 10 to 20 seconds before it starts, and the effects fired, and names only of the music and loops sounding now, while the DM turns on "Players see what is playing". Never the library, the playlists, the order, a scene's sound, an effect's name or the effects board. Silent behind a blackout.
- **The server moves playlists on** (D-261102-466): at most two stored ops a track, from a tick queued like any command, while anyone is connected. A room left without members stops its sound after 15 minutes.
- **Versions:** the next free op version and the next free snapshot version (6.7).

## 2. Today

- `client/features/encounter/alerts.ts` synthesizes the turn chime and the five trap sounds with Web Audio under `rvtt.sound`. `client/features/pings/feedback.ts` has its own AudioContext for the ping under `rvtt.pingSound`. This design reuses it (5.5). No audio file ships or is stored.
- Assets are the image kinds map, token, handout and thumb (server/assets/kinds.ts), re-encoded by sharp and served whole. Animated maps adds the `video` kind and the media machinery named above.
- A room's quota (`ROOM_QUOTA_MB`, 1024 by default) counts every asset row but starter picks. At its quota a room is read-only but for the ops in `DELETIONS` (server/rooms/actor.ts:268-281, gate at :1263).
- Every op applies with the server's time (`Env.at`). The store's `serverOffset` (client/state/store.ts `withServerReading`) is a lower bound off by one network delay, 20 to 150 ms on a phone (D-261031-181).
- The actor queues server commands through `sys()`, fills fields from asset rows before an op applies (`withAssetSizes`, `loadAssetFacts`), and a room unloads 60 seconds after its last socket closes. Board controllers (features/layers.ts) live inside `MapStage`, which the room and the display leave out with no scene, behind a blackout and while loading.

## 3. Rules and other VTTs

No SRD 5.2.1 rule touches music or sound. Sound sets a mood and carries no game fact the DM does not also say or write.

Ideas only: **Foundry VTT** plays playlists in sequence, shuffled, all at once or as a soundboard, each sound with repeat and fade, ambient sounds placed on the map, each player's own volume sliders, and clients seek from a shared start. **Roll20's jukebox** plays playlists in order, shuffled, together or looped, with a master volume for each player. **Syrinscape** layers music and loops with random one-shots, a one-shot board, and a player app on each device. **Owlbear Rodeo** has audio only through extensions. Taken: playlists with repeat, shuffle and fades, loops beside the music, a one-tap board, each device's volume, a shared start. Left out for now: sounds on the map, random one-shots, every playlist at once, outside services.

### 4.2 Adding sounds

Each Add button opens a file picker (`accept="audio/*"`, up to 20 files). The files queue under the button, one row each, with the shared loading indicator, the upload percent from animated maps' progress path (client/lib/api.ts) and one Cancel. One file is read at a time:

1. **Before decoding** (D-261102-464): a file over 200 MB is refused. Its length comes from its header where the app reads one (MP3 frames, a WAV header), else from a muted `<audio>` element's metadata on a `blob:` URL, and a length past the use's cap is refused unread. Its channel count and sample rate come from its header: an MP3's frames, WAV's `fmt `, AIFF's `COMM`, FLAC's STREAMINFO, Ogg's Vorbis or Opus identification header, an M4A's `stsd` sample entry (and for AAC the larger of that and its `esds` channel configuration), a raw AAC file's ADTS `channel_configuration`, or a WebM's Tracks `Channels`. A file whose channels the app cannot read this way, or with a count other than 1, 2, 4 or 6, is refused before any decoding, since the browser's decoder allocates by the file's own channels. A rate it cannot read counts as 96 kHz. A file that decodes whole (step 2) must fit this device's budget, one bound for all of it (D-261102-464): the file's own bytes, plus its length times 4 bytes times its channels at its own rate (the decoder's output before resampling), plus the same at 44.1 kHz (the resampled copy), plus 2 channels at 44.1 kHz for the stereo mix when it has more than 2. The budget is 800 MB on a computer and 150 MB on a phone or tablet (a touch device without a fine pointer): about 3 minutes of a stereo 44.1 kHz FLAC there, 16 minutes on a computer. The DM guide says so: on a phone, a file other than a constant bitrate MP3 of 64 to 192 kbps or a plain WAV (step 2) is best added from a computer when it runs longer than about 3 minutes.
2. "Reading Rain.wav…": a constant bitrate MP3 of 64 to 192 kbps is measured by decoding 60-second pieces split on frame boundaries, each with the 10 frames before it as pre-roll whose output is dropped, since a frame may draw on earlier frames' bits. Pre-roll and the decoder's delay are counted at the file's own rate and converted to the 44.1 kHz output (10 × 1152 input samples are 10,584 output samples for a 48 kHz MP3). This is loudness only, so a few samples off at an edge do not matter, and a piece that fails to decode falls back to decoding the file whole within the budget. A WAV of 8, 16, 24 or 32-bit integers or 32 or 64-bit floats (plain or `WAVE_FORMAT_EXTENSIBLE`) at 44.1 or 48 kHz is read straight from its PCM in pieces. Neither is held decoded whole. Anything else, other WAVs included, decodes whole in an `OfflineAudioContext` at 44.1 kHz, under the budget. That a browser can read it is the type check.
3. **Channels**: mono and stereo stay as they are. 4 and 6 channels are mixed to stereo by the Web Audio specification's speaker rules before measuring, piece by piece for a WAV. Other counts are refused.
4. **Loudness**: ITU-R BS.1770 integrated loudness, gated, and the peak. The level brings it to -18 LUFS, between -24 and +12 dB, never so high that the peak passes -1 dBFS, kept in tenths of a dB (D-261102-465).
5. "Converting Rain.wav… 40%": an MP3 of 64 to 192 kbps has its tags stripped (7.1) and its frames copied. Anything else, a faster MP3 included, goes to a Worker in 10-second pieces, transferred and not copied, and `@breezystack/lamejs` 1.2.7 encodes it at 128 kbps stereo or 64 kbps mono, at 48 kHz for a 48 kHz WAV and 44.1 kHz otherwise. The decoded audio is released.
   - **Lengths** (D-261102-464): the use caps of 4.1 apply exactly to the source's own length, checked here before encoding, except that an MP3 to be copied, whose own length is already frames and padding, takes the same 100 ms allowance. The stored `ms` is the encoded length, frames times 1152 over the rate, which frame rounding and the encoder's delay and padding make up to about 100 ms longer. So the reducer and the import allow 100 ms over each upper cap, and nothing under a lower one, which encoding never shortens. Playback schedules by `ms`, so every device keeps the same padding.
6. "Uploading Rain.wav… 60%" to `PUT /api/rooms/:id/assets?kind=sound`, then `sound.create` with the name (the file name less its extension, `_` as a space, at most `MAX_NAME`, under `NAME_CHARS`), the section's use and the level. "Added Rain." goes after 3 seconds.

Refusals, each on its row, the others going on: "Rain.ogg: this browser can't read it. Try Chrome or Firefox, or make it an MP3 first.", "Battle.mp3: longer than 15 minutes.", "Thunder.wav: an effect is 30 seconds at most. Add it as background or music.", "Fanfare.mp3: music is 30 seconds at least. Add it as an effect.", "Drip.wav: a background sound is 2 seconds at least.", "Click.wav: shorter than a fifth of a second.", "Score.flac is too big for this device to read. Add it from a computer.", "Rain.wav: this device ran out of memory reading it. Add it from a computer.", "Mix.wav has 8 channels. Use a mono or stereo file.", "Rain.caf: this app can't tell how many channels it has. Make it an MP3 or WAV first.", "Big.wav is over 200 MB." and "The room is out of storage space. Delete sounds or images it no longer uses." (which stops the batch), plus the upload errors features/images/errors.ts already words. A batch lives in memory. A file uploaded but never named is deleted a day later (7.3).

### 4.4 This device, the ask, and one page per browser

This device's **Sounds** section: **Room sound**, a switch, "Music, background sounds and effects the DM plays. If everyone sits at one table, turn it on for one device only, such as the table display. Music uses about 60 MB of data an hour. On an iPhone it pauses music from other apps and plays even with the ringer off.", then while on **Music**, **Background** and **Effects** (0 to 100%, 80% at first, squared into the gain), then **Turn sound** and **Ping sound** as today. Kept as `rvtt.roomSound` ("on", "off", absent until a choice) and `rvtt.soundMix` through `setting()` in features/device/settings.ts, with a `storage` listener. Absent reads as on for a DM device and asks on a player's. A display keeps its own (4.5).

**The ask** (D-261102-469). On a player's device with no choice, the first confirmed state with something to hear raises a toast, "The DM is playing sound for the room.", with **Play here** and **Not on this device**: at load when something already plays (a late joiner), when music or a loop starts, or when an effect is fired after the page loaded. Passed unanswered, the device stays quiet and does not ask again in that page load: Room sound in the Sound panel and This device turns it on. **Play here**'s own click handler sets Room sound on and calls `context().resume()` and `blessPool()` itself, in that click, since the window's capture listeners ran before the setting changed (5.5). It plays what sounds now. An effect already over is never played late.

**One page plays per browser** (D-261102-469). Pages of one room in one browser post on a `BroadcastChannel` named for the room every 2 seconds: a page id, display or member, eligible (enabled: Room sound on, or a display's Sound on and not behind a blackout, and unlocked), playing, released, and when last shown. An entry lapses 5 seconds after its last post. **The owner keeps playing** while it stays eligible, through tab switches. A new owner is chosen only when there is none, the owner turns ineligible, its entry lapses, or a display becomes eligible beside a member owner. Then the winner among live eligible pages is a display first, then the page shown last, then the lowest page id. **Ownership is a Web Lock** where the browser has them (`navigator.locks`, which join requests uses for its asks): a page plays only while it holds `rvtt-sound:<room id>`. The winner requests it and drops the request if it stops being the winner. The owner releases it when it posts released, on `pagehide` too. The browser grants it to one page at a time and frees it when a page closes or crashes, so an owner whose main thread stalls while its audio renders on keeps it, and nobody else plays. A page the browser suspends in the background keeps it too, so another tab of the room stays quiet until that page runs again or is discarded. **The audio lease**, for a browser without Web Locks, is a gain node of its own (`leaseGain`, after the master), armed only while another live page of the room posts on the channel, so a page alone, such as an Android phone with its screen off and its timers slowed, never fades itself (checked in live play). While armed, the owner schedules that gain to fade to silence on the audio clock 6 seconds ahead and renews it with each post, which says whether it is armed. Web Audio runs that fade on its rendering thread, so a page whose main thread stalls goes silent by itself. **A suspended context.** The audio clock stops while a context is suspended or interrupted, so a deadline there proves nothing, and rendering may resume before the page hears of it. So the moment the page sees its context leave `running` (its `statechange`, or a once-a-second drift check over which the audio clock advanced 0.5 s less than the page's own clock), it cancels the lease's schedule, holds `leaseGain` at 0, posts released and gives up ownership, its lock included. It stays at 0 through recovery until a fresh election makes it the owner again. A lone page does the same and, as the only candidate, wins that election once it runs again. A page that thinks it owns gives way at once to any other page it sees posting that it plays. **The handoff.** The winner posts that it wants to play. The owner fades out over 0.3 s, holds `leaseGain` at 0, posts released and gives up ownership, and only then does the winner start, with Web Locks once the lock is granted. Without them a winner takes over by time only from an owner whose last post was armed, 7 seconds after it, when the lease has silenced that owner. From an unarmed owner it waits for the released post and keeps that owner's claim after its entry lapses, so an unarmed owner that crashed leaves the others quiet until they reload. So with Web Locks no two pages of a room play at once in one browser. Without them the one exception is a page that loads while another's main thread is stalled: it hears no posts and may play beside that page until it runs again and gives way. A locked or frozen page never takes the sound from a working one. A quiet page's Sound panel says "Another window on this device plays the sound." So "Open on this device" plays the display window alone, and the DM's page plays during a blackout. Where that laptop's sound goes to the TV, by HDMI for one, players may hear it behind the blackout, which the DM guide says. A laptop on its own speakers is heard only at the DM's seat.

**Media controls.** The Media Session title is "RaccoonVTT · <room name>", never a track. Its pause turns Room sound off on this device and its play turns it on (FEATURES.md says so).

### 4.6 Scenes, blackout and the X-card

## 5. Sync and the browser

### 5.1 The server's time

A client frame `{ t: "time", k }` and its answer `{ t: "time", k, at }`, `at` the server's `Date.now()`, the clock `Env.at` reads. server/rooms/ws.ts answers it as it answers `ping`, from displays and previews too. A device sends none while the room plays nothing, so a rolled-back server never meets one. Each measurement epoch starts when sound starts here, on a wake (`visibilitychange`, `pageshow`) and on a reconnect, and drops every earlier sample and any answer whose `k` belongs to an earlier epoch. It sends five probes 250 ms apart, then one every 25 seconds while sound plays, keeps the shortest round trip of the epoch's last 8, drops any over a second, and reads the offset as `at - (sent + rtt / 2)` against `performance.now()`. Before the epoch's first answer it uses the last epoch's reading while the store's `serverOffset` agrees with it within a second, else the store's (D-261102-871, "As built"). Not the `pong`: older pages' strict schema would drop a pong with a time and reconnect.

### 5.2 One timeline (shared/sound.ts `occurrencesAt`)

A sound placed at `at`, of length L, that repeats with a crossing c (a loop: 2 s, or L/4 under 8 s, Repeat one: the run's crossfade, which may be 0) has the period P = L - c. Occurrence k begins at `at + kP`, plays the file from 0 to L, fades in over its first c seconds and out over its last c. At time t it is k = floor((t - at) / P) at position (t - at) - kP, and while that position is under c, occurrence k - 1 also sounds at position P plus it. There is no occurrence before `at`: k is never negative, and occurrence k - 1 joins only when k > 0. A sound that does not repeat (a playlist track, an effect) has one occurrence. A late join, a pause, a resume and every change read this one helper. The first occurrence has an envelope of its own: a loop fades in over 2 s from `at`, a music track or an effect starts at full volume, a join or resume fades in over 0.3 s, and a stop fades out over 2 s.

### 5.3 Voices

- Music and loops play through `<audio>` elements from a pool of 11 (2 for music, 8 for loops crossing their loop points, 1 for Listen here), each through a `MediaElementAudioSourceNode` to its gain, the channel's gain, a master gain 3 dB down, a compressor (`DynamicsCompressorNode`, threshold -3 dB, ratio 20, attack 3 ms, release 250 ms) and the lease gain (4.4). That keeps the loudest mix the caps allow under 0 dBFS in a browser test's offline render, though a compressor with an attack is no brick wall, so a sharp transient may still clip briefly. iOS ignores an element's own volume. Music streams by Range. A loop of 5 MB or less (about 5 minutes) plays from a `blob:` URL of its whole file, fetched once, since Safari's media cache may not keep range answers and each pass would download again. A bigger loop streams by Range. The live play review checks blob audio on an iPhone.
- Each element is aimed at its position plus the reported `outputLatency` and `baseLatency` when its voice starts: a join, a resume, a track or a loop's pass. From then on it plays at speed 1 and nothing seeks it or changes its rate (D-261102-940, amending D-261102-468's rates and seeks). On a weak connection each seek waited on the network again, and a phone's music stuttered. Devices start within a second or two of each other, and each new track or pass starts on the timeline again. One check stays: the first check that finds a placed element playing seeks it once if it is more than 2 s short of where it was put, a seek the browser dropped before the file loaded (settle.ts). A pass the timeline ended while its element still plays, having fallen behind, fades out over 0.3 s. Each check also maps server time to the context's clock afresh (the clock offset and `performance.now()` against `currentTime`), which effect starts use, so a context clock that stood still never shifts a start.
- **The next track.** When `next` arrives its element loads at position 0, paused. A timer at `next.at` less the output latency calls `play()`. A late start plays on late. The crossfade runs from `next.at` over the run's fade.
- **Gain** is the device's slider squared, times the room's volume squared, times `10^(level / 200)`, times the timeline's envelope, ramped over 0.3 s on any change.

### 5.4 Effects

Effects decode into `AudioBuffer`s and start on the context's clock at `at` less the output latency. Decoded effects, playing ones included, take at most 48 MB, with one decode in flight. A new one first evicts the least recently played cached ones, then stops the oldest playing one if it still does not fit, and six play at once at most. An entry starts on a page once at most: in the page's first projection after it loads the room, if it still has 3 seconds or more to sound, or later only if it was fired after that load and its id was never withdrawn from this page's list (the engine keeps a set of withdrawn ids, filled only when a confirmed projection drops an entry the page held, never by a reconnect's cleared store). So an entry that comes back after Undo, Redo or a change of use, or one fired before the load that a player sees only later, never starts there. Ready late by up to a second, it plays whole, late. Later, it starts partway in with 3 seconds or more left, else skips. The live play review measures how late a first fire is on phones.

**Cancelling** (D-261102-477). Each load and each voice belongs to its list entry's id. It is cancelled when that entry leaves the projected list, which happens only when the effect is stopped, deleted or given another use, or when the list passes its cap of 16 (6.3). An entry kept while it sounds never leaves just because newer effects were fired, so a long effect is not cut and another effect still loading is untouched. The engine-wide generation is kept for Stop all, Room sound off, a blackout and disposal, each of which cancels every load and voice, so a fetch or decode that finishes late never starts a sound. Built-ins are synthesized at the same moment from the shapes in engine/builtins.ts. Every start is 400 ms after the op's server time (`LEAD_MS`).

### 5.5 Starting audio, interruptions, the background, Bluetooth and TVs

- **Activation** reuses client/lib/activation.ts from lane audiounlock, whose `countsAsActivation` takes `pointerup` from touch or pen, `touchend`, `click`, `keydown` other than Escape and `pointerdown` from a mouse. The engine's context (features/sound/engine/context.ts, in the main bundle, not the lazy engine chunk) is armed by `armAudioUnlock({ context, wanted: soundWanted })`, where `soundWanted` reads Room sound on a member's page and the display's own Sound setting on a display, which creates or resumes it inside the gesture and listens again whenever it leaves `running`. The element pool is armed by `onActivation(blessPool)`: while Room sound is off the attempt answers false and touches nothing, since an element's play takes an iPhone's audio session from other apps. Otherwise it plays `/sounds/silence.mp3` (a tenth of a second from a committed script) on each pooled element synchronously and answers a promise of true once every `play()` resolved, so a rejection keeps it listening. A call while a blessing is in flight returns that blessing's promise and plays nothing again. The engine calls the pool's `rearm()` when the context or the audio session leaves `running` or `active`. Both listen for the same events, so one gesture does both. Play here calls the same two attempts from its click (4.4). The chime and ping keep their own contexts on the same helper.
- **Interruptions and the background** (D-261102-473). Sound goes on while the page is hidden wherever the browser lets it: Android Chrome does, and an iPhone may with the audio session at "playback" (set while room sound plays here, "auto" after). Whatever the browser or a call stops, the engine notices from the context's and the audio session's `statechange`, `visibilitychange` and `pageshow`, marks its voices stopped, and when it can play again resumes (or waits for the next activation) and seeks each voice to the timeline. The wake lock is unchanged.
- **Bluetooth** adds 100 to 300 ms, covered by `outputLatency` (Baseline 2025) where the system reports it.
- **TVs.** At its first unlock a display checks for Web Audio, `createMediaElementSource` and three elements playing together. Without them it shows 4.5's line and plays nothing. Desktop browsers and Chrome-based TV browsers have them. LG documents webOS setups that lack them. The live play review checks a TV at the table.

### 5.6 What "synced" promises

Every device that plays is at the same moment, within about a tenth of a second on computers and Android phones and a fifth on iPhones, before any Bluetooth delay the device does not report. A device that joins, wakes or reconnects is in place within about 6 seconds of its first sound on a good connection. That suits players apart and a table playing from one speaker. Several phones at one table playing aloud sound doubled, which the help says. These are targets the e2e sync steps and the live play review measure.

## 6. The model

### 6.1 State (shared/sound.ts, new, and shared/model.ts)

```ts
export type SoundUse = "music" | "loop" | "effect"
/** DM only. `level` in tenths of a dB, -240 to 120. */
export type Sound = { id: SoundId, rev: number, name: string, assetId: string, ms: number, use: SoundUse, level?: number }
/** DM only. Music sounds, at most 100, no id twice. */
export type Playlist = { id: PlaylistId, rev: number, name: string, sounds: SoundId[], shuffle?: true, repeat: "all" | "one" | "off", fade: 0 | 2 | 5 | 10 }
/** `at`: the server time, in ms since 1970, of the sound's own 0 (its first occurrence). */
export type SoundAt = { soundId: SoundId, at: number }
export type MusicRun = {
  run: string                  // new at each play, skip, resume and scene start
  playlistId?: PlaylistId      // DM only, a label: it may name a deleted playlist
  order: SoundId[]             // DM only: this run's order, fixed at its start
  index: number                // DM only: the place of `now` in `order`
  repeat: "all" | "one" | "off", fade: 0 | 2 | 5 | 10
  now: SoundAt
  next?: SoundAt               // from 10 to 20 s before it starts
  paused?: number              // DM only: the position in ms where it paused
}
export type Playing = {
  rev: number
  music: MusicRun | null
  loops: Record<string, SoundAt & { volume: number }>    // at most 4
  effects: { id: string, soundId?: SoundId, builtin?: TrapSound, at: number }[]     // the newest 8, and older ones still sounding, 16 at most
  dmEffects: { id: string, soundId?: SoundId, builtin?: TrapSound, at: number }[]   // the same, fired behind a blackout, DM only
  volume: { music: number, effects: number }             // 0 to 100, for everyone
}
export type SceneSound = { music: null | { playlistId?: PlaylistId, soundId?: SoundId, repeat, fade, shuffle?: true }, loops: { soundId: SoundId, volume: number }[] }
// RoomState gains sounds (keys.sound(id), "sound:<id>"), playlists (keys.playlist(id)) and playing (the singleton keys.playing).
// Scene gains sound?: SceneSound (DM only, an empty one is silence). RoomSettings gains soundNames?: true.
```

The names switch lives in the room's settings, so its Setup undo never meets the playback that rewrites `playing` every few minutes. For the same reason **no Setup op writes `playing`** (D-261102-471): `Tx.inverse()` records whole entities with their guards, so a Setup step that touched `playing` would be refused by the next tick or volume change. A Setup op may read `playing` to refuse, and what it leaves stale there (a deleted playlist's id, an effect given another use) is read through at playback. Pure helpers in shared/sound.ts: `occurrencesAt`, `playingAt(music, t)` (the track sounding at t, `next` once its time passed), `following(run)`, `reanchor(music, t)`, `soundDue(playing, sounds)` and `soundStep(playing, sounds, t)`.

So a full room can always fall silent and free space. A room whose replay failed still takes nothing.

### 6.3 Reducer (shared/reducer/sound.ts, new)

Every start is `t = Date.parse(env.at) + LEAD_MS`, so replay lands on the same times. Devices play from the confirmed state only, so a device's own clock never places a sound.

- **Uses hold to lengths**: Music 30 s to 15 min, Background 2 s to 15 min, Effect 0.2 to 30 s, each upper cap with 4.2's 100 ms allowance. So a track is longer than two crossfades plus 10 seconds, which 6.4 needs.
- `music.play` alone compares `order` with its playlist: the same ids, each once, each a music sound (or the one `soundId`). `now` is `order[index]` at t.
- **During a crossfade** (t at or after `next.at`), every music op first promotes: `next` becomes `now` with its own `at` and `index` moves on, and the outgoing track, which `playing` no longer names, fades out over 0.3 s on every device. Then `music.pause` keeps the position within the occurrence sounding at t and drops `next`. `music.resume` places that occurrence at `t - paused` under a new run. `music.skip` starts `following` at t, or ends the run on its last track with Repeat off. So a Skip during a crossing goes past the incoming track, which Now playing already names, and its button says "Skip past <name>" then. `music.stop` clears it. `music.set` calls `reanchor`: `now.at` moves to the start of the occurrence sounding at t under the old period, so the place heard goes on. Wherever `next.at` is placed (a reanchor, a resume near a track's end, a reveal), it is at least t, and the crossfade shortens to what remains of the outgoing track.
- `loop.start` refuses a fifth loop and a sound not used as Background. `effect.fire` takes an Effect or a built-in into `effects`, or `dmEffects` behind a blackout. Each list keeps its newest 8 and, past those, the entries still sounding at t, 16 at most, so firing more never cuts a long effect.
- `sound.scene` applies the scene's sound (4.6), and with `onMove` does nothing unless `meta.playerSceneId` is that scene. `sound.stopAll` clears music, loops and both effect lists.
- `sound.update` of `use` (Setup) refuses while the live run's order or a loop names the sound, and removes it from every playlist and scene sound it no longer fits, in the same op. It does not write `playing`: entries for the sound in either effect list stay as history, and playback, the projection and the visible set skip an entry whose sound is no longer an Effect, so it stops wherever it sounds. `playlist.delete` (Setup) clears the music of every scene sound that names it, and leaves a live run's `playlistId` as a label.
- `sound.delete` (no step) refuses a track in the live run's order or a loop that plays (4.1's words), removes the sound's entries from both effect lists, so its devices stop it, and removes it from every playlist and scene sound.
- `sys.sound` refuses a `from` that is not the current `playing.rev`, then takes the result it carries after a shape check.
- **Shape checks** (the state schema, `sys.sound`, import): the run's and the loops' ids name existing sounds of the right use, `index` lies in `order`, no id twice in an order or a playlist, at most 4 loops and 16 entries a list, finite times. An effect entry may name any sound id, present or not, since it is history. A run's `playlistId` need not exist, and its order is never compared with a playlist now, since a run keeps the order it started with.

shared/reads.ts: playback ops read `keys.playing` and the sounds they name, `music.play` its playlist, `sound.scene` its scene and `meta`. `sound.update` and `sound.delete` read `keys.playing` (the first to refuse, the second to edit the effect lists) and the playlists and scenes they edit. `playlist.delete` reads its playlist and the scenes it edits, never `playing`. sizeGuard counts the three new collections in the room-level part.

### 6.4 The server moves the music on

- **When** (`soundDue`). With a `next`: `next.at + fade`, when its crossfade has ended and it becomes `now`, the old track already silent. With none: the reveal, at the later of 20 seconds before the following track's start and `now.at + fade`, or `now`'s end when Repeat is off and nothing follows. Repeat one, a pause or no run: never. With tracks of at least 30 s and a crossfade of at most 10, every reveal comes 10 to 20 seconds before its start, and a track leaves the players' projection and visible set only after it stops sounding.
- **The tick** (D-261102-466). At the due time a timer enqueues a `soundTick` command in the actor's queue. Processed in order, it computes `soundStep` from the staged state and `Date.now()`, rechecks the members rule below and the replay, and stages `sys.sound` with the concrete result when anything changed, so a Play, Skip or Stop queued before it always wins and replay never recomputes. A refused step logs once a run and is not tried again until a commit changes `playing` or 60 seconds pass, whichever comes first, so a step that keeps failing ticks once a minute at most. The timer runs while any socket is open, a display's included, and is set after each commit, at load and when a socket opens.
- **Members leave.** The actor keeps when a member socket was last open. 15 minutes after that with only displays, or none, connected, a queued tick stops music, loops and effects. At load that time is the room's last commit (`rooms.last_active_at`), so a room loaded more than 15 minutes after its last commit starts silent and a deploy in the middle of a session keeps its sound.
- **Catching up** covers short gaps only: a room still loaded with no socket (under the 60-second unload) and a load within 15 minutes. `soundStep` skips whole passes of the order arithmetically.
- **The switch off**: no timer, and at load live sound that names files is stopped. **Cost**: two ops a track at most, a few hundred bytes each.

### 6.5 Projection, fan-out and privacy (shared/project.ts)

| Field | DM | Player and display |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sounds` | whole | the sounds of `now`, `next`, the loops and the `effects` entries whose sound is an Effect only: `id`, `assetId`, `ms`, `level`, `use`, and `name` for `now` and the loops while `soundNames` is on |
| `playlists`, `Scene.sound`, `dmEffects` | whole | never |
| `playing.music` | whole | `run`, `repeat`, `fade`, `now`, `next`, or null while paused |
| `playing.loops`, `volume` | whole | whole |
| `playing.effects` | whole | the entries for built-ins and for sounds still used as Effects |
| all of `playing` | whole | null behind a blackout (`paused(state, viewer)`) |

- `visibleAssetIds` adds the asset of every sound a viewer's projection holds, so the asset and display routes serve a player only what sounds now, the next track and the effects in the open list, and nothing while the switch is off.
- **Fan-out.** `dependents` adds, for players, the sound entries named before and after whenever `keys.playing`, `soundNames` or a sound's `use` changes, so a newly named sound arrives in the same batch and a withdrawn name or a reclassified effect goes. The reverse holds too: when a sound's `use` changes (by its op, Undo or Redo) and either effect list names it, `keys.playing` is a dependent for players, so its entry leaves their projection, and its voice stops, in that batch without waiting for another playback op (sound.itest.ts).
- So a player never holds the library, the playlists, the order, a scene's sound, an effect before it is fired, an effect's name, the next track's name, or a name the DM keeps. The projection reads no clock: the server's ticks write the state. View as a player projects the same way and plays nothing.

### 6.6 Undo and references

Setup history: "renaming Rain", "using Rain as background", "creating the playlist Tavern night", "changing the playlist Tavern night", "deleting the playlist Tavern night", "changing the sound of Forest road" and "Players see what is playing: on". Uploading, deleting a sound and playback record no step and clear neither Redo (D-261102-471). **Typed references** (shared/reducer/misc.ts `referencesOf`, `checkReferences` and `restoreReferenceConflicts`, which do not check the whole state on their own) check two ways. Forward, a playlist names music sounds, a scene sound names a playlist or a music sound and loop sounds, each present and of that use. A restore that brings back a playlist naming a deleted sound, a scene sound naming a deleted playlist, or either naming a sound whose use changed since, is refused. Reverse, against `playing` as well, a restore that changes a sound's `use` is refused while the live run's order or a loop names it under its current use, just as `sound.update` refuses, and while any playlist or scene sound names it under its current use, unless the same restore puts those back too. So "Effect to Background, start it as a loop, Undo setup" is refused with "Stop Rain first. It is playing as background." A restore never removes a sound (no Setup op creates one), and removing a playlist leaves a run's label alone. Effect entries are history and never block. `restoredAssetUsable` checks kind `"sound"`.

### 6.7 Versions, export and import

- **Snapshot version**: the next free one, a fill step: a snapshot without them gets `sounds: {}`, `playlists: {}` and `playing: null`. An older build then refuses a room that may hold sounds.
- **Op version**: the next free one. The new ops, and `scene.update`'s `sound` and `room.update`'s `soundNames`, are new fields that older strict schemas refuse. No stored op changes meaning, so nothing is gated. Several Later-list builds in one deploy share one bump.
- server/rooms/stateSchema.ts takes the new keys with 6.3's shape checks and 8's bounds. server/rooms/remap.ts maps sound, playlist, loop and effect ids and each `assetId`.
- **Backup** (D-261102-475): sound files go in the zip as `assets/<sha256>-sound-full.mp3` with `media` and the row's facts in the manifest. **Include sounds**, on at first, says "Sounds add 212 MB." (server/http/export.ts takes the choice). Off, the zip leaves out the files and every part of the state about sound. **Import** sets `playing` to null, so an imported room starts silent, and keeps the library, playlists and scene sounds, each file through the upload's check against the quota. After the remap it refills every sound's `ms` from its checked row (`refillSoundFacts` in remap.ts, beside animated maps' `refillMapVideos`) and checks each use against that length with 4.2's allowance. A sound whose file fails the check, or whose length does not fit its use, is dropped with its places in playlists and scene sounds, and the import's answer counts them in `soundsLeftOut`, which client/lib/api.ts's `importRoom` type carries and ImportRoomCard.tsx shows ("2 sounds were left out."). A zip without sounds, or an import while the switch is off, drops every part about sound.

## 7. Intake, storage and serving

### 7.1 The check (shared/audio/mp3.ts, new, for both sides)

`walkMp3(bytes)` reads frame headers only. The server takes a file that is MPEG-1 Layer III from its first byte to its last, at 44.1 or 48 kHz, every frame at one bitrate from 64 to 192 kbps and one channel mode, with at most one Xing, Info or LAME frame first, ending on a frame boundary, with no tag and no other byte. The length is frames times 1152 over the rate, from 0.2 s to 15 min 1 s. The client first runs `stripTags`: an ID3v2 tag at the start by its own size (at most 16 MiB), and ID3v1, APEv2 and Lyrics3 tags at the end, at most three, then the same strict walk. Refusals: `sound_type`, `sound_length` and `sound_off` (shared/apiErrors.ts).

### 7.2 Storing and serving

- server/assets/kinds.ts: kind `sound`, 24 MiB (15 minutes at 192 kbps is 20.6 MiB), 15 minutes to arrive at 32 KiB a second or more, an intake slot per session as videos have (slots.ts), the quota headroom videos keep (quota.ts: 32 MiB left free), DM only, players read it, `media` `mp3`, a pipeline number of its own. db/schema.sql adds `sound` to the kind check and `audio jsonb` (`ms`, `channels`, `kbps`), idempotent like the thumb and video steps.
- `AssetService.ingest` branches as for videos: no sharp, `checkSound`, one file `<sha256>-sound-full-v1.mp3`. The same bytes reuse the row, so two sounds may name one file.
- The upload route accepts `audio/mpeg` for kind `sound` alone, checked before any body byte, and answers `sound_off` while the switch is off. Hourly: `SOUND_HOURLY`, 120 a session and 240 a room.
- Served as `audio/mpeg` from the row, with animated maps' single-range answers, strong ETag, `nosniff`, sandboxing CSP, `cross-origin-resource-policy: same-origin` and `private, immutable`. While the switch is off every sound variant answers 404.
- `GET /api/rooms/:id/storage`, DM only: `{ used, quota, sounds }` in bytes, kept per room and recomputed at most every 10 seconds and after an upload or delete. The image library's list leaves sounds out.

### 7.3 Cleanup and backups

A sound row no `sounds` entry names takes `unused_since` and goes a day later, by the thumbnails' rule (server/assets/cleanup.ts), so an upload whose `sound.create` never landed frees its space. Delete also asks the asset route to delete the row at once, which it does once nothing names it. Nightly backups copy `.mp3` beside the image and video files (docker/backup.sh's BusyBox-safe patterns, scripts/backup-check.ts, the regenerated selfhost/compose.yml).

### 7.4 Security and the encoder's license

The server never decodes audio, so a crafted file meets no decoder there (D-261102-463). A file is served with a fixed type and never runs as a page. A crafted frame can still reach a player's decoder inside the browser's media sandbox, only in the room of the DM who added it, the exposure animated maps accepts for video. docs/assurance-case.md says what a sound gets. The CSP needs nothing beyond animated maps' `media-src 'self' blob:`, and lamejs is plain JavaScript.

`@breezystack/lamejs` is LGPL-3.0. Its package's LICENSE is a short note, so the full LGPL-3.0 and GPL-3.0 texts are committed (client/features/sound/upload/licenses/) and a Vite plugin emits them beside the encoder's chunk, as `pdfjsDecoders` does for pdf.js's decoders (vite.config.ts), with a `/*! @license */` banner naming the package, its version, its license and where its source is. The library stays its own chunk, so a recipient can rebuild the app with a changed library from the app's own AGPL source (docs/development.md says how). CREDITS.md gains a line for it under the code shipped to browsers.

## 8. Performance, sizes and caps

| What | Cap | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A file | 24 MiB, 0.2 s to 15 min, 64 to 192 kbps | 15 minutes at 192 kbps is 20.6 MiB |
| By use | Music 30 s and up, Background 2 s and up, Effect 30 s at most, 100 ms over each upper cap for encoding | 6.4's reveal lead, a loop's crossing |
| Reading in the DM's tab | 200 MB of file, 800 MB on a computer and 150 MB on a phone or tablet for a whole decode, counting the file's bytes, its own channels at its own rate (96 kHz when unknown), the 44.1 kHz copy and the stereo mix, a file whose channels are unread refused | An iPhone tab dies near a few hundred MB |
| Effect lists | the newest 8 and those still sounding, 16 at most, each list | A long effect is never cut by newer fires |
| Sounds a room | 300 | About 60 KB of DM-only state |
| Playlists | 30, 100 tracks each, no id twice | About 120 KB at the worst |
| At once | 4 loops, 1 music run, 6 effects | 11 elements, 48 MB of decoded effects |
| Storage | the room's quota, 1 GiB by default, 32 MiB headroom | About 17 hours at 128 kbps |

On a mid-range phone one 128 kbps stream costs about 1 to 2% of a core, so music and 4 loops stay under 10%. The voice check runs once a second and draws nothing. The panel re-renders once a second only while shown with music playing. Data: about 58 MB an hour for music, plus one download of each loop of 5 MB or less. A bigger loop streams by Range and may download again on each pass, up to about 58 MB an hour more for each such loop. The engine loads lazily the first time the room plays something, except the activation helper and context.ts. The encoder chunk loads only to convert. The lane measures that a 10-minute stereo WAV converts in under a minute on a laptop, and that a 7-minute stereo WAV reads on an iPhone. The live play review measures battery over an evening.

## 15. Decisions

- **D-261102-460** Scope: S1 is the DM's own files behind the server owner's switch (D-261102-513), music, loops, effects, scene sound, sync, each device's choice and the display. Built-ins work with the switch off. S2 is a starter pack. Rejected: a pack in S1 (each work needs its own license and provenance check), never one, sounds on the map in S1, built-ins hidden with the switch off (they download nothing).
- **D-261102-461** Music one at a time, up to 4 loops each at its own volume, effects by tap, the 5 trap sounds as built-ins. Rejected: Syrinscape-style moods with random one-shots, every playlist at once, more than 4 loops, no built-ins.
- **D-261102-462** One Sound panel in play, the DM's always and a player's while something plays. Rejected: a top bar button or mute (the project owner asks for less over the map), a DM panel section (already the longest), a key.
- **D-261102-463** The DM's browser decodes, measures and makes a constant bitrate MP3, copying frames or encoding with `@breezystack/lamejs` 1.2.7 in a Worker, with its LGPL texts beside its chunk. The server walks frames and decodes nothing. Rejected: ffmpeg on the server (size, CPU, the largest media parser there is, as animated maps' D-261102-442 found), keeping originals as sent (Ogg fails on older iPhones, variable bitrate seeks inexactly, tags carry names and art), a WebAssembly encoder (`'wasm-unsafe-eval'`), WebCodecs (not everywhere, no MP3), lamejs 1.2.1 (its build is not an ES module).
- **D-261102-464** Music 30 s to 15 min, Background 2 s and up, Effects to 30 s, checked exactly on the source with 100 ms allowed over each upper cap for encoding, 24 MiB at 64 to 192 kbps, a 200 MB file and 800 or 150 MB in the DM's tab for a whole decode, counting the file's bytes, its header's channels at its own rate (96 kHz when unread), the 44.1 kHz copy and the stereo mix, a file whose channels are unread refused, all before decoding, 15 minutes to upload, the quota with 32 MiB headroom. Rejected: an hour (over a GB decoded), decoding before the checks (a phone tab dies before it can say so), a two-channel estimate (a 6-channel file passes it and then allocates three times as much), counting unread channels as 6 (the decoder allocates a 16-channel file's own channels first), exact caps on the encoded length (an honest 30-second effect encodes to 30.01 s), copying 320 kbps files, 40 MiB (no allowed file needs it).
- **D-261102-465** Loudness evened at upload to -18 LUFS bounded by the peak, kept in tenths of a dB, a master 3 dB down and a compressor, measured to keep the loudest allowed mix under 0 dBFS. Rejected: none (the DM chases every volume), measuring on the server (needs a decoder), a volume per sound set by hand, claiming the compressor never lets a transient clip.
- **D-261102-466** Library collections and a `playing` singleton, times from `env.at` plus 400 ms, a queued tick computed from the staged state with a `from` rev, a refused tick backing off until a commit to `playing` or 60 s, ticking while any socket is open, a stop 15 minutes after the last member, a stop at load after 15 minutes without a commit, stops and deletes allowed at the quota. Rejected: re-arming a refused tick at its passed due time (it spins), devices working out the schedule (players would hold the whole order), computing the step before queuing it (a stale step overwrites a newer Play), no ticks for a display (the TV falls silent at the next track), timers in an empty room, a full room that cannot stop.
- **D-261102-467** Players get now, next 10 to 20 s early, the loops, the effects fired in the open whose sounds are still Effects and the volumes, names of `now` and the loops only, with "Players see what is playing" off at first, and nothing behind a blackout, not even later. Rejected: names on at first, names for `next` and effects (a title before it plays, an effect's file name), preloading the board, sound behind a blackout, the whole order.
- **D-261102-468** A `time` frame with measurement epochs, one timeline helper with occurrences, elements through Web Audio with five fixed rates and a seek past 150 ms, loops from a blob, effects as buffers on the context's clock. Rejected: `serverOffset` alone (a network delay off), a time on `pong` (older pages drop it and reconnect), decoded buffers for music (over 100 MB a track), seeks only, free-running rates (some browsers glitch on each change), streaming loops (Safari may download each pass).
- **D-261102-469** Each device decides: a player is asked at the first state with something to hear, late joins and effects included, and the answering tap unlocks, DM devices and displays start on, the pool is unlocked only with consent, and one page per browser plays: the owner keeps it while eligible and holds a Web Lock where the browser has them, so a takeover waits for its release or the browser freeing a closed page's lock. Without Web Locks an audio-clock lease on its own gain node, armed only beside another live page, silences a stalled owner on its own, a takeover by time comes only after an armed lease's end, and an unarmed owner is waited on for its release. Either way a page drops ownership the moment its context leaves running. Rejected: every device on (a table echoes), off with a switch to find, one room setting for all, unlocking every page's pool (it pauses other apps' music on an iPhone), the shown page always winning (every tab switch hands off, and a locked page silences a working one), a heartbeat lapse alone (a stalled page can still sound), a lease on a lone page (an Android phone with its screen off fades itself), trusting a lease through a suspension (its clock stood still), a takeover by time from an unarmed owner (a stalled page that still renders sounds beside the new one), stealing the lock once a lease ran out (a suspended page's old deadline may sound as it resumes), the lease beside Web Locks (it silences a stalled owner nobody replaces), asking again after a passed ask in the same page load.
- **D-261102-470** The DM sets the music's, effects' and each loop's volume for everyone, loops starting at 70% or the scene's, and each device its own three. Rejected: one device slider, a volume per sound, a remembered last volume (each co-DM's device would remember another).
- **D-261102-471** Library edits, playlists, scene sounds and the names switch are Setup, with typed references checked forward and reverse on undo, `playing` included. No Setup op writes `playing`: a deleted playlist's id stays in a run as a label and a reclassified effect's entries stay as history, skipped at playback. Uploads, deletes and playback record no step. Rejected: playback on the Play history, an undoable delete, cleaning `playing` inside a Setup op (the next tick or volume change would refuse its undo), refusing a use change while an effect entry names it (blocked until 8 more effects fire).
- **D-261102-472** A scene's sound, silence included, saved from what plays, started by the move only while the players are still there, or by Play it, keeping what matches, copied by Duplicate. Rejected: a fifth settings group, starting it inside `room.setPlayerScene` (a core op under a gate), an unconditional start after the move (two DMs leave scene B with A's sound), no way to save silence.
- **D-261102-473** Sound plays on in the background wherever the browser lets it, and every stop by the browser or a call is caught up on return. Rejected: stopping on every hide (a player glancing at their sheet loses the music), promising background play on iPhones, a silent loop to keep a locked page alive.
- **D-261102-474** The display plays by default, Sound On or Off in its menu, a card while audio waits, and a can't-play line where its browser lacks the mixer. Rejected: a volume in the 5-second menu, off at first, a degraded mixer for such TVs now.
- **D-261102-475** Sounds in backups with Include sounds on at first, an import that starts silent and keeps the library. Rejected: always including them (past the web import's 95 MB), never, keeping the state without the files, importing `playing` (an imported room would start playing).
- **D-261102-476** The next free op and snapshot versions, the snapshot a fill step, or one shared bump. Rejected: no bump.
- **D-261102-477** The DM's X-card card gains Stop the sound, which stops effects too. An effect is cancelled by its own entry leaving (stopped, deleted, reclassified, or past the list's 16), and the engine-wide generation only by Stop all, Room sound off, a blackout and disposal, with a list that keeps sounding entries, and an entry starts on a page once at most, never after it was withdrawn there. Rejected: stopping on every X-card by itself, a stop that leaves effects playing, one generation for every leaving entry (aging out cancels unrelated loads and cuts long effects), starting any entry newer than the load (Undo of a change of use replays it).
- **D-261102-478** No new key or gesture, and lane audiounlock's `client/lib/activation.ts` for every unlock: `armAudioUnlock` for the context, `onActivation` for the pool. Rejected: a mute key, `pointerdown` alone (a touch is not an activation), a helper of this feature's own, `armAudioUnlock` alone (it resumes a context and blesses no element).
- **D-261102-479** A 400 ms lead, effects whole up to a second late, partway with 3 s left, else skipped, never replayed for a late consent, and no log line, toast or caption for a change of sound. Rejected: no lead, a log line per track, captions for effects (the DM's words carry what matters).

The build review's first round, the engine and the panel (lane musfix-a):

- **A pause** (6.3) silences the DM's devices too, whose state keeps the paused run whole, and there it fades over 2 s, as a player's music does when its projection drops the run.

- **The server's switch** is unknown until `/api/config` answers. Meanwhile the DM's panel shows the shared loading line, "Checking for sound files…", never the off line. A failed read says "Couldn't check whether this server offers sound files. The built-in effects still work." with Try again (D-261102-741).
- **TVs** (5.5, D-261102-742). A display checks for Web Audio and `createMediaElementSource` only, not for three elements playing together. The blessing plays a tenth of a second of silence, so most elements have ended by the time it settles, and a count of elements sounding then would turn away a TV that works. A TV that plays only one element at a time is not told apart, and the live play review checks a TV at the table.

The build review's second round and the integration on the whole stack (lane musint):

- **A refused play** (5.5, D-261102-737). The fast lane on sounds heard in music playback (lane sndglitch, D-261102-870 and D-261102-871), measured in Chrome with a tone recorded off the mixer's output by an AudioWorklet:

- **The pitch is not kept** (5.3). With `preservesPitch` on, Chrome time-stretches every nudge, and each change of rate stepped the waveform, a click often with a few milliseconds of dropout, while the stretch warbled between: 28 such glitches at 38 changes of rate in two minutes of nudges, none with the pitch let go. A nudge is now 26 cents of pitch at 1.5% and 51 at 3%, for a second or two.
- **A wake keeps the clock's last reading** (5.1). A wake's new epoch fell back to the store's offset, one network delay off, just as the wake's forced check read the voices, so with a socket 40 ms slower one way every show of the window sought each voice (an audible dip) and nudged it back after: 12 seeks at 12 shows in two minutes, 1 with the last reading standing in until the epoch's first answer. The store's offset is still used when it is more than a second from that reading, as after a sleep that stopped the page's clock.

Live play review 30 (lane lp30fix):

- **A restart** (5.3, 5.6, LP30-SND-1). A file an element is loading when the server goes away fails its voice, as a file that cannot load does, and the Sound panel says the music didn't load. Once the room reconnects every failed voice loads its file again, as Try again does, so music and background sounds come back in place without a tap. A file that still cannot load fails again. A voice placed on an element whose last load of the same file failed sets the source again, since only that loads it, and only an error of the voice's own file fails the voice: one of a source swapped in is the check's to put back.

## Design review

Five rounds of design review, by Claude and Codex reviewers, shaped this note. What they changed, in brief:

- **The DM's tab.** One memory bound for a whole decode counts the file's own bytes, its header's channels at its own rate (96 kHz when the header says nothing), the 44.1 kHz copy and the stereo mix. A file whose channels cannot be read from its header, or whose count is not 1, 2, 4 or 6, is refused before any decoding, with each container's header named (4.2). Constant bitrate MP3s and plain WAVs are read in pieces for loudness, with pre-roll, and never decoded whole.
- **Lengths.** The use caps hold exactly on the source's own length, with 100 ms allowed over each upper cap for the encoder's padding, in the reducer and the import alike (4.2).
- **Setup and playback apart.** No Setup op writes `playing`, so its undo never meets the ticks. A deleted playlist's id stays in a run as a label, an effect given another use stays in the lists as history that playback skips, and typed references are checked forward and reverse on every restore (6.1, 6.3, 6.6).
- **The tick.** It is queued like any command, computed from the staged state with a `from` rev, backs off once refused, runs while any socket is open, and stops the sound 15 minutes after the last member (6.4).
- **Effects.** Each load and voice belongs to its list entry and is cancelled when that entry leaves. The lists keep entries still sounding, so firing more never cuts a long effect, and an entry starts on a page once at most (5.4).
- **One page plays per browser.** Ownership is a Web Lock where the browser has one. Without it an audio-clock lease on a gain node of its own, armed only beside another live page, silences a stalled owner, and a page whose context leaves `running` gives up ownership at once (4.4).
- **Privacy.** Players get the next track only 10 to 20 seconds before it starts, names only of what plays now and only while the DM shares them, and a reclassified effect leaves their projection in the same batch (6.5).
- **Words and smaller points.** The refusals' words, Skip past an incoming track, the scene's sound line, and the import's count of sounds left out.
- **Placed once, then left to play** (5.3, D-261102-940, 2026-11-04). Through a phone on a weak signal the music stuttered: each seek the drift check made after a stall waited on the network again, and the speed changes bent the pitch. The owner: "We just want it to start and stop within a second or two of one another to be close enough. When the DM pauses something and then resumes it we should simply start everyone from that point that the DM resumed from rather than trying to keep constantly in sync." Each voice is now placed when it starts and then plays at speed 1. Effects ready up to 2 s late play whole, late, where 1 s did before.
