Status: draft 5, revised for design review rounds 1 to 4 (HX-ASTRA-01 to 15 and HO-1 to HO-23, all adopted, decided as D-261025-11 and D-261025-13) and the owner's room default (D-261025-10). The design starts now, and its build waits for the current scope and follows the per-player vision run. D-261025-10 adds the room's default for new scenes. D-261025-11 and D-261025-13 settle the reviews' questions. ## 1. The answer in short

- **A scene's grid is Squares, Hex rows or Hex columns.** A room setting, "New scenes", picks the shape a new Battle mat or Map image starts with, Squares by default. Builder scenes, starter maps and Universal VTT imports start as squares. The new scene form shows the shape and changes it for that one scene. The scene's Grid settings change it later.
- **The world stays measured in cells.** One cell is the distance between two neighboring hex centers, the room's feet per cell, so a hex is one cell across its flat sides. Positions, the fog's bitmap, sight, lights, radii, area shapes and free walls keep their units and their code. Only what reads the grid's cells changes.
- **Every hex is one step.** Hexes have no diagonals, so the room's diagonal rule only combines a distance with height.
- **Sizes follow the DMG's hex table:** Tiny, Small and Medium 1 hex, Large 3, Huge 7, Gargantuan 12.
- **Areas keep their true shapes**, graded per hex by the room's rule, at least half by default, as the 2024 DMG says for "squares or hexes". An emanation is the exception: it covers the hexes within its size in steps of its creature's hexes, so it agrees with the ruler.
- **Walls on hexes are free walls**, drawn as lines from H2. No hex edge keys.
- **Three phases.** H1 runs a fight on a hex map without walls: place, move, measure, areas, fog and party vision. H2 adds walls, ways around them, squeezing, difficult ground, riding and carrying, and traps other than pits. H3 adds heights, stairs, pits, Reveal room and Find the grid for hexes. Painted ground, pillars, Count the squares and the Universal VTT import stay square, each saying so in plain words.
- **The server holds each phase's line.** On a hex scene it refuses every op whose rules are not hex yet, and the shape switch, an undo and a redo all refuse to leave square-only data on a hex scene or hex data on a square one.

## 2. What the 2024 rules say

- **Areas of effect, 2024 DMG ("Running the Game", Areas of Effect),** as quoted in a public rules forum and not checked against the book, which neither review could open: "An area of effect must be translated onto squares or hexes to determine which potential targets are in the area", "choose an intersection of squares or hexes to be the point of origin", and "If an area of effect covers at least half a square or hex, the entire square or hex is affected."
- **Emanation, 2024 rules glossary:** an emanation extends from the creature or object it comes from in every direction. On hexes that reads as steps from the creature's hexes (HX-D11).
- **Creature space on hexes.** The 2014 DMG's table "Creature Size on Squares and Hexes" gives Tiny 4 per hex, Small and Medium 1 hex, Large 3 hexes, Huge 7 and Gargantuan 12 or more, and says a miniature of another footprint still uses the creature's real size for every other rule. No 2024 text on hex footprints was found, so this note follows the 2014 table.
- **Movement.** The 2024 Player's Handbook counts a grid in 5 ft squares with its diagonal rule. Nothing found in the 2024 books covers moving on hexes. This note counts one step per hex, and a hex has no diagonal to rule on.
- **Squeezing.** The 2024 rule lets a creature squeeze into a space sized for a creature one size smaller. From H2 it works on hexes as on squares (D-261025-13): a Large creature into one hex.
- **No rule covers** height on a hex grid, hex walls or hex fog. Those follow the app's square rules where they carry over, as below.

## 4. The hex grid

### 4.1 Geometry

- **Two orientations.** Hex rows have pointed tops, so a row runs straight across. Hex columns have flat tops. Every rule is written for rows and reads columns by swapping x and y. That swap is a mirror, not a turn: the neighbor order, clockwise from east on rows, reads counterclockwise from south on columns, and so does every rule with a handedness (rings, stair facings, rotation steps). A chirality test pins it.
- **Constants.** `HEX_S = 0.8660254037844386` (√3/2, the spacing of rows) and `HEX_R = 0.5773502691896258` (1/√3, center to corner), written as literals. In doubles `1.5 * HEX_R !== HEX_S`, so a corner computed from the hex above differs from the same corner computed from the hex below. One function, `cornerAt(g, index)`, gives every corner by its own index, rounded to the 1/64 lattice, for Large anchors, area origins and wall ends alike. Nothing derives a corner from a center.
- **Keys.** A hex is `"col,row"`, offset coordinates, odd rows shifted half a cell right. The center of hex (c, r) is `(c + 0.5 + 0.5 * (r % 2), HEX_R + r * HEX_S)`. Distance, lines and rounding go through cube coordinates inside `hex.ts` and never reach stored data.
- **The scene's hexes** lie wholly inside its rectangle. Bounds stay in cells (at most 200 by 200), so a 30 by 20 scene holds 30 hexes in even rows, 29 in odd rows and 22 rows, and a 200 by 200 one 230 rows.
- **Which hex holds a point:** the hex whose center is nearest, ties to the smaller row, then the smaller column. On hex columns the ties mirror: the smaller column, then the smaller row. A fog sub-cell's center is an odd multiple of 1/8, so the upright sides of hex rows never split one, and the rounding settles the sloped sides the same way everywhere. A scene hex holds 12 to 16 sub-cells.
- **Exactness.** `hex.ts` uses only `+`, `-`, `*`, `/`, `Math.floor`, `Math.round`, `Math.abs`, min and max, and joins the test that bans `sqrt`, `hypot`, trigonometry and powers in what vision and segments import. The reducer reads it on replay, so every device must agree bit for bit.
- **The map's origin** is reduced into the lattice's period at or after the image's top-left, never negative, so a hex scene never reads as an older import (`cameWithGrid`).

### 4.2 Footprints

| Size | Hexes | Anchor | Reference hex | Box side |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tiny | 1 | its hex's center | its hex | 0.5 |
| Small, Medium | 1 | its hex's center | its hex | 1 |
| Large | 3: a hex and the two below it | the corner the three share | the top hex | 2 |
| Huge | 7: a hex and its six neighbors | the center hex's center | the center hex | 3 |
| Gargantuan | 12: the Large three and the nine around them | the same corner | the top hex | 4 |

- **Position.** A token's `x, y` stays the top-left of its size's box, whose center is the anchor. Drawing, hit areas, the action bar, drag frames, tethers and labels work unchanged. Only rules that read occupied cells branch on the grid.
- **Anchors** for Tiny, Small, Medium and Huge are hex centers. For Large and Gargantuan they are corners (`cornerAt`), one lattice the same shape as the centers, so every size moves in the same six steps.
- **Ties.** A snap ties to the smaller row, then the smaller column, mirrored on hex columns (the smaller column, then the smaller row). Large and Gargantuan snap to the nearest corner as rounded onto the 1/64 lattice. A resize keeps the reference hex where it was: a Medium growing Large keeps its hex as the top one, and a Large shrinking Medium keeps its top hex.
- **A leg's steps** are the hex steps between its start and end reference hexes, so a leg whose stops differ in size still counts.
- **A custom size** reads as its size category (`sizeCategory`, longer side), anchored at the center of its stored box as drawn. The size picker offers categories only on a hex scene.
- **Inside the scene.** Wherever a rule keeps a footprint inside the scene today (placement, a player's teleport, landings), its hexes must be the scene's hexes.
- **Height** keeps its default, the box side times the feet per cell.

### 4.3 Distance, movement and chunks

- **Between two creatures:** the fewest hex steps between any hex of one and any hex of the other. Adjacent hexes are 1 step, shared hexes 0.
- **With height:** vertical steps as today. `five` takes `max(h, v)`, `euclid` `hypot(h, v)` rounded to the foot, and `fiveTen` `max + floor(min / 2)` with the path's vertical counter carried across legs as `legFeet` carries it (`distance.ts:68-85`). The horizontal counter stays 0, since a hex has no diagonal.
- **Chunks.** Hex cell data lives in chunks of 16 by 16 hexes by key, so a 200 by 200 hex rows scene reaches chunk row 14. Free walls keep the world chunk of their midpoint. Every bound check on cell data goes through one `inScene(grid, bounds, key)`: `reducer/map.ts:140` and its callers, `walls.ts:557` and `1441`, `builder/cells.ts`, and the per-cell grids of `walk.ts` and `heights.ts`, sized by the scene's hex columns and rows. Chunk indices up to 14 fit the room import's schema (-100 to 100). `protocol.ts`'s `chunkIndex` names only world chunks (free walls, fog tiles) and stays, and so does `limits.ts:893`, which sizes raster fog tiles. A test marks the last row of a 200 by 200 hex rows scene and the last column of a hex columns one.

### 4.4 Walk spots and sweeps

- **The hex line** of a leg runs from its start reference hex to its end one, by cube interpolation from a start nudged by 1e-6. On a hex scene `routeWalk` gives it, so every reader of walk spots reads the same hexes: `routeSeen`, the area sweep, the trap sweep from H2, and the drag preview's corners (`ephemeral.ts`). A leg through one fogged hex is not `routeSeen`.
- **Area sweeps keep height.** A leg takes `max(hex steps, vertical steps) / SWEEP_STEP` samples, as `areaTriggers.ts:348` counts elevation today. Each sample stands on the hex line's hex at its fraction, with its elevation interpolated. So a creature rising from 0 to 50 ft through a 5 ft sphere at 25 ft enters it.

## 5. Decisions, with rejected options

- **HX-D1 Orientation:** both, Hex rows first in every list. Hex map art comes either way, and one swap covers both. Rejected: one orientation (half the hex maps could not line up), Hex columns first.
- **HX-D2 World unit:** one cell is the spacing of hex centers. Rejected: a hex coordinate world (fog, sight, lights, areas and free walls would all change). Rejected: one cell across a hex's corners.
- **HX-D3 Distance:** one step per hex, the room's rule only for height. Rejected: Euclidean horizontal distance under true distance (a ruler would disagree with the hexes the table counts).
- **HX-D4 Footprints:** the DMG table, 1, 3, 7, 12. Rejected: Large as a 4-hex diamond (no center), Gargantuan as 19 (far beyond 20 ft), custom boxes on hexes.
- **HX-D5 Tiny:** one hex, at its center. Rejected: four spots per hex (no even split).
- **HX-D6 Large's triangle** points one way, two hexes below one. Rejected: both ways (two lattices, and the creature would turn as it moves).
- **HX-D7 Walls:** free walls only. In H2 a hex scene's Walls and doors draws Wall lines: tap or drag point to point, each line a free wall, door or window, split past 8 cells. Points snap to hex corners while Snap to hex corners is on (the default), else to the 1/64 lattice, for tracing art. Rejected: hex edge keys (three sides per cell, a new derive and new automatic walls, for walls that seldom follow hex sides on map art). Rejected: Wall lines on squares too (not asked for).
- **HX-D8 Blocking on hexes (H2):** a step is allowed when every footprint hex's center-to-center step crosses no movement wall, touching counting as today, and at its end no movement wall crosses the line between two neighboring hexes of the footprint. So a creature walks where its whole footprint fits, and squeezes (HX-D17) where only the footprint one size smaller does. A Large creature walks two rows and squeezes through one. The DM's moves are never stopped. Rejected: a square core around the anchor (a Large anchor in a one-row corridor lies on the corridor's wall). Rejected: no squeezing on hexes (D-261025-13: the 2024 rule applies on any grid).
- **HX-D9 The builder on hexes:** Builder scenes may be hexes (D-261025-11), and the builder offers what each phase supports, saying so for the rest: H1 lights, stamps, labels and Clear build, H2 Wall lines, Remove, door states, Difficult ground and Erase by hexes, H3 Heights and stairs. Painted ground, rooms, pillars and automatic walls stay square. Rejected: the whole builder on hexes (textured hex floors and hex automatic walls are a large build for dungeons people draw on squares). Rejected: refusing hexes for Builder scenes.
- **HX-D10 Fog and sight:** the world raster as it is. A token is read on the sub-cells inside its hexes (its squeezed hexes while it squeezes). From H2, when walls exist, only those whose centers its stored center sees past no sight wall count, for sight, fog withholding and drag previews alike, so a wall through one of its hexes never shows it from the far side. A stored center within 1/4096 cell of a sight wall steps off it as an eye does (`stepOffWalls`). One that cannot reads no sub-cell, and the token is unseen and counts as under fog: it fails closed. Party members stay shown as always. Rejected: whole hexes joined to the reference hex (a wall can cut the reference hex itself). Reveal room on hexes (H3) floods hexes, a step blocked where a movement wall crosses the line between two centers. Rejected: a hex fog raster.
- **HX-D11 Areas:** true shapes graded per hex, except emanations (D-261025-11). An emanation covers every hex within `floor(size / feetPerCell)` steps of its creature's hexes, height counted by the room's rule from the footprint's vertical extent, and clipped by walls through probes at each footprint hex's center, a hex counting when the room's rule passes on its visible part. An emanation that stays put stores its creature's size around its origin (`AreaFootprint.size`), and its hexes are `footprintHexes(grid, size, snapAnchor(grid, size, origin))` on every read. Moving the area moves the origin (`placement.ts:606`), so the hexes move with it, across an odd row as across an even one, since they are found in world space and never by shifting keys. A sweep (`areaTriggers.ts:369`) and an undo read them the same way. It still writes w and h, as its size's box, beside `size`, so a square scene after a switch or a restore reads the same creature (`storedBox`). An older one with only w by h reads as the size whose box that is (the longer side for an uneven box). A size reads on any grid, so a template holds no cell keys, and a switch or a restore re-reads it as it re-reads a token. A free origin snaps to the nearest hex corner or center. A cone, line or cube cast from a token starts at the corner or side midpoint of its footprint's outline where the aim leaves it. The 45° step reads 30°. A placed area moves by whole lattice steps. Rejected: true-shape emanations (a 15 ft one catches 10 of the 24 hexes the ruler puts at 20 ft). Rejected: absolute hex keys for a stationary footprint (they stay behind when the area moves). Rejected: corners only (an eastward cone from a hex rows creature would start off center). Rejected: hex stencils per spell.
- **HX-D12 Line up grid:** a box 3 hexes across, holding its 7 hexes, 3 by 2.89 cells on hex rows (2.89 by 3 on columns), kept to that shape as it is dragged, resized by its corners and nudged. Count the squares stays square. Find the grid stayed square until H3, which searches every map for squares and hexes (docs/design-v1-grid-detect.md 10.7). Rejected: placing two hex centers (a worse fit on a small screen).
- **HX-D13 Imports and exports:** a Universal VTT import makes a square scene whatever the room's default, and says so. A scene that came with its grid (`cameWithGrid`: `gridFromFile` or a negative origin) cannot change shape. Export as image draws hexes at a whole number of px per hex and names the file `crypt-hex-70px.png`, which the upload's size hint already ignores. Rejected: the `WxH` name for hexes (other VTTs would read squares).
- **HX-D14 The shape's invariant:** cell data belongs to the exact shape it was written under, Hex rows and Hex columns being different shapes, since one key names different places on each. A scene holds only cell data of its own shape, and on hexes only what its phase supports. So a switch between any two shapes, rows to columns included, is refused while the scene holds cell data, naming it: painted ground, edge walls and "no wall" marks, heights, stair marks, difficult ground, any trap, a fog draft shape of kind `cells`, a squeezed token, and in H1 also free walls and any attach link. A stamp marks the shape of grid-keyed content only: a chunk's cells, edges, heights, `up` and `rough`, a fog draft's `cells` shapes, and every trap (`BuildChunk.grid`, `FogDraft.grid`, `Trap.grid`, absent on squares). The reducer writes it whenever it writes such content on a hex scene and drops it with the last of it. A restore (undo or redo) passes two separate checks, each refused with the switch's words. The stamp check runs only when the value it writes holds grid-keyed content, and fails when its stamp is not its scene's shape. The phase check runs on everything it writes, and fails when it brings onto a hex scene what that scene's phase refuses: in H1 free walls, traps, attach links and squeezed tokens. So neither two DMs nor a long history can bring rows data onto columns, square data onto hexes, or a later phase's things into an earlier one. Free walls and world-coordinate fog shapes (rects, polygons, strokes, all) mean the same on every grid and carry no stamp: fog shapes restore on any shape in every phase, so an H1 fog undo goes through, and free walls from H2, so a Wall line's undo after a switch goes through. Tokens keep their spots, and every rule reads them at their nearest anchor at once. A map scene keeps its cellPx and origin and opens Line up grid. Rejected: converting square data to hexes, refusing while tokens are on the scene, snapping every token in the same op.
- **HX-D15 Arrow keys:** Left and Right move along a row on hex rows. Up and Down go right of straight from an even row and left of it from an odd row, so presses zigzag and keep to one column. Hex columns mirror it. Rejected: six keys.
- **HX-D16 Size row:** a hex scene's size reads in the room's feet-per-cell steps, with a line saying how many hexes it holds. Rejected: sizes typed in hexes and rows (rows do not fit whole cells).
- **HX-D17 Squeezing (H2, D-261025-13):** as on squares, by the 2024 rule. A squeezing creature takes the footprint one size smaller, on that size's anchors: a Large one stands on one hex, a Huge one on a Large's three. Every rule reads the squeezed footprint while `squeeze` is set, as on squares. A move with `squeeze` is accepted where the smaller footprint fits by HX-D8 and no full footprint holding it does, the narrow space test on hexes. Each step squeezed pays the same extra as on squares, and any move without the flag ends it. Rejected: only the DM's moves putting a creature in a narrower space.
- **HX-D18 Hex lines:** drawn in the board's own redraws, as square lines are, but only the hexes in view and only while a hex is at least 6 px across, so a pan costs the hexes on screen. Rejected: every hex on every redraw (46,000 on the largest scene).
- **HX-D19 The room's default (D-261025-10, D-261025-13):** "New scenes" in Room settings, Grid and distance, last in the group. A Battle mat and a Map image (pasted ones included) start with it. A Builder scene and a starter map start as squares whatever it says, as a Universal VTT import does, since the builder's core tools and the starter art are square. The form's Grid row changes the shape for that one scene, and a Builder scene set to hexes shows the builder's limits there. A starter map made as hexes opens Line up grid rather than use its measured square grid. A duplicate keeps its source's shape. Only the form reads the setting: a `scene.create` without `gridShape` makes squares, so replay never depends on it. Rejected: a per-device preference, remembering the last shape used, Builder and starter maps following the default.
- **HX-D20 Phase refusals (D-261025-11):** the server refuses on a hex scene every op whose rules are not hex yet (section 6), so no stored op on a hex scene ever needs a later phase's rule gated. Rejected: gating each later rule by op version (more replay paths to test for ops nobody could make).
- **HX-D21 A shape change reprojects** the room, as a change of fog or vision does, and so do its undo and redo, since tokens can fall under fog or out of sight without moving (HX-ASTRA-01).
- **HX-D22 A wall change reprojects the tokens around it (H2, HO-19, HX-ASTRA-12).** HX-D10's clip makes a token's withholding depend on walls, so on a hex scene any write that changes walls (every op that moves `wallsVersion`, `door.set` and `build.walls` among them, and a restore of a chunk) adds to `dependents` (`project.ts:2163`) every token on the changed chunk or its 8 neighbors, a token being on a chunk when the world box of its footprint (the squeezed one while it squeezes) overlaps the chunk's, whatever vision and fog do. Eight suffice: a free wall reaches at most 4 cells past its chunk, and a footprint is at most 4 cells across. A chunk of hex keys (H3's heights make raised edges) counts by the world box its hexes cover, grown the same 16 cells each way. Rejected: counting it as a global change (every Wall line stroke would reproject the room).

## 7. Model and versions

```ts
type GridShape = "hexRows" | "hexColumns";
// Scene: absent reads as squares, the shape of every scene before it.
gridShape?: GridShape;
// RoomSettings: the shape new scenes start with, absent reads as squares.
newScenes?: GridShape;
// AreaFootprint: the size an emanation that stays put was cast from, read around its origin on hexes.
size?: Exclude<CreatureSize, "custom">;
// BuildChunk, Trap and FogDraft: the hex shape they were written under, absent for squares.
grid?: GridShape;
```

- **Ops.** `scene.create` takes `gridShape`. `scene.update`'s patch takes `gridShape: "squares" | GridShape`, "squares" removing the field, so a square scene's stored form and its undo hash never change. `room.update`'s settings patch takes `newScenes` the same way. The reducer refuses a switch or a restore that breaks HX-D14, naming the data in the scene's own cell word: "This scene has difficult ground and 2 traps on its squares, which can't stand on hexes. Remove them to change the grid." and "Can't undo that: difficult ground on squares can't stand on this scene's hexes." DM only. `scene.duplicate` and the room export copy the shape, and the reducer never reads `newScenes`. The schema takes `AreaFootprint.size` from the size list, never `custom`. The stamps are in H1's model, and H1's restore check reads an absent stamp on grid-keyed content as squares.
- **Players** receive `gridShape` in their scene copy (`project.ts:999`), since they snap and draw by it. `tx.ts:748` counts it in a scene's change of visibility (HX-D21). A change of `newScenes` reprojects as every settings change does today, which is rare and cheap enough.
- **Meanings on a hex scene.** `map.cellPx` and `phaseGrid.cellPx` are px across a hex's flats. `originPx` is the canonical pixel of world (0, 0), reduced as 4.1 says. `bounds` are cells. Cell keys name hexes, in chunks of 16 by 16 hexes (4.3).
- **Versions.** H1 takes the next free op and snapshot pair at build time, identity steps as shared/migrate.ts practice, so an older build refuses a room that may hold a hex scene. H2 and H3 take their own pairs, since a build of the phase before would misread their data. HX-D20's refusals mean no rule needs a gate below a phase's op version, and H1's fixture (section 6), replayed unchanged by H2 and H3, proves it.
- **Schemas.** `shared/protocol.ts` and `server/rooms/stateSchema.ts` take the new fields. H2 loosens a trap wire's ends to the 1/64 lattice with its op version, and permissions keep a square scene's on the half lattice.
- **Code contract** (`shared/rules/hex.ts` and `shared/rules/grid.ts`, built first):

```ts
type Grid = { shape: "squares" } | { shape: "hex"; orient: "rows" | "columns" };
sceneGrid(scene): Grid      // not gridOf, which reducer/phases.ts already names
hexCenter(g, hex), hexAt(g, point), hexDistance(g, a, b), hexNeighbors(g, hex) // clockwise from east on rows
hexLine(g, a, b), cornerAt(g, index), inScene(g, bounds, key)
footprintHexes(g, size, anchor), referenceHex(g, size, anchor), snapAnchor(g, size, center)
sceneHexes(g, bounds), hexSubcells(g, hex)   // the fog sub-cells whose centers it holds
```

Rule functions take the grid as a required parameter. Their square path is today's code. Existing tests gain an explicit square grid in their calls, a mechanical edit, and keep every assertion as it is.

## 10. Tests

- **Hex math, with property tests** (seeded `rng`, `shared/testutil.ts`, a few thousand cases a run, both orientations):
  - Distance is symmetric, zero only for one hex, keeps the triangle inequality, and equals a breadth-first search on random boards.
  - The six neighbors are distinct and at distance 1. `hexAt(hexCenter(h))` is `h`. `cornerAt` gives one point per corner whichever hex asks.
  - A line from a to b has distance + 1 hexes, each next to the last.
  - A footprint has 1, 3, 7 or 12 distinct, connected hexes, and its reference hex is one of them.
  - Snapping is idempotent and ties the same way on every run.
  - Every sub-cell of a scene belongs to exactly one hex, or to none at its edge.
  - Rows and columns agree under the mirror, chirality included.
- **Determinism.** `hex.ts` joins the banned-function test. A golden test pins a hex scene's sight tiles and withheld tokens.
- **Rules.** Every case of section 9 marked unit. The square path stays untouched: existing tests only gain the explicit square grid their calls now need, and every assertion stays as it is.
- **Replay.** H1's fixture of the ops it accepts on a hex scene, replayed unchanged by H2 and H3.
- **Mutation checks.** Each new test fails with its rule removed: the line's nudge, the Large lattice offset, the reference hex kept on a resize, the vertical samples of a sweep, the shape in `tx.ts`'s visibility check, a restore's stamp check, the trap zone's sub-cells, distance by the two anchors' hexes only, the rect in the sight test, the wall clip, the origin in a stationary footprint, the tokens HX-D22 adds, a stamp compared on content that holds no grid keys.
- **e2e.** A new `e2e/hex.e2e.ts` (prefix HX): the room's default set to Hex rows, a mat made, a goblin and a Large ogre placed, the goblin moved 4 hexes on its turn, measured, a fireball and Spirit Guardians read for who they catch, fog revealed, and a player seeing the same. Plus the alignment roots, `maps.e2e.ts` for the Grid rows and Line up grid, and `reach`.
- **Bench.** `scripts/bench-grid.ts`: hex lines at fit and at 100% on the largest scene, area coverage for a 1,000 ft line, against design 9.3's budgets. From H2, the wall clip at the sight caps: per token in a projection and per spot in `seenAtEvery`, up to 12 hexes of sub-cells against the sight walls near them.
